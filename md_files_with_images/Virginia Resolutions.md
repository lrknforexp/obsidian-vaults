# Virginia Resolutions

## Overview

The **Virginia Resolutions** (1798) were a set of resolutions drafted by **[[James Madison]]** and passed by the Virginia legislature in response to the **[[Alien and Sedition Acts]]**. Together with **[[Thomas Jefferson]]'s** [[Virginia and Kentucky Resolutions|Kentucky Resolutions]], they argued that states had the right to judge whether federal laws were constitutional — an early articulation of **states' rights** and **nullification** theory.

## Background

The Federalist Congress had passed the Alien and Sedition Acts, criminalizing criticism of the government and targeting Democratic-Republican newspaper editors. Jefferson and Madison believed the acts violated the **[[First Amendment]]** and exceeded federal authority.

## Key Arguments

Madison's Virginia Resolutions argued that the Constitution was a compact between the states, and that states therefore had the right to "interpose" their authority when the federal government exceeded its powers. Madison, however, envisioned interposition as a collective political protest, not a right of individual states to unilaterally void federal law.

## Later Misuse

South Carolina's **nullifiers** in the 1830s cited the Virginia and Kentucky Resolutions as justification for their claim that individual states could nullify federal tariff laws — a position Madison himself vigorously rejected.
