# Department of the Navy

## Overview

The **Department of the Navy** was established by Congress on April 30, 1798, during the **[[Quasi-War]]** with France, when the urgent need for naval power made a separate navy department necessary. Previously, naval affairs had been handled by the Department of War. The Department oversaw the construction and operation of American warships throughout the 19th century.

## Background

The [[Quasi-War]] with France (1798–1800) — an undeclared naval conflict — demonstrated that the United States needed a professional navy and a dedicated government department to manage it. Congress quickly created the Department and authorized the construction of warships.

## Early Naval Power

The new U.S. Navy performed impressively in the Quasi-War, capturing over 80 French vessels. The frigates built in this era — including the **USS Constitution** ("Old Ironsides") — became iconic symbols of American naval power.

## War of 1812

The Department oversaw the Navy during the **[[War of 1812]]**, in which American frigates won surprising victories against the Royal Navy in single-ship engagements, though Britain's superior numbers eventually blockaded American ports.
