---

The **Election of 1800** was one of the most consequential elections in American history. It transferred power from the **[[Federalist Party]]** to the **[[Democratic-Republican Party]]** — the first peaceful transfer of power between rival political parties in modern history. Jefferson called it the "Revolution of 1800."

---

## The Crisis

> [!info] The 73-73 Tie
> Jefferson and his running mate **[[Aaron Burr]]** each received **73 electoral votes**, creating a tie that threw the election to the House of Representatives. The House voted **36 times** before [[Alexander Hamilton]] — who despised Burr — persuaded Federalist representatives to support Jefferson. This crisis directly led to the **[[12th Amendment]]**.

---

## Why It Mattered

> [!info] A Peaceful Revolution
> The election demonstrated that **power could be transferred peacefully** between rival parties — a revolutionary idea in 1800, when most governments changed hands through violence or intrigue. Jefferson's inauguration proved the American constitutional system could survive partisan conflict. He called it a "revolution" because it returned power to democratic principles after what he saw as Federalist tyranny.

---

## Consequences

- Ended Federalist dominance of the federal government
- **[[midnight judges]]** — Adams's last-minute judicial appointments
- **[[Marbury v. Madison]]** — arose directly from the transition
- Democratic-Republicans dominated American politics for the next two decades
