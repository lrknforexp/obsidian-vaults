# Ohio River Valley

## Overview

The **Ohio River Valley** — the region along and around the Ohio River, stretching from western Pennsylvania to the Mississippi — was one of the most strategically important and contested regions in early American history. It was the focal point of the **[[French and Indian War]]**, the site of intense conflict with Native Americans, and the gateway to westward expansion.

## French and Indian War

France and Britain both claimed the Ohio Valley, each building forts to assert control. Virginia sent young **[[George Washington]]** to demand French withdrawal in 1753 (they refused). The skirmish at Fort Necessity (1754) triggered the [[French and Indian War]]. The valley's strategic importance — as a water route into the interior — made it worth fighting a global war over.

## Native American Resistance

Dozens of Native American nations lived in the Ohio Valley. After British victory in 1763, **[[Pontiac's Rebellion]]** erupted partly in response to British policies in the region. After American independence, conflict with Native peoples over Ohio Valley lands continued through the **Battle of Fallen Timbers** (1794) and the **Treaty of Greenville** (1795).

## Gateway to Expansion

The **[[Northwest Ordinance]]** (1787) organized the Ohio Valley into territories (Ohio, Indiana, Illinois, Michigan, Wisconsin) and established the process for admitting new states, with slavery banned in the region.
