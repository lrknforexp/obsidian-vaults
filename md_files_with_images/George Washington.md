**George Washington** (1732–1799) was the commander of the **Continental Army** during the [[American Revolution]] and the **first President of the United States** (1789–1797). Called the "indispensable man" of the founding era, he set crucial democratic precedents — most importantly, voluntarily surrendering power after two terms.

---

## Revolutionary Leadership

> [!info] Holding the Army Together
> Washington's greatest achievement during the Revolution was keeping the Continental Army alive through catastrophic defeats and desperate hardship. From the **[[Battle of Trenton]]** to the suffering at **[[Valley Forge]]** to the final victory at **[[Battle of Yorktown]]**, Washington's personal leadership held the cause together when others would have quit.

---

## Presidency

> [!info] Precedents Washington Set
> As the first president, everything Washington did set a precedent. Key decisions included:
> - Forming a **cabinet** of advisors
> - Using **[[Alexander Hamilton]]**'s financial program to establish national credit
> - Suppressing the **[[Whiskey Rebellion]]** to demonstrate federal authority
> - Issuing the **[[Farewell Address]]** warning against permanent alliances and political factions
> - Retiring after **two terms**, establishing the precedent formalized by the **[[22nd Amendment]]**

---

## Legacy

Washington is universally regarded as the essential figure of the founding era. His willingness to surrender power distinguished the American republic from the dictatorships that followed other revolutions.
