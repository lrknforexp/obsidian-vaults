# John Quincy Adams

## Overview

**John Quincy Adams** (1767–1848) was the **6th President of the United States** (1825–1829) and one of America's most accomplished diplomats. The son of President [[John Adams]], he negotiated the **[[Adams-Onís Treaty]]**, helped craft the **[[Monroe Doctrine]]**, and later became a powerful anti-slavery voice in Congress.

## Early Diplomatic Career

As Secretary of State under [[James Monroe]], Adams was the primary architect of American foreign policy in the 1810s–1820s. He negotiated the **[[Adams-Onís Treaty]]** (1819) acquiring Florida, helped formulate the **[[Monroe Doctrine]]** (1823), and extended America's northern border to the Pacific in the Convention of 1818 with Britain.

## The "Corrupt Bargain" Election of 1824

In 1824, four candidates split the electoral vote. The House chose Adams over the popular vote leader **[[Andrew Jackson]]**, reportedly after Adams offered [[Henry Clay]] the position of Secretary of State in exchange for Clay's support — the so-called "**Corrupt Bargain**." Jackson supporters were furious and organized to defeat Adams in 1828.

## Presidency and Defeat

Adams proposed ambitious internal improvements and a national university, but Congress (dominated by Jackson's allies) blocked most of his agenda. He lost the 1828 election decisively to Jackson.

## Post-Presidential Career

Uniquely, Adams returned to Congress as a Representative (1831–1848), becoming a fierce opponent of slavery and the "gag rule" that prevented anti-slavery petitions from being heard.
