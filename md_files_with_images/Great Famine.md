# Great Famine

## Overview

The **Great Famine** (1845–1852), also known as the Irish Potato Famine or *An Gorta Mór* ("the Great Hunger"), was a catastrophic famine in Ireland caused by a potato blight that destroyed the primary food source of Ireland's rural poor. Approximately **one million people died** of starvation and disease, and another **one million emigrated**, primarily to the United States, in the famine years alone.

## Causes

Ireland's rural poor had become dangerously dependent on a single crop — the **potato** — for subsistence. When *Phytophthora infestans* (potato blight) destroyed successive harvests beginning in 1845, millions faced starvation. British government policies — continuing to export food from Ireland even during the famine and providing inadequate relief — contributed to the death toll.

## Emigration to America

The famine triggered a massive emigration wave. By 1860, approximately **1.5–2 million** Irish had emigrated to America. Irish immigrants settled heavily in Boston, New York, and other northeastern cities, transforming American urban demographics. They faced severe anti-Catholic discrimination and took the hardest, lowest-paid jobs.

## Impact on America

Irish immigrants became a major political force (especially in the Democratic Party), provided crucial **[[Immigrant labor|labor]]** for canals and railroads, and contributed enormously to American culture. By 1860, Irish immigrants and their children were one of the largest ethnic groups in the U.S.
