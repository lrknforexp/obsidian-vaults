# Hartford Convention

## Overview

The **Hartford Convention** (December 1814 – January 1815) was a secret meeting of New England **[[Federalist Party|Federalist]]** delegates who gathered in Hartford, Connecticut, to discuss grievances against the [[War of 1812]] and the **[[Democratic-Republican Party|Democratic-Republican]]** administration. Its outcome contributed to the collapse of the Federalist Party.

## Background

New England strongly opposed the War of 1812, which had devastated its maritime commerce. Some extreme Federalists (the "Blue Lights") even favored secession, though the convention's moderate majority rejected this.

## What the Convention Did

The convention's final report called for **constitutional amendments** to limit Southern power (including removing the [[Three-Fifths Compromise]]) and restrict the ability to declare war, impose embargoes, and admit new states. It demanded these changes as conditions for continued New England participation in the war.

## Disastrous Timing

The convention's delegates arrived in Washington with their demands just as news reached the capital of two events: **Andrew Jackson's** victory at the **Battle of New Orleans** and the signing of the **Treaty of Ghent** ending the war. Their demands seemed treasonous and foolish simultaneously.

## Legacy

The Hartford Convention destroyed the Federalist Party's credibility and effectively ended it as a national political force, ushering in the "**Era of Good Feelings**" under President [[James Monroe]].
