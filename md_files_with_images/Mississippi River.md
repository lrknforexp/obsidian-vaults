# Mississippi River

## Overview

The **Mississippi River** is the largest river system in North America, stretching approximately 2,340 miles from Minnesota to the Gulf of Mexico. In American history, it served as a crucial transportation artery, a contested boundary, a barrier to expansion, and the economic lifeline of the interior United States.

## Colonial and Early National Significance

Control of the Mississippi and its outlet at **New Orleans** was essential for American westward settlers to ship goods to market. Spain's control of New Orleans gave it enormous leverage over American commerce. The **[[Pinckney's Treaty|Treaty of San Lorenzo]]** (1795) gave Americans the right to navigate the river and deposit goods at New Orleans.

## Louisiana Purchase

Jefferson's primary motivation for the **[[Louisiana Purchase]]** (1803) was securing control of New Orleans and the Mississippi. When Napoleon offered the entire Louisiana Territory, Jefferson seized the opportunity.

## Transportation Revolution

The **[[Steamboat]]**, pioneered by **[[Robert Fulton]]** (1807), transformed the Mississippi into a bustling commercial highway. By the 1830s–1840s, hundreds of steamboats carried cotton, grain, and passengers up and down the river, making cities like New Orleans, St. Louis, and Memphis major commercial centers.
