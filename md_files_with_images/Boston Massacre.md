# Boston Massacre

## Overview

The **Boston Massacre** (March 5, 1770) was a confrontation in Boston in which British soldiers fired into a crowd of colonists, killing **five people**. The event was seized upon by colonial leaders like **[[Samuel Adams]]** as propaganda against British tyranny and became a catalyst for the [[American Revolution]].

## Background

Tensions between Boston colonists and the British troops stationed there (to enforce the **[[Townshend Acts]]**) had been building for months. Colonists resented the military presence, and skirmishes between soldiers and workers were common.

## The Incident

On the evening of March 5, a crowd began harassing a lone British sentry at the Custom House. More soldiers arrived, and the crowd grew larger and more threatening, pelting the soldiers with ice, rocks, and insults. Amid the chaos, soldiers fired without a clear order. Five colonists were killed, including **Crispus Attucks**, an African American man who became the first martyr of the Revolution.

## Propaganda and Trial

Paul Revere's famous engraving depicted the soldiers firing in cold blood on innocent civilians — a distortion of events, but enormously effective propaganda. Ironically, **[[John Adams]]** successfully defended the British soldiers at trial, with most acquitted.

## Significance

The Boston Massacre inflamed colonial opinion against British occupation and helped unite colonists behind the patriot cause, contributing to the path toward revolution.
