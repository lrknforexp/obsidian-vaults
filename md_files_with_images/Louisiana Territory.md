# Louisiana Territory

## Overview

The **Louisiana Territory** (also called Upper Louisiana) was the vast region north of New Orleans that France ceded to the United States in the **[[Louisiana Purchase]]** of 1803. Stretching from the [[Mississippi River]] to the Rocky Mountains and from the Gulf of Mexico to the Canadian border, it was one of the largest land acquisitions in history and doubled the size of the United States.

## From France to Spain to France to America

France originally claimed the region and named it after King Louis XIV. France ceded it to Spain in 1762 after the Seven Years' War. Napoleon forced Spain to return it to France in 1800 (Treaty of San Ildefonso), then sold it to the United States in 1803 for $15 million — approximately 4 cents per acre.

## Jefferson's Vision

President **[[Thomas Jefferson]]** saw the Louisiana Territory as the heartland of an "**Empire of Liberty**" — a vast space where American farmers could maintain the agrarian republic he envisioned. He commissioned the **[[Lewis and Clark Expedition]]** to explore and map the territory.

## Later Significance

The Louisiana Territory became the battleground for the **slavery expansion** debate. The **[[Missouri Compromise]]** (1820) drew a line through the territory — slavery permitted south of 36°30', banned north of it — temporarily resolving but not ending the conflict.
