# Railroad

## Overview

The **railroad** was the most transformative technology of 19th-century America, knitting the vast continent together economically and culturally and accelerating both industrialization and westward expansion. The first American railroads appeared in the late 1820s; by 1860, the U.S. had over 30,000 miles of track — more than all of Europe combined.

## Early Development

The first American railroads (1827–1830) used horse-drawn cars on iron rails. Steam locomotives quickly replaced horses. The **Baltimore & Ohio Railroad** (1827) and the **South Carolina Railroad** (1833) were early pioneers.

## Economic Impact

Railroads fundamentally transformed the American economy: they reduced transportation costs and times by 90%, connected farmers and manufacturers to distant markets, facilitated the **[[Market Revolution]]**, and created demand for iron, coal, and engineering. The railroad became the first "big business" in America.

## Transcontinental Railroad

The **transcontinental railroad**, completed on May 10, 1869, when the Central Pacific and Union Pacific tracks met at Promontory Summit, Utah, linked the Atlantic and Pacific coasts. Built largely by Chinese and Irish immigrant laborers, it completed the continental vision of **[[Manifest Destiny]]**.

## Social Impact

Railroads created **standard time zones** (1883), enabled mass migration westward, devastated Native American cultures by opening the plains to settlement, and accelerated **[[Urbanization]]**.
