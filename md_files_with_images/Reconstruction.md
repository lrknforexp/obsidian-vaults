# Reconstruction

> [!info] Reconstruction
> **Overview**
>
> **Topic**
> Federal program to rebuild the South and integrate freed people after the Civil War
>
> **Era**
> 1865–1877
>
> **President(s)**
> Lincoln (briefly), Andrew Johnson, [[Ulysses S. Grant]]

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Era:** 1865–1877
> **Key Amendments:** 13th (abolished slavery), [[14th Amendment|14th]] (citizenship/equal protection), 15th (Black male suffrage)
> **Ended by:** Compromise of 1877 (Hayes-Tilden election)
> **Result:** Withdrawal of federal troops; end of Black political power in South for generations

---

**Reconstruction** (1865–1877) was the federal program to rebuild the South, integrate formerly enslaved people into American society, and determine the terms on which Confederate states would rejoin the Union after the [[Civil War]].

---

## Two Visions of Reconstruction

> [!info] Lincoln vs. the Radicals
> **Lincoln's Plan (10% Plan):** Lenient terms — a Southern state could rejoin when 10% of voters swore loyalty oaths
>
> **Radical Republican Plan:** Far more demanding — required new state constitutions guaranteeing Black suffrage; wanted to fundamentally transform Southern society
>
> After Lincoln's assassination (1865), President **Andrew Johnson** pursued a lenient policy, angering Radical Republicans who took control of Reconstruction in 1867.

---

## Achievements

> [!info] What Reconstruction Accomplished
> - **13th Amendment** — abolished slavery (1865)
> - **[[14th Amendment]]** — granted citizenship and equal protection (1868)
> - **15th Amendment** — guaranteed Black male suffrage (1870)
> - **Freedmen's Bureau** — provided education and assistance to formerly enslaved people
> - African Americans elected to Congress and state legislatures for the first time

---

## End of Reconstruction

> [!info] The Betrayal
> The **Compromise of 1877** ended Reconstruction — federal troops were withdrawn from the South in exchange for Republican Rutherford Hayes winning a disputed presidential election. Without federal protection, Southern states rapidly disenfranchised Black voters through violence, poll taxes, and literacy tests. The **Jim Crow** era began.
