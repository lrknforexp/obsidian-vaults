# Industrial Revolution

## Overview

The **Industrial Revolution** was the transition from hand production and agriculture to machine-based manufacturing that began in Britain in the late 18th century and spread to the United States in the early 19th century. In America, it transformed the economy, society, and the nature of work, particularly in New England.

## American Beginnings

American industrialization began with **Samuel Slater**, who memorized the designs of British textile machinery and built the first American factory in Pawtucket, Rhode Island in 1793. The **Lowell System** in Massachusetts in the 1820s–1840s became a model of early American industrial capitalism.

## Key Technologies

The **steam engine** powered factories and [[Steamboat|steamboats]] and later [[Railroad|railroads]]. The **[[Telegraph]]** enabled rapid long-distance communication. The **cotton gin** (1793) mechanized cotton processing but also dramatically increased the demand for enslaved labor in the South.

## Social Impact

The Industrial Revolution created a new **working class** (initially often young women at Lowell), an **urban population**, and a clear divide between factory owners and wage laborers. It fueled the **[[Market Revolution]]** and shifted the U.S. economy toward manufacturing.
