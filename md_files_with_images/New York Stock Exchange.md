# New York Stock Exchange

## Overview

The **New York Stock Exchange** (NYSE), established in 1792, is the world's largest stock exchange and a symbol of American capitalism. It traces its origins to the **Buttonwood Agreement** (May 17, 1792), in which 24 stockbrokers signed an agreement to trade securities with each other under a Buttonwood tree on Wall Street in lower Manhattan.

## Origins

Trading in government securities and bank stocks began in New York after the new federal government, under **[[Alexander Hamilton]]'s** financial program, issued bonds to fund the national debt. The Buttonwood Agreement formalized trading relationships among brokers.

## Early Role

The NYSE facilitated capital formation for the young republic: banks, insurance companies, and later canals and railroads raised money by selling stock to investors. Hamilton's financial system — a national debt, a national bank, and established credit — made New York the financial capital of America.

## Connection to Hamilton's Vision

Hamilton's financial program (assumption of state debts, creation of the [[National Bank]], securities trading) directly created the conditions for the NYSE's founding. He believed a sophisticated financial system was essential for national economic development.
