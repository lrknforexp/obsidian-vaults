# 22nd Amendment

> [!info] 22nd Amendment
> **Overview**
>
> **Topic**
> Presidential term limits
>
> **Ratified**
> 1951
>
> **Triggered by**
> [[Franklin D. Roosevelt]]'s four terms in office

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Ratified:** 1951
> **Limit:** Two terms (maximum 10 years if completing a predecessor's term)
> **Proposed by:** Republican-controlled Congress, 1947
> **Exempted:** Harry Truman (in office when it passed)

---

The **22nd Amendment**, ratified in **1951**, limits the President of the United States to **two terms** in office. It was passed in direct response to [[Franklin D. Roosevelt]]'s unprecedented **four consecutive terms** as president (1933–1945), which broke the informal two-term tradition established by [[George Washington]].

---

## Background

> [!info] Washington's Precedent
> From [[George Washington]]'s voluntary retirement after two terms in 1796, an informal two-term tradition governed the presidency for 150 years. Washington believed no man should hold power too long. Roosevelt broke it in 1940, citing the emergency of World War II, then won again in 1944.

---

## Passage and Ratification

After Roosevelt's death in 1945, the Republican-controlled Congress passed the amendment in 1947. It was ratified by the states in 1951. President Harry Truman, who was in office when it passed, was exempted from its provisions.

---

## Significance

> [!info] Why It Matters
> The amendment formalized Washington's two-term tradition into **constitutional law**, reinforcing the principle that democratic republics must guard against the concentration of executive power in any one individual. It remains one of the most debated amendments, with periodic calls for repeal or modification.
