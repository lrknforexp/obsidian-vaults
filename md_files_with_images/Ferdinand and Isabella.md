# Ferdinand and Isabella

## Overview

**Ferdinand II of Aragon** and **Isabella I of Castile** were the monarchs whose marriage in 1469 united the two most powerful kingdoms of Spain, creating modern Spain. Their reign (1474–1516) was transformative: they completed the **Reconquista**, expelled Jews and Muslims, established the Spanish Inquisition, and sponsored **Christopher Columbus's** voyages to the Americas in 1492.

## The Reconquista

Ferdinand and Isabella completed the 700-year Christian reconquest of Iberia by capturing **Granada** in January 1492, the last Muslim stronghold in Spain. In the same year, they expelled all Jews who refused to convert to Christianity (the Alhambra Decree).

## Sponsorship of Columbus

Columbus approached multiple European monarchs before Queen Isabella agreed to fund his westward voyage to Asia in 1492. His "discovery" of the Americas opened an era of Spanish colonization that transformed both hemispheres.

## Legacy in the Americas

Ferdinand and Isabella's sponsorship of exploration led directly to the establishment of **[[New Spain]]**, the **[[Encomienda System]]**, and the entire framework of Spanish colonialism in the Americas. The [[Law of Burgos]] (1512) under their successor regulated (though did not eliminate) the exploitation of indigenous peoples.
