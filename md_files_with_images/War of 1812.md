# War of 1812

> [!info] War of 1812
> **Overview**
>
> **Topic**
> Second war between the United States and Great Britain
>
> **Dates**
> 1812–1815
>
> **President**
> James Madison

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Dates:** 1812–1815
> **Combatants:** U.S. vs. Britain (and Native American allies)
> **Causes:** Impressment, trade restrictions, British support of Native resistance
> **Key Events:** Burning of Washington D.C., Battle of New Orleans
> **Result:** Status quo ante bellum (neither side gained territory)

---

The **War of 1812** was a conflict between the United States and Great Britain, fought largely over British interference with American shipping and impressment of American sailors during the Napoleonic Wars, as well as British support for Native American resistance to U.S. expansion.

---

## Causes

> [!info] Why America Went to War
> - **Impressment** — Britain forcibly taking sailors from American ships into the Royal Navy
> - **Trade interference** — British Orders in Council blocked American trade with Napoleonic Europe
> - **Native American alliances** — Britain encouraged Native resistance to American westward expansion
> - **War Hawks** — Congressional leaders like Henry Clay pushed for war to expand into Canada and Florida

---

## Key Events

> [!info] Major Moments
> - **Burning of Washington D.C.** (1814) — British forces captured and burned the capital, including the White House
> - **Battle of Baltimore** — American defense inspired Francis Scott Key to write "The Star-Spangled Banner"
> - **Battle of New Orleans** (January 1815) — [[Andrew Jackson]]'s stunning victory, fought after the peace treaty was signed
> - **Treaty of Ghent** (1814) — ended the war with no territorial changes

---

## Legacy

> [!info] Long-Term Consequences
> The War of 1812 produced a surge of American nationalism and the "**Era of Good Feelings**." It ended significant Native American resistance east of the Mississippi (Tecumseh died in battle). The **[[Hartford Convention]]**'s opposition to the war destroyed the **[[Federalist Party]]**. Andrew Jackson's fame from New Orleans launched his political career.
