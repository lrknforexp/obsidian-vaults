# Matthew Lyon

## Overview

**Matthew Lyon** (1749–1822) was an Irish-born Vermont congressman who became the first person prosecuted and convicted under the **[[Alien and Sedition Acts]]** (1798). His case became a cause célèbre for opponents of the acts and demonstrated their use as a political weapon against **[[Democratic-Republican Party|Democratic-Republicans]]**.

## Political Career

Lyon was a fiercely independent Republican congressman from Vermont, known for his combative personality. He had a literal fistfight with a Connecticut Federalist congressman on the House floor in 1798 — hitting him with fire tongs after being spat upon.

## Prosecution Under the Sedition Act

In 1798, Lyon published a letter criticizing President **[[John Adams]]**'s handling of foreign affairs and his "unbounded thirst for ridiculous pomp." He was charged under the Sedition Act for publishing "false, scandalous, and malicious" writing against the president.

## Conviction and Aftermath

Lyon was convicted and sentenced to four months in jail and a $1,000 fine. He ran for reelection from jail — and won. Supporters paid his fine and celebrated him as a martyr. His case energized opposition to the Sedition Act and contributed to Jefferson's electoral victory in 1800.
