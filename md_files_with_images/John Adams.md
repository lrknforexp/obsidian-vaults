**John Adams** (1735–1826) was the **2nd President of the United States** and one of the most important Founding Fathers. A brilliant lawyer and tireless advocate for independence, he guided the [[Second Continental Congress]] toward the [[Declaration of Independence]] and served as the first American minister to Britain.

---

## Presidency (1797–1801)

> [!info] Adams's Difficult Term
> Adams inherited a crisis with France (the **[[Quasi-War]]**), which consumed his presidency. When French agents demanded bribes in the **[[XYZ Affair]]**, Adams's refusal to pay made him briefly popular. However, his signing of the **[[Alien and Sedition Acts]]** (1798) — which criminalized criticism of the government — seriously damaged his reputation and contributed to his defeat.

---

## The Boston Massacre Defense

One of Adams's most principled acts was defending the British soldiers accused in the **[[Boston Massacre]]** (1770). Despite enormous public pressure, he successfully argued for their acquittal, demonstrating his commitment to rule of law over popular passion.

---

## Legacy

> [!info] Forgotten Founder
> Adams is often overshadowed by Washington and Jefferson, but he was essential to American independence. He died on July 4, 1826 — the same day as Jefferson — exactly 50 years after the Declaration of Independence. His last words were reportedly "Thomas Jefferson survives" — he did not know Jefferson had died hours earlier.
