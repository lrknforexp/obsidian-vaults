# Pennsylvania

## Overview

**Pennsylvania** was one of the most diverse and influential of the original thirteen colonies, founded in 1681 by **William Penn** as a Quaker colony dedicated to religious tolerance and peaceful relations with Native Americans. Its largest city, **[[Philadelphia]]**, served as the colonial capital and birthplace of American independence.

## Founding and Character

Penn's "Holy Experiment" attracted Quakers, German immigrants (Pennsylvania Dutch), Scots-Irish settlers, and members of many other religious groups. Pennsylvania's tradition of tolerance and diverse population gave it a more cosmopolitan character than either Puritan New England or Anglican Virginia.

## Revolutionary Significance

Pennsylvania hosted the **[[First Continental Congress]]**, the **[[Second Continental Congress]]**, and the **Constitutional Convention**. The **[[Declaration of Independence]]** and U.S. Constitution were both signed in Philadelphia. Valley Forge — where Washington's army survived its darkest winter — is in Pennsylvania.

## Economic Importance

Pennsylvania's fertile farmland, iron industries, and later coal and steel made it one of America's most economically important states. The **[[Whiskey Rebellion]]** (1794) in western Pennsylvania tested federal authority in the state's frontier regions.
