# Trail of Tears

![[Trail_of_Tears_Lindneux.jpg]]

## Overview

The **Trail of Tears** refers to the forced removal of approximately **60,000–100,000 Native Americans** from their ancestral homelands in the southeastern United States to "Indian Territory" west of the Mississippi (present-day Oklahoma) between 1830 and 1850. The removal, mandated by the **[[Indian Removal Act]]** of 1830, resulted in the deaths of thousands from disease, starvation, and exposure.

## Background

After the [[Indian Removal Act]] was signed by President **[[Andrew Jackson]]**, the U.S. government negotiated (often through fraud and coercion) removal treaties with the **Five Civilized Tribes**: Cherokee, Chickasaw, Choctaw, Creek, and Seminole.

## Cherokee Removal

The **Cherokee Nation** challenged removal in court. In *Worcester v. Georgia* (1832), the Supreme Court ruled in the Cherokee's favor. Jackson allegedly responded, "John Marshall has made his decision; now let him enforce it," and proceeded with removal anyway.

In 1838–1839, U.S. soldiers forced approximately **15,000 Cherokees** west at gunpoint during winter. Roughly **4,000** died along the route, which the Cherokee called **"Nunna daul Tsuny"** ("the trail where they cried").

## Significance

The Trail of Tears represents one of the most tragic episodes of ethnic cleansing in American history and a fundamental violation of treaty rights and Supreme Court decisions.
