# Ulysses S. Grant

## Overview

**Ulysses S. Grant** (1822–1885) was the commanding general of Union forces during the [[Civil War]] and the 18th President of the United States (1869–1877). His aggressive military leadership brought Union victory, but his presidency was marked by both significant achievements in civil rights and damaging corruption scandals.

## Civil War Career

Grant rose through the war by winning key victories: **Fort Donelson** (1862), **Vicksburg** (1863, splitting the Confederacy), and the **Overland Campaign** (1864). President Lincoln promoted him to commanding general in 1864. His strategy of relentless pressure, regardless of casualties, ground down the Confederate army. He accepted Lee's surrender at **Appomattox Court House** on April 9, 1865.

## Presidency

Grant's presidency (1869–1877) oversaw **Reconstruction**, including strong enforcement of civil rights for African Americans. He signed the **[[14th Amendment|Civil Rights Act of 1875]]** and used the Army to suppress the Ku Klux Klan. However, his administration was plagued by major corruption scandals (Crédit Mobilier, Whiskey Ring) involving his associates.

## Legacy

Grant is increasingly recognized by historians for his genuine commitment to civil rights for formerly enslaved people — a legacy overshadowed for decades by Lost Cause mythology. His *Personal Memoirs* (1885), written as he was dying of cancer, is considered one of the finest military memoirs in the English language.
