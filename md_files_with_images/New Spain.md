# New Spain

## Overview

**New Spain** was the colonial territory of the Spanish Empire in North America, Central America, and the Caribbean, established following the conquests of the **[[Conquistadors]]** in the 16th century. At its height, New Spain encompassed present-day Mexico, Central America, much of the Caribbean, the southwestern United States, and Florida — making it the largest colonial territory in the Americas.

## Establishment

New Spain was formally established in 1535 with Mexico City (built on the ruins of the Aztec capital Tenochtitlán) as its capital. A Viceroy appointed by the Spanish Crown governed the territory.

## Economy and Society

New Spain's economy rested on: silver mining (Potosí and Zacatecas produced enormous wealth), **[[Encomienda System|encomienda]]** labor, agriculture, and trade. Spanish settlers formed an elite class; **mestizos** (mixed Spanish-indigenous heritage), indigenous peoples, and enslaved Africans occupied lower social positions in a rigid racial hierarchy (**sistema de castas**).

## Legacy in the U.S.

Parts of New Spain became U.S. territory: Florida (1821 via [[Adams-Onís Treaty]]), Texas, California, and the Southwest after the **[[Mexican-American War]]** (1848). Spanish place names, architecture, law, and culture remain deeply embedded in the American Southwest.
