# Kansas-Nebraska Act

## Overview

The **Kansas-Nebraska Act** (1854), championed by Senator **Stephen Douglas** of Illinois, organized the Kansas and Nebraska territories and allowed their residents to decide whether to allow slavery through **popular sovereignty**. It effectively repealed the **[[Missouri Compromise]]** of 1820, which had banned slavery north of 36°30' latitude, and reignited the national crisis over slavery.

## Background

Douglas needed Southern support for a transcontinental railroad route through the North and offered the repeal of the Missouri Compromise as a compromise. The bill passed with Southern votes, outraging Northerners who had considered the Missouri Compromise a sacred agreement.

## "Bleeding Kansas"

The act turned Kansas into a battleground. Pro-slavery and anti-slavery settlers both flooded in, each trying to control the territorial government. Violence erupted — including abolitionist **John Brown's** Pottawatomie Massacre — giving rise to the term "Bleeding Kansas."

## Political Realignment

The Kansas-Nebraska Act destroyed the **Whig Party**, split the Democratic Party along sectional lines, and directly caused the formation of the **Republican Party** in 1854, dedicated to preventing slavery's expansion. It set the nation on an accelerating path toward the [[Civil War]].
