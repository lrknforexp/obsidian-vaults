# Presbyterians

## Overview

**Presbyterians** are a Protestant denomination with roots in the Calvinist Reformation, governed by **elders** (presbyters) elected by congregations rather than bishops appointed from above. In American history, Presbyterians were particularly influential in the Middle Colonies and the South, played important roles in the [[American Revolution]], and contributed to the [[First Great Awakening]].

## Colonial Influence

Presbyterians were concentrated in the Middle Colonies (New Jersey, Pennsylvania, New York) and the Scots-Irish backcountry of Appalachia. Their tradition of educated ministry led them to establish important institutions including the College of New Jersey (later Princeton University).

## Revolutionary Connections

Presbyterians were disproportionately represented among patriots during the [[American Revolution]]. The Revolutionary war was sometimes called the "Presbyterian Rebellion" by British observers. John Witherspoon, the only clergyman to sign the **[[Declaration of Independence]]**, was a Presbyterian minister and president of Princeton.

## Great Awakening Divisions

The **[[First Great Awakening]]** split American Presbyterians between "New Side" (pro-revival) and "Old Side" (anti-revival) factions — a division that reflected broader tensions between emotional revivalism and ordered, educated religion.
