# Anglicans

## Overview

**Anglicans** (members of the Church of England, called **Episcopalians** in America after independence) were the established church in the Southern colonies and New York before the American Revolution. Anglican churches received government support and were closely tied to British colonial authority — a connection that made Anglicanism politically vulnerable during the Revolution.

## Colonial Role

The Anglican Church was the established (state-supported) church in Virginia, Maryland, the Carolinas, Georgia, and parts of New York. Wealthy Southern planters often dominated local vestries (church governing boards), intertwining church and social power.

## Revolutionary Era Complications

Many Anglican clergy were Loyalists (some clergy had sworn loyalty oaths to the Crown). After independence, the American Anglican Church reorganized as the **Protestant Episcopal Church** (1789), severing ties with the Church of England and creating its own governance.

## Social Influence

Anglican/Episcopal churches were associated with the colonial elite. **[[George Washington]]**, **[[Thomas Jefferson]]**, **[[James Madison]]**, and many other founders were nominally Anglican/Episcopal, though their personal beliefs varied widely.
