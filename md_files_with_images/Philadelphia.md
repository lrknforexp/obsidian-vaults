# Philadelphia

## Overview

**Philadelphia** was the largest and most important city in colonial and early republican America, serving as the nation's capital for most of the period between independence and 1800. It was the site of the **[[Second Continental Congress]]**, the signing of the **[[Declaration of Independence]]** (1776), and the **Constitutional Convention** (1787).

## Colonial Prominence

Founded by William Penn in 1682 as a Quaker colony emphasizing religious tolerance, Philadelphia grew into the largest city in British North America by the mid-18th century. Its diverse population, thriving commerce, and intellectual culture made it a center of colonial life.

## Revolutionary Capital

Philadelphia hosted the **[[First Continental Congress]]** (1774), the **[[Second Continental Congress]]** (1775–1783), and the signing of the **[[Declaration of Independence]]** on July 4, 1776. It served as the American capital during most of the Revolutionary War (except when British forces occupied it in 1777–1778).

## Constitutional Convention

In 1787, delegates gathered at Philadelphia's **Independence Hall** to draft the U.S. Constitution. The building had also hosted the signing of the Declaration of Independence.

## First National Capital

Philadelphia served as the capital of the United States from 1790 to 1800, while **[[Washington, D.C.]]** was being built, under the **Residence Act of 1790** compromise between Hamilton and Jefferson.
