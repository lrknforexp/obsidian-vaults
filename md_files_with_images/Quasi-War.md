> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Dates:** 1798–1800
> **Type:** Undeclared naval conflict
> **Trigger:** French seizure of American merchant ships after [[Jay's Treaty]]
> **Key Incident:** [[XYZ Affair]] (1797)
> **Result:** Convention of 1800 ended the conflict; ended the French alliance of 1778

---

The **Quasi-War** (1798–1800) was an undeclared naval conflict between the United States and France. Angered by America's signing of **[[Jay's Treaty]]** with Britain, French warships began seizing American merchant vessels. The crisis nearly led to a full-scale war and consumed the presidency of **[[John Adams]]**.

---

## The XYZ Affair

> [!info] The Bribery Demand
> Adams sent three diplomats to France to resolve the crisis. French Foreign Minister **[[Talleyrand]]**'s agents (referred to as X, Y, and Z in diplomatic dispatches) demanded a **$250,000 bribe** and a loan to France before negotiations could begin. The Americans refused. **[[Charles Cotesworth Pinckney]]**'s reported response — "Millions for defense, but not one cent for tribute!" — became a patriotic rallying cry.

---

## The Naval War

> [!info] American Performance
> Despite having a small navy, American warships performed impressively — capturing over **80 French vessels**. The conflict spurred creation of the **[[Department of the Navy]]** (1798) and the **[[U.S. Marine Corps]]** (1798). Frigates like the *USS Constitution* ("Old Ironsides") were built during this period.

---

## Political Consequences

The Quasi-War led Adams to sign the controversial **[[Alien and Sedition Acts]]** (1798). His decision to seek peace rather than full war — ending the conflict with the Convention of 1800 — split the Federalist Party and contributed to his defeat in the **[[Election of 1800]]**.
