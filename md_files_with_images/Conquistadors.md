# Conquistadors

## Overview

**Conquistadors** were Spanish soldiers, explorers, and adventurers who led the conquest of the Americas in the 15th and 16th centuries. Armed with superior weapons and, unwittingly, devastating diseases like **[[Smallpox|smallpox]]**, they overthrew the Aztec and Inca empires and claimed vast territories for the Spanish Crown.

## Key Conquistadors

**Hernán Cortés** conquered the Aztec Empire (Mexico) in 1519–1521, aided by indigenous allies who resented Aztec rule and by epidemic disease that devastated the Aztec population. **Francisco Pizarro** conquered the Inca Empire (Peru) in 1532–1533, capturing and executing the Inca emperor Atahualpa. **Hernando de Soto** explored the American Southeast (1539–1542), and **Francisco Vázquez de Coronado** explored the American Southwest (1540–1542).

## Methods and Motivations

Conquistadors were motivated by **"God, Glory, and Gold"** — spreading Christianity, personal fame, and plundering precious metals. They operated under the **[[Encomienda System]]**, which granted them rights to indigenous labor in exchange for Christianizing native peoples.

## Impact

The conquests resulted in the collapse of major indigenous civilizations, the deaths of millions from disease and violence, and the establishment of **[[New Spain]]** and other colonial territories that would be exploited for centuries.
