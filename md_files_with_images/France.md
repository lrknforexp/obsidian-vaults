# France

## Overview

**France** played a pivotal role in American history from the colonial era through the early republic. France was Britain's chief rival in the struggle to colonize North America, an essential ally during the **[[American Revolution]]**, and a source of intense diplomatic conflict during the 1790s–1800s.

## Colonial Rivalry

France controlled Canada and the Mississippi Valley, allying with Native Americans against British expansion. The **[[French and Indian War]]** (1754–1763) ended French power in North America, as France ceded Canada and lands east of the Mississippi to Britain.

## Revolutionary Alliance

The **[[Franco-American Alliance]]** (1778) was decisive in American independence. French troops, especially at **[[Battle of Yorktown|Yorktown]]**, and the French navy were essential to defeating Britain. **[[Benjamin Franklin]]** was the key American diplomat who secured French support.

## Post-Revolutionary Tensions

The **[[French Revolution]]** (1789) created ideological divisions in America — **[[Thomas Jefferson]]** and Democrats sympathized with France, while **[[Alexander Hamilton]]** and Federalists were horrified by its violence. The **[[Quasi-War]]** (1798–1800) brought the U.S. and France to the brink of full-scale war.

## Louisiana Sale

**[[Napoleon Bonaparte]]** sold the **[[Louisiana Territory]]** to the United States in 1803, dramatically expanding American territory.
