---

**Washington's Farewell Address** (1796) was a letter published in newspapers as [[George Washington]] declined to seek a third term. Written with help from **[[Alexander Hamilton]]**, it offered warnings about the dangers threatening the new republic that proved prophetic.

---

## Key Warnings

> [!info] Washington's Three Great Warnings
> **1. Political Factions**
> Washington warned that political parties ("factions") would become tools of ambition that would divide the nation and undermine republican government. This warning was already being ignored as he wrote — the Federalist and Democratic-Republican parties were in fierce competition.
>
> **2. Permanent Foreign Alliances**
> Washington warned against "permanent alliances" with foreign nations, arguing America should trade with all but have permanent political ties with none. This became the basis of American **isolationism** for over a century.
>
> **3. Sectionalism**
> Washington warned against the North-South divide growing around issues like slavery — a danger that would eventually cause the [[Civil War]].

---

## Legacy

> [!info] Long-Term Impact
> The Farewell Address shaped American foreign policy toward neutrality and non-intervention for over 100 years — until World War I and World War II forced America to abandon isolationism. It is still read aloud in the U.S. Senate each year on Washington's birthday.
