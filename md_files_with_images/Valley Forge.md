# Valley Forge

## Overview

**Valley Forge** (December 1777 – June 1778) was the site of the **Continental Army's** winter encampment outside Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, and became one of the most iconic moments of the [[American Revolution]]. During six months of brutal cold, disease, and shortage, approximately **2,000 soldiers died** — yet the army that emerged in spring was a transformed, professional fighting force.

## The Suffering

Washington's army of roughly 12,000 men arrived at Valley Forge in December 1777 after a demoralizing fall campaign in which the British had captured Philadelphia. Soldiers lacked adequate food, clothing, and shelter. Men marched barefoot through snow, leaving bloody footprints. Disease (typhus, smallpox, dysentery) killed far more than battle.

## The Transformation

The winter at Valley Forge was also a period of intensive military training. **Baron Friedrich von Steuben**, a Prussian military officer, drilled the soldiers relentlessly, transforming untrained militiamen into disciplined professional soldiers using his *Regulations for the Order and Discipline of the Troops of the United States*.

## Significance

Valley Forge represents the endurance and determination that **[[George Washington]]** and his soldiers showed in keeping the Revolution alive against enormous odds. The army that marched out in June 1778 was significantly more capable and went on to fight successfully until victory at **[[Battle of Yorktown|Yorktown]]**.
