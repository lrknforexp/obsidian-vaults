# First Amendment

## Overview

The **First Amendment** to the U.S. Constitution, ratified in **1791** as part of the **[[Bill of Rights]]**, protects five fundamental freedoms: **freedom of religion** (both free exercise and non-establishment), **freedom of speech**, **freedom of the press**, **freedom of peaceful assembly**, and the right to **petition the government**.

## Historical Context

The First Amendment was demanded by **[[Anti-Federalists]]** who feared the new federal government would suppress the very liberties the Revolution was fought to protect. **[[James Madison]]** drafted it and shepherded the Bill of Rights through Congress.

## Key Protections

The **Establishment Clause** prohibits the government from establishing an official religion. The **Free Exercise Clause** protects individuals' right to practice their religion. Freedom of speech and press protect political dissent — crucial for a functioning democracy. The right of assembly allows citizens to organize and protest.

## Early Tests

The **[[Alien and Sedition Acts]]** (1798) directly challenged First Amendment principles by criminalizing criticism of the government. Jefferson and Madison argued in the **[[Virginia and Kentucky Resolutions]]** that these laws were unconstitutional.

## Significance

The First Amendment is foundational to American democracy. It protects the political speech and religious liberty that the colonists had fought Britain to secure.
