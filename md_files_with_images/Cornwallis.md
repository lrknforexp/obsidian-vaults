# Cornwallis

## Overview

**Charles Cornwallis** (1738–1805) was a British general who served as one of the principal commanders of British forces during the [[American Revolution]]. He is best remembered for his surrender at **[[Battle of Yorktown|Yorktown]]** in 1781, which effectively ended the war.

## Military Career in America

Cornwallis participated in key early battles including the **Battle of Long Island** (1776) and the **Philadelphia Campaign** (1777). He was an aggressive and capable commander who won several engagements in the South, including the **Battle of Camden** (1780).

## The Southern Campaign

After initial successes in South Carolina, Cornwallis pushed into North Carolina and Virginia. His army was bloodied at the **Battle of Guilford Court House** (1781) — technically a British victory but with severe casualties — weakening his force before the final campaign.

## Yorktown and Surrender

Cornwallis marched to Yorktown, Virginia, where he became trapped by [[George Washington]]'s combined American-French army on land and the French fleet at sea. After a three-week siege, he surrendered his army of roughly 8,000 men on **October 19, 1781**, sending a subordinate to hand over his sword, claiming he was too ill to attend.

## Later Life

Cornwallis went on to a distinguished later career as Governor-General of India and Viceroy of Ireland.
