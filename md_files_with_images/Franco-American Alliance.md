# Franco-American Alliance

## Overview

The **Franco-American Alliance** (1778) was a military and political alliance between the United States and France during the [[American Revolution]]. It brought France officially into the war on the American side, providing crucial military support — including troops, naval power, and supplies — that proved decisive in achieving American independence.

## Background

France had been secretly supplying the American rebels since 1776 through a front company, motivated by a desire to weaken its longtime rival Britain. After the American victory at the **Battle of Saratoga** (October 1777) — proving the Americans could win — France decided to openly enter the war.

## Terms of the Alliance

France recognized American independence and agreed to fight alongside the Americans until independence was secured. The U.S. agreed not to make a separate peace with Britain without French consent. France also allied with Spain and the Netherlands against Britain, turning a colonial rebellion into a global war.

## Military Impact

French support was essential: French troops fought at **[[Battle of Yorktown|Yorktown]]**, and crucially, the French fleet under Admiral de Grasse defeated the British navy at the **Battle of the Chesapeake**, trapping Cornwallis and making the decisive American victory possible.
