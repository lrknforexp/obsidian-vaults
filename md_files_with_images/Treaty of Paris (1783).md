# Treaty of Paris (1783)

## Overview

The **Treaty of Paris (1783)** formally ended the **[[American Revolution]]** and established the United States as an independent nation. Negotiated by **[[Benjamin Franklin]]**, **[[John Adams]]**, and John Jay, the treaty was remarkably favorable to the United States, granting vast territorial concessions from Britain.

## Negotiation

The American negotiators were instructed to consult with France (their ally) but ultimately negotiated a separate preliminary agreement with Britain before informing the French. This angered France but secured better terms for the Americans.

## Key Terms

Britain recognized American **independence** and sovereignty. The United States received all territory east of the Mississippi River, south of Canada, and north of Florida. Britain retained Canada. Americans were given **fishing rights** off Newfoundland. Congress was to "earnestly recommend" that states return property confiscated from Loyalists and pay debts owed to British creditors (provisions widely ignored).

## Significance

The treaty gave the young United States far more land than many expected — doubling or tripling the territory of the original colonies. This vast territory became the source of **[[Manifest Destiny]]** and westward expansion debates for the next seven decades.
