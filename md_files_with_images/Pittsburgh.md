# Pittsburgh

## Overview

**Pittsburgh**, Pennsylvania, located at the confluence of the Allegheny and Monongahela Rivers (forming the Ohio River), was a strategically crucial location in colonial and early American history. It grew from a contested frontier fort into one of America's first industrial cities, becoming the center of iron and steel production.

## Fort Pitt

The site of Pittsburgh was the location of **Fort Duquesne** (French) and later **Fort Pitt** (British), a key battleground of the **[[French and Indian War]]**. George Washington's early military career involved failed attempts to seize the French fort in 1754.

## Whiskey Rebellion

Pittsburgh was at the center of the **[[Whiskey Rebellion]]** of 1794. Western Pennsylvania farmers, dependent on converting grain to whiskey for transport and trade, revolted against the federal excise tax on whiskey. President [[George Washington]] personally led a militia force to suppress the rebellion, demonstrating federal authority.

## Industrial Growth

By the early 19th century, Pittsburgh's location at the head of the Ohio River, surrounded by coal and iron deposits, made it ideal for manufacturing. It became America's leading iron producer and later the center of the steel industry, earning the nickname "Steel City."
