# Patrick Henry

## Overview

**Patrick Henry** (1736–1799) was a Virginia lawyer, politician, and orator who became one of the most passionate voices for American independence and individual liberty. Famous for his declaration **"Give me liberty, or give me death!"** (1775), he was a leading figure of the [[American Revolution]] and later a prominent **[[Anti-Federalists|Anti-Federalist]]**.

## Revolutionary Career

Henry burst onto the colonial political scene with his fiery opposition to the **[[Stamp Act]]** in 1765, arguing in the Virginia House of Burgesses that only Virginians could tax Virginians (the "Stamp Act Resolves"). He was elected to the **[[First Continental Congress]]** and became Governor of Virginia twice during the Revolution.

## Anti-Federalism

Henry was one of the most powerful voices against ratification of the Constitution at the Virginia ratifying convention (1788). He feared the strong central government would destroy states' rights and individual liberties, and argued passionately for a **[[Bill of Rights]]** before ratification.

## "Give Me Liberty"

His most famous speech, delivered at the Second Virginia Convention in March 1775, urged Virginia to join the armed resistance against Britain. The exact words were recorded by a biographer years later, but the passionate spirit of the speech was universally acknowledged.

## Legacy

Henry's insistence on a Bill of Rights influenced Madison's eventual drafting of the first ten amendments, making him an indirect architect of America's fundamental liberties.
