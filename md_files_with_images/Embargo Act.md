# Embargo Act

## Overview

The **Embargo Act of 1807** was a law signed by President **[[Thomas Jefferson]]** that prohibited American ships from trading with foreign ports. It was intended to punish Britain and France for seizing American merchant ships during the Napoleonic Wars and to demonstrate American economic power — but it proved a disastrous failure that hurt American merchants far more than European powers.

## Background

Britain and France were locked in a massive war. Both nations seized American ships trading with the other. Britain also practiced **impressment** — forcibly taking sailors from American ships and pressing them into the Royal Navy. Jefferson, philosophically opposed to war, sought an economic solution.

## The Embargo

The Act banned all exports from American ports. Jefferson believed Europe depended on American trade and would be forced to respect American neutrality. The reality was different: Britain found alternative suppliers, while American farmers, merchants, and shippers suffered enormous losses.

## Failure and Repeal

The embargo devastated the American economy, especially in New England where commerce was vital. Smuggling became widespread. Congress repealed it in 1809 just before Jefferson left office, replacing it with the weaker Non-Intercourse Act.

## Significance

The embargo's failure showed the limits of **economic coercion** as foreign policy and contributed to the tensions that ultimately led to the **[[War of 1812]]**.
