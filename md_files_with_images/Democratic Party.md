# Democratic Party

## Overview

The **Democratic Party** is one of the two major American political parties. It evolved from the **[[Democratic-Republican Party]]** founded by [[Thomas Jefferson]] and [[James Madison]]. After the collapse of that party in the "Era of Good Feelings," **[[Andrew Jackson]]** and his allies reorganized it as the Democratic Party in the late 1820s–1830s.

## Origins

Jackson's political coalition — supporting states' rights, limited federal government, westward expansion, and the interests of the "common man" over Eastern elites — formed the foundation of the new Democratic Party. Jackson's 1828 election is considered the party's founding moment.

## Antebellum Era

The Democratic Party dominated American politics through the 1840s–1850s, but was increasingly defined by its support for **Southern interests** and resistance to restrictions on slavery's expansion. The party split in 1860 between Northern and Southern factions, allowing Republican **Abraham Lincoln** to win the presidency.

## Civil War and Reconstruction

Democrats opposed many Reconstruction measures and remained the dominant party in the South for decades after the [[Civil War]] (the "Solid South").

## Significance for APUSH

The Democratic Party's formation represents the **Second Party System**, the rise of mass democratic politics, and the growing sectional tensions over slavery that would eventually cause the Civil War.
