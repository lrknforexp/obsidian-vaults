# First Continental Congress

## Overview

The **First Continental Congress** met in Philadelphia in **September–October 1774**, bringing together delegates from twelve of the thirteen colonies (Georgia did not attend) in response to Britain's **Coercive Acts** (Intolerable Acts), passed to punish Massachusetts after the Boston Tea Party.

## Background

Britain's response to colonial resistance — closing Boston's port, revoking Massachusetts's self-government, and quartering soldiers in colonists' homes — alarmed colonies beyond Massachusetts. Leaders recognized a need for coordinated action.

## What the Congress Did

The Congress agreed to boycott British goods through the **Continental Association** and gave Britain until the following spring to repeal the Coercive Acts, promising further action if it did not. It drafted petitions to King George III and Parliament asserting colonial rights. Crucially, it agreed to reconvene in May 1775 — setting up the **[[Second Continental Congress]]**.

## Significance

The First Continental Congress was the first major unified political action by the colonies and established an intercolonial political network. When Britain refused to address colonial grievances, the colonies moved from protest toward revolution. By the time the Second Continental Congress met in 1775, fighting had already begun at **Lexington and Concord**.
