# U.S. dollar bill

## Overview

The **U.S. dollar bill** is the fundamental unit of American paper currency. The dollar as a unit was officially adopted by Congress in 1792 (Coinage Act), though paper dollars became dominant much later. The faces on American currency reflect historical choices about which figures best represent national values and economic history.

## Hamilton and the Dollar

**[[Alexander Hamilton]]**, the first Secretary of the Treasury, established the financial architecture that made a stable American dollar possible: the national bank, assumption of war debts, and a functioning credit system. Hamilton's portrait appears on the $10 bill.

## Key Figures on Currency

- **$1 bill:** [[George Washington]] — the father of the nation
- **$2 bill:** [[Thomas Jefferson]] — author of the [[Declaration of Independence]]
- **$5 bill:** Abraham Lincoln — preserver of the Union
- **$10 bill:** [[Alexander Hamilton]] — architect of American finance
- **$20 bill:** [[Andrew Jackson]] — champion of the "common man" (controversially, given his role in the [[Trail of Tears]])
- **$50 bill:** [[Ulysses S. Grant]] — Union general and president

## National Bank Debates

The debates over the **[[National Bank]]** — whether the federal government should control currency and credit — were among the most contentious of the early republic. Jackson abolished the Second Bank; its absence led to financial instability.
