# George Whitefield

![[George_Whitefield_preaching.jpg]]

## Overview

**George Whitefield** (1714–1770) was a British Anglican minister and one of the most influential preachers of the **[[First Great Awakening]]**. His theatrical, emotionally powerful open-air preaching style drew massive crowds throughout the American colonies and Britain, making him arguably the first transatlantic celebrity.

## Preaching Style

Whitefield was a theatrical and passionate speaker who could project his voice to enormous crowds without amplification — the actor David Garrick reportedly said Whitefield could move audiences to tears simply by saying "Mesopotamia." He preached to crowds of thousands in fields and town squares, deliberately bypassing established church buildings.

## Tours of the Colonies

Whitefield made **seven tours** of the American colonies between 1738 and 1770. His 1739–1740 tour was the defining event of the First Great Awakening, drawing crowds of up to 20,000 in Boston and Philadelphia. Even the skeptical [[Benjamin Franklin]] attended and was impressed, though he remained unconverted.

## Theological Message

Whitefield preached **Calvinist** theology emphasizing the need for dramatic personal conversion ("new birth") and God's grace. He criticized the established clergy for cold, intellectual preaching that failed to stir souls.

## Significance

Whitefield's revivals helped create a shared intercolonial religious culture and demonstrated that Americans across colonial boundaries shared common experiences — a precondition for later political unity.
