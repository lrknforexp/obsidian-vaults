# Westward Expansion

![[American_Progress_Gast.jpg]]

## Overview

**Westward Expansion** refers to the 19th-century process by which the United States extended its territory from the original thirteen states on the Atlantic coast to the Pacific Ocean. Driven by **[[Manifest Destiny]]**, the belief that American expansion was divinely ordained, it involved land acquisitions, wars, treaties, and the displacement of hundreds of thousands of Native Americans.

## Key Acquisitions

The **[[Louisiana Purchase]]** (1803) doubled U.S. territory overnight. The **[[Adams-Onís Treaty]]** (1819) acquired Florida. The **[[Texas Annexation]]** (1845) brought in the vast Southwest. The **[[Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo]]** (1848) after the [[Mexican-American War]] added California and the Southwest. The **Oregon Treaty** (1846) divided the Pacific Northwest with Britain.

## Migration Routes

Hundreds of thousands of settlers moved west on the **Oregon Trail**, **California Trail**, and **Santa Fe Trail**. The California Gold Rush (1848) accelerated migration dramatically.

## Impact on Native Americans

Westward expansion was catastrophic for Native Americans. The **[[Indian Removal Act]]** forced tribes from the East. The **[[Trail of Tears]]** killed thousands. As settlers moved west, wars, disease, and broken treaties destroyed Native nations across the continent.
