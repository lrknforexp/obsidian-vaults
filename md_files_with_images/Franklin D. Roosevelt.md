# Franklin D. Roosevelt

## Overview

**Franklin Delano Roosevelt** (1882–1945), known as **FDR**, was the 32nd President of the United States, serving from 1933 until his death in 1945 — the longest presidency in American history. He led the nation through two of its greatest crises: the **Great Depression** and **World War II**.

## Early Career

A distant cousin of Theodore Roosevelt, FDR served as New York State Senator, Assistant Secretary of the Navy, and Governor of New York. He contracted polio in 1921, leaving him largely paralyzed from the waist down — a disability he carefully concealed from the public.

## The New Deal

Elected during the depths of the Great Depression (25% unemployment), Roosevelt launched the **New Deal** — a sweeping package of relief, recovery, and reform programs including Social Security, the FDIC, the SEC, the CCC, and the WPA. While debated by historians, the New Deal reshaped the relationship between the federal government and American citizens.

## World War II

FDR guided the U.S. through WWII, forging the Allied coalition with Britain and the Soviet Union. He died on April 12, 1945, just weeks before Germany's surrender, having led the nation to the brink of victory.

## Constitutional Legacy

FDR's four terms led directly to the **[[22nd Amendment]]** limiting presidents to two terms.
