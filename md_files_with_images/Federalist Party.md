> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Active:** 1789–1820s
> **Leaders:** [[Alexander Hamilton]], [[John Adams]], [[John Jay]]
> **Ideology:** Strong central government, loose constitutional interpretation, mercantile economy, pro-British foreign policy
> **Opposition:** [[Democratic-Republican Party]]
> **Notable Achievements:** Ratification of the Constitution, National Bank, Jay's Treaty

---

The **Federalist Party** was the **first political party** in United States history, emerging in the late 1780s and 1790s around support for the newly ratified [[U.S. Constitution]] and [[Alexander Hamilton]]'s ambitious economic program. Federalists believed in a strong national government, a professional military, and close ties with [[Great Britain]]. They dominated American politics through the 1790s but collapsed as a national force after 1800, becoming extinct by the 1820s.

---

## Origins

The Federalist Party grew directly out of the debate over the [[U.S. Constitution]] at the [[Constitutional Convention]] (1787). Those who supported ratification — calling themselves **Federalists** — were generally wealthy merchants, lawyers, and landowners concentrated in the Northeast. Their opponents, the **[[Anti-Federalists]]**, feared the Constitution gave the federal government too much power.

> [!info] The Federalist Papers
> To argue for ratification, [[Alexander Hamilton]], [[James Madison]], and [[John Jay]] wrote **85 essays** collectively known as the **[[Federalist Papers]]** — one of the most important works of political philosophy in American history. The essays argued that a strong central government was not a threat to liberty but a guarantee of it.
>
> Note: Madison later broke with Hamilton and became a leader of the opposition [[Democratic-Republican Party]].

---

## Core Beliefs

| Belief | Details |
|---|---|
| **Strong central government** | The national government should be supreme over the states |
| **Loose constitutional interpretation** | The Constitution grants **implied powers** — the government can do things not explicitly listed if they serve the national interest |
| **[[National Bank]]** | A federally chartered bank was essential for economic stability |
| **Manufacturing economy** | Favored tariffs and government support for commerce and manufacturing over agrarian ideals |
| **Pro-British foreign policy** | Britain was America's most important trading partner; conflict with Britain would be economically devastating |
| **Strong military** | A professional standing army and navy were necessary for national security |

---

## Hamilton's Economic Program

The Federalist economic agenda, designed by [[Alexander Hamilton]], was the defining domestic policy of the era:

> [!info] Hamilton's Financial Plan
> 1. **Assumption** — the federal government assumed all state debts from the Revolution, tying creditors' financial interests to the survival of the federal government
> 2. **[[National Bank]]** — the First Bank of the United States (chartered 1791) stabilized the currency and provided government financing
> 3. **Tariffs** — protective tariffs on imported goods encouraged American manufacturing
> 4. **Excise taxes** — including the whiskey tax that sparked the [[Whiskey Rebellion]]
>
> Jefferson and Madison argued this plan was **unconstitutional** (Congress had no explicit power to charter a bank) and that it unfairly benefited wealthy Northern merchants over Southern farmers.

---

## Key Leaders

**[[Alexander Hamilton]]** — Secretary of the Treasury under Washington; the intellectual architect of Federalism. He died in an 1804 duel with [[Aaron Burr]].

**[[John Adams]]** — Second President of the United States (1797–1801); the only Federalist to win the presidency after Washington. His signing of the [[Alien and Sedition Acts]] badly damaged the party's popularity.

**[[John Jay]]** — First Chief Justice of the Supreme Court; negotiated [[Jay's Treaty]]; co-author of the [[Federalist Papers]].

---

## Decline and Fall

The Federalists were devastated by the **Election of 1800**, in which [[Thomas Jefferson]] defeated [[John Adams]]. Several factors contributed to their collapse:

> [!info] Why the Federalists Failed
> - The **[[Alien and Sedition Acts]]** (1798) were widely seen as a dangerous attack on free speech and civil liberties, turning public opinion against the party
> - Hamilton's economic policies benefited wealthy elites and alienated farmers and working-class voters
> - The party was dominated by Northeastern merchants and had little appeal in the agrarian South and West
> - Hamilton's **death in 1804** removed the party's most dynamic leader
> - The party **opposed the [[War of 1812]]** and even discussed secession at the [[Hartford Convention]] (1814–1815) — after which they were permanently discredited as unpatriotic

The Federalist Party effectively ceased to exist as a national political force by the 1820s.

---

## Legacy

Though the Federalists failed as a party, their ideas survived:
- The [[National Bank]] and Hamilton's economic framework shaped American capitalism
- The principle of **loose constitutional interpretation** (implied powers) was validated by the Supreme Court in [[McCulloch v. Maryland]] (1819)
- Federalist foreign policy caution — avoiding entangling alliances — influenced American diplomacy for over a century
