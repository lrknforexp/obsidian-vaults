# Andrew Jackson

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Born:** March 15, 1767 — Waxhaws, South Carolina/North Carolina border
> **Died:** June 8, 1845 — Nashville, Tennessee
> **Presidency:** 1829–1837 (7th President of the United States)
> **Party:** [[Democratic Party]] (founder)
> **Known For:** Battle of New Orleans, [[Indian Removal Act]], Bank War, "Jacksonian Democracy"

---

**Andrew Jackson** was the 7th President of the United States, the founder of the modern **[[Democratic Party]]**, and one of the most transformative — and controversial — figures in American history. A war hero from the [[War of 1812]], he championed the "common man" against elite institutions, destroyed the **[[National Bank]]**, and forced the brutal removal of Native Americans from the Southeast. He was the first president from outside the original colonial elite (Virginia or Massachusetts), and his presidency defined a new era of mass democratic politics.

---

## Early Life

Jackson was born in poverty on the Carolina frontier. Both his parents died before he was 15 — his mother of cholera while nursing prisoners during the [[American Revolution]], in which the teenage Jackson also served, being captured and scarred by a British officer's sword. He was entirely self-made — a lawyer, land speculator, and soldier from Tennessee.

---

## Military Hero

Jackson's military career made him nationally famous:

### Battle of Horseshoe Bend (1814)
Jackson decisively defeated the **Creek Nation** in Alabama, opening massive tracts of Native land in the Deep South to American settlement.

### Battle of New Orleans (January 8, 1815)

> [!info] The Hero of New Orleans
> Leading a mixed force of regulars, militia, free Black soldiers, and pirates under Jean Lafitte, Jackson inflicted a crushing defeat on a British force attempting to seize New Orleans — **two weeks after the [[War of 1812]] had formally ended** via the Treaty of Ghent. The victory made him the most celebrated man in America and launched his political career.

---

## Road to the Presidency

### Election of 1824 — The "Corrupt Bargain"

Jackson won the most popular votes and most electoral votes in 1824 but failed to win an outright majority. The election went to the [[House of Representatives]], which chose **[[John Quincy Adams]]** after [[Henry Clay]] threw his support to Adams. Adams then made Clay his Secretary of State.

Jackson called this a **"corrupt bargain"** — an outrage he nursed for four years while organizing his political comeback.

### Election of 1828

Jackson won decisively, running as the champion of ordinary farmers, workers, and frontiersmen against the corrupt Eastern elite. His inauguration was famously chaotic — crowds of supporters swarmed the White House, standing on furniture and breaking china.

---

## Presidency (1829–1837)

### Jacksonian Democracy

> [!info] The Common Man
> Jackson expanded democratic participation — he championed the elimination of property requirements for voting, opened government appointments to ordinary citizens (the **spoils system**), and positioned himself as a tribune of the people against entrenched privilege.

### The Indian Removal Act (1830)

> [!info] Trail of Tears
> Jackson pushed the **[[Indian Removal Act]]** through Congress, authorizing the forced relocation of all Native American tribes east of the Mississippi River to "Indian Territory" west of Arkansas. The **Trail of Tears** (1838–1839) — primarily the forced march of the Cherokee Nation — resulted in the deaths of approximately **4,000–6,000 people** from disease, starvation, and exposure. The Supreme Court ruled the removal unconstitutional in *Worcester v. Georgia* (1832); Jackson reportedly said "John Marshall has made his decision, now let him enforce it."

### The Bank War

Jackson vetoed the recharter of the **[[National Bank]]** in 1832, calling it a corrupt monopoly serving wealthy elites and foreign investors. He then systematically dismantled it by withdrawing federal deposits. The resulting financial instability contributed to the severe **Panic of 1837** depression.

### Nullification Crisis (1832)

South Carolina declared federal tariff laws null and void within the state (echoing the [[Virginia and Kentucky Resolutions]]). Jackson threatened to send federal troops to collect the tariffs and to hang nullification leaders. [[Henry Clay]] brokered a compromise that defused the crisis — but Jackson's firm defense of federal supremacy over state nullification was one of his most important presidential legacies.

---

## Legacy

Jackson is one of the most divisive figures in American history:
- **Admirers** see him as a champion of democracy, the common man, and strong executive leadership
- **Critics** point to the Indian removal — a genocide by modern standards — and his defense of slavery as his defining legacy
- His face appears on the **$20 bill** (though there have been ongoing efforts to replace it with Harriet Tubman)
