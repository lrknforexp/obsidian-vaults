# Three-Fifths Compromise

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Date:** 1787
> **Location:** [[Constitutional Convention]], Philadelphia
> **Provision:** Enslaved people counted as ⅗ of a person for congressional representation and taxation
> **Effect:** Dramatically increased Southern states' political power in the [[House of Representatives]]
> **Abolished by:** [[14th Amendment]] (1868)

---

The **Three-Fifths Compromise** was one of the most consequential — and morally compromised — deals struck at the **[[Constitutional Convention]]** of 1787. It determined that enslaved people would count as **three-fifths (⅗) of a free person** for the purposes of apportioning seats in the [[House of Representatives]] and direct taxes. While framed as a compromise between North and South, it enormously amplified the political power of slaveholders and embedded slavery into the Constitution.

---

## Background: The Representation Problem

At the Constitutional Convention, delegates had to decide how to apportion seats in the new House of Representatives — by population, since larger states would have more seats.

This immediately raised a question with no clean answer: **Would enslaved people be counted?**

> [!info] The Two Positions
> **Southern states** wanted enslaved people counted fully — this would give them far more seats in the House (and thus more power), even though enslaved people had no rights and no votes.
>
> **Northern states** argued that if enslaved people were property (as Southern law defined them), they shouldn't count for representation at all — just as nobody proposed counting livestock or furniture.
>
> Each position was logically consistent within its own premises but deeply self-interested.

---

## The Compromise

James Wilson of Pennsylvania proposed a solution that had been used under the [[Articles of Confederation]] for taxation purposes: count enslaved people as **three-fifths** of a free person.

> [!info] The Math
> Under the Three-Fifths Compromise:
> - If a state had 100 free people and 100 enslaved people, it counted as having **160 people** for representation purposes (100 + ⅗ × 100)
> - This gave slaveholding states **significantly more** seats in the House than they would have had if only free people were counted
> - It also affected the [[Electoral College]], which was based on congressional representation — giving slaveholding states more presidential electors

---

## Impact on American Politics

> [!info] The "Slave Power"
> The Three-Fifths Compromise gave slaveholders **disproportionate political power** for the next 78 years:
>
> - Southern states had more House seats than their free population justified
> - This translated into more presidential electors and more influence over federal appointments
> - Of the first 16 presidents, **12 were slaveholders**; of the first 36 Speakers of the House, **21 were from slave states**
> - Critics called this the **"Slave Power"** — the outsized influence of slaveholders over the federal government
>
> Anti-slavery advocates argued that counting enslaved people for representation while denying them any rights was a grotesque logical contradiction — and they were right.

---

## Moral Contradiction

> [!info] The Fundamental Problem
> The Three-Fifths Compromise forced the Constitution to simultaneously treat enslaved people as:
> - **Property** — for purposes of the law, they had no rights
> - **People** — for purposes of representation, they were counted (at 60%)
>
> This contradiction could not be permanently papered over. The growing incompatibility between the language of liberty in the [[Declaration of Independence]] and the reality of slavery — amplified by the political power the Three-Fifths Compromise gave slaveholders — was a central driver of the crisis that produced the [[Civil War]].

---

## End of the Compromise

The Three-Fifths Compromise was effectively abolished by the **[[14th Amendment]]** (1868), ratified after the [[Civil War]], which declared that all persons born or naturalized in the United States were citizens and that all persons should be counted equally for representation.
