# AP United States History — Course Notes

> [!info] About This Course
> **Advanced Placement United States History** (also known as **AP U.S. History** or **APUSH**) is a college-level survey course and examination offered by the [[College Board]] as part of the [[Advanced Placement Program]]. The course spans nine chronological periods from the **pre-Columbian era** to the **present day**.

## Exam Format

> [!info] AP Exam Structure
> The AP U.S. History exam is **3 hours and 15 minutes** long and consists of:
> - **Section I** — Multiple Choice (55 questions, 55 min) + Short Answer Questions (3 questions, 40 min)
> - **Section II** — Document-Based Question / DBQ (1 question, 60 min) + Long Essay Question / LEQ (1 question, 40 min)

---

## Key Themes (AP Historical Thinking Skills)

These themes recur across all units and are central to the exam:

- **[[Causation]]** — What caused major events? What were their short- and long-term effects?
- **[[Continuity and Change Over Time]]** — What changed across time, and what stayed the same?
- **[[Comparison]]** — How did different regions, groups, or time periods differ from one another?
- **[[Contextualization]]** — How does the broader historical context shape specific events?
- **[[Argumentation]]** — How do historians construct and support historical arguments with evidence?

## Quick Reference — Major Documents & Legislation

| Document | Date | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| [[Law of Burgos]] | 1512 | First Spanish laws governing treatment of Native Americans |
| [[Navigation Acts]] | 1651–1696 | Restricted colonial trade to benefit Britain; fueled colonial resentment |
| [[Articles of Confederation]] | 1777 | America's first (failed) constitution; too weak to govern effectively |
| [[U.S. Constitution]] | 1787 | Replaced the AOC; established the federal government with three branches |
| [[Bill of Rights]] | 1791 | First ten amendments; guaranteed individual rights to appease Anti-Federalists |
| [[Jay's Treaty]] | 1794 | Resolved conflicts with Britain; established president as chief foreign diplomat |
| [[Alien and Sedition Acts]] | 1798 | Restricted immigration and free speech; triggered the nullification debate |
| [[Virginia and Kentucky Resolutions]] | 1798–1799 | Jefferson and Madison's argument that states could nullify unconstitutional federal laws |
| [[Marbury v. Madison]] | 1803 | Supreme Court case establishing the power of judicial review |
| [[Louisiana Purchase]] | 1803 | Doubled U.S. territory for $15 million; acquired from Napoleon's France |
| [[Missouri Compromise]] | 1820 | Temporarily resolved slavery expansion debate; established the 36°30' line |
| [[Erie Canal]] | 1825 | Man-made waterway connecting the Great Lakes to NYC; transformed trade |
| [[Indian Removal Act]] | 1830 | Forced relocation of Native American tribes to west of the Mississippi |
| [[Kansas-Nebraska Act]] | 1854 | Repealed the Missouri Compromise; reignited sectional crisis over slavery |

> [!info] Study Tip
> For each unit, make sure you can explain **causation** (why did it happen?), **significance** (why does it matter?), and **continuity/change** (what changed, what stayed the same?). The AP exam rewards **analytical thinking and argumentation**, not just memorization of facts. Practice writing thesis statements for every major topic.
