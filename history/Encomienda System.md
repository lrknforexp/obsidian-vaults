# Encomienda System

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Used by:** Spanish colonizers in [[New Spain]]
> **Period:** 1500s–1700s
> **Mechanism:** Spanish colonists (encomenderos) given authority over Native American laborers
> **Regulated by:** [[Law of Burgos]] (1512)
> **Result:** Exploitation of Native labor; demographic collapse of indigenous populations

---

The **encomienda system** was the Spanish colonial forced labor arrangement under which the Crown granted a Spanish colonist (an **encomendero**) authority over a designated number of Native Americans who were required to provide labor or tribute in exchange for supposed "protection" and Christian instruction.

## How It Worked

The Spanish Crown granted an encomendero:
- Authority over a **specific territory** and its Native inhabitants
- The right to **demand labor or tribute** from Native workers
- Nominal obligation to provide food, housing, and Christian education

In theory, it was a mutual exchange. In practice, it was **slavery under a different name**.

> [!info] The Gap Between Law and Reality
> [[Ferdinand and Isabella]] and later Spanish monarchs issued laws attempting to limit abuse — including the **[[Law of Burgos]]** (1512). Encomenderos systematically ignored these protections. They forced Native people into brutal labor in mines and fields, paid little or nothing, and physically abused workers who resisted.

## Effects

- **Demographic collapse** — combined with epidemic disease ([[Smallpox]], measles, influenza), the system contributed to the deaths of **more than half** of the Native American population within a century of contact
- **Cultural destruction** — forced conversion to Christianity, suppression of Native languages and practices
- **Resistance** — Native peoples revolted repeatedly; many practiced traditional religions in secret
