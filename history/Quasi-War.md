# Quasi-War

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Date:** 1798–1800
> **Parties:** United States vs. France
> **Type:** Undeclared naval war
> **Theater:** Caribbean Sea and Atlantic Ocean
> **Result:** Convention of 1800 (Treaty of Mortefontaine); normalized U.S.-French relations
> **U.S. President:** [[John Adams]]

---

The **Quasi-War** was an **undeclared naval conflict** between the United States and the [[French Republic]] fought primarily in the Caribbean Sea and Atlantic Ocean between **1798 and 1800**. It was never formally declared by either side, hence the name "quasi" (meaning "almost" or "seemingly") — but it involved hundreds of naval engagements and posed a serious threat to American commerce and sovereignty. The crisis also directly produced the [[Alien and Sedition Acts]] and contributed to the creation of the permanent **U.S. Navy**.

---

## Background: The Alliance Problem

The roots of the Quasi-War lay in the revolutionary era:

- During the [[American Revolution]], France had been America's most vital ally, providing troops, ships, and funding
- The 1778 **[[Franco-American Alliance]]** committed the U.S. to defend French possessions in the Caribbean
- The [[French Revolution]] (1789) and subsequent wars with Britain put the U.S. in an impossible position: honor the French alliance (and go to war with Britain) or stay neutral

> [!info] Jay's Treaty as Trigger
> President [[George Washington]] chose neutrality and negotiated **[[Jay's Treaty]]** with Britain in 1794. France viewed this as a **betrayal of the alliance** and a de facto alignment with Britain, France's enemy. In retaliation, French naval vessels and privateers began **seizing American merchant ships** in the Caribbean.

---

## The XYZ Affair (1797–1798)

President [[John Adams]] attempted to resolve the crisis diplomatically, sending three envoys — [[Charles Cotesworth Pinckney]], [[John Marshall]], and [[Elbridge Gerry]] — to Paris to negotiate.

> [!info] The XYZ Affair
> French Foreign Minister [[Talleyrand]]'s agents (referred to in published dispatches only as **"X," "Y," and "Z"**) demanded that the American envoys:
> - Pay a **bribe of $250,000** to Talleyrand personally
> - Provide France with a **$10 million loan**
> - Formally apologize for anti-French remarks Adams had made
>
> The Americans refused. Pinckney's reported response — **"No! No! Not a sixpence!"** — became a rallying cry. When Adams released the dispatches to Congress, public outrage erupted. The slogan **"Millions for defense, but not one cent for tribute!"** swept the country.

---

## The Naval War

With diplomacy failed and public fury at a peak, the U.S. began an undeclared naval war:

- Congress authorized the construction of new warships and the expansion of the Navy
- The **[[Department of the Navy]]** was created in 1798 (previously handled by the War Department)
- The [[U.S. Marine Corps]] was re-established in 1798
- American naval vessels and French ships fought **hundreds of engagements** in the Caribbean
- The most famous American success was the USS *Constellation* defeating the French frigate *L'Insurgente* in February 1799

> [!info] Scale of the Conflict
> Between 1798 and 1800, French vessels had seized approximately **800 American merchant ships**. The U.S. Navy recaptured dozens of vessels and inflicted significant losses on French naval forces in the Caribbean.

---

## Domestic Consequences: The Alien and Sedition Acts

The war fever generated by the XYZ Affair allowed the [[Federalist Party|Federalist]]-controlled Congress to pass the **[[Alien and Sedition Acts]]** (1798):

- Federalists argued that French agents and sympathizers within the U.S. posed a domestic threat
- The acts allowed the president to deport "dangerous" aliens and criminalized anti-government speech
- [[Thomas Jefferson]] and [[James Madison]] responded with the **[[Virginia and Kentucky Resolutions]]**, arguing the acts were unconstitutional

---

## Resolution

By 1799, Napoleon Bonaparte had seized power in France and had little interest in continuing the conflict with the U.S. Adams, against fierce opposition from his own [[Federalist Party]], sent new envoys to Paris.

> [!info] Convention of 1800
> The **Convention of 1800** (Treaty of Mortefontaine) formally ended the Quasi-War:
> - France agreed to stop seizing American ships
> - The U.S. released France from its obligations under the 1778 alliance
> - Relations were normalized
>
> Adams considered this his greatest achievement as president — he had **kept America out of a full-scale war** at enormous political cost, as the peace move enraged Federalist war hawks and contributed to his defeat in the [[Election of 1800]].

---

## Legacy

- Established the **permanent U.S. Navy** as an institution
- Demonstrated that the U.S. could defend its commercial interests on the high seas
- The [[Alien and Sedition Acts]] produced a constitutional crisis and accelerated the political rise of [[Thomas Jefferson]]
- Adams's peace decision split the Federalist Party and contributed to its eventual collapse
