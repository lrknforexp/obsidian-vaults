# John Adams

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Born:** October 30, 1735 — Braintree (now Quincy), Massachusetts
> **Died:** July 4, 1826 — Quincy, Massachusetts
> **Presidency:** 1797–1801 (2nd President of the United States)
> **Party:** [[Federalist Party]]
> **Vice President:** [[Thomas Jefferson]]
> **Known For:** [[Quasi-War]], [[Alien and Sedition Acts]], keeping the U.S. out of war with France

---

**John Adams** was a Founding Father, the first Vice President, and the **2nd President of the United States** (1797–1801). A brilliant lawyer and political theorist, he played a critical role in the [[American Revolution]] and the founding of the republic. His presidency was dominated by the naval crisis with France known as the [[Quasi-War]] and the deeply controversial [[Alien and Sedition Acts]]. He was the **only Federalist president besides [[George Washington]]**, and his decision to pursue peace with France — against his own party's wishes — ended his political career but may have saved the young republic from a catastrophic war.

---

## Early Life and the Revolution

Adams grew up in Massachusetts and became a successful lawyer in Boston. He was a delegate to both the [[First Continental Congress]] and the [[Second Continental Congress]], where he was one of the most forceful advocates for independence. He nominated [[George Washington]] to command the [[Continental Army]] and helped persuade Congress to adopt the [[Declaration of Independence]] in 1776.

> [!info] Defending the British Soldiers
> In one of the most principled acts of the founding era, Adams — a passionate Patriot — agreed to defend the British soldiers accused in the **[[Boston Massacre]]** (1770). He believed every accused person deserved a fair trial and secured acquittals for most of the soldiers. He later called it one of the best things he ever did.

---

## Diplomatic Career

Adams served as a diplomat in Europe throughout much of the Revolution and its aftermath:
- Helped negotiate the **[[Treaty of Paris (1783)]]**, which formally ended the Revolution and secured American independence
- Served as the first U.S. Ambassador to Great Britain (1785–1788)
- Helped negotiate critical wartime loans from the Netherlands

---

## Vice Presidency (1789–1797)

Adams served two terms as Vice President under [[George Washington]] — a role he famously called "the most insignificant office that ever the invention of man contrived." He presided over the Senate and cast numerous tie-breaking votes in support of the Federalist agenda.

---

## Presidency (1797–1801)

### The Quasi-War with France

Adams inherited a deteriorating relationship with France, which had been seizing American merchant ships in retaliation for [[Jay's Treaty]]. The [[XYZ Affair]] (1797–1798) outraged Americans when French agents demanded bribes before negotiations could begin.

> [!info] Adams's Greatest Achievement
> Despite enormous political pressure — from the public, from Congress, and from within his own [[Federalist Party]] — to declare war on France, Adams chose diplomacy. He sent new envoys to Paris in 1799, negotiating the **Convention of 1800** that ended the [[Quasi-War]].
>
> This decision almost certainly **prevented a catastrophic war** with France but destroyed his political coalition. War hawks in his own party, led by [[Alexander Hamilton]], worked to undermine his reelection. Adams later said keeping the peace with France was the greatest service he ever rendered his country.

### The Alien and Sedition Acts (1798)

Adams signed the [[Alien and Sedition Acts]] in 1798, during the height of the Quasi-War crisis. The acts allowed the president to deport dangerous aliens and criminalized anti-government speech. They were wildly unpopular and became a major issue in the [[Election of 1800]].

### The Midnight Judges

In the final days of his presidency, Adams rushed to appoint as many [[Federalist Party|Federalist]] judges as possible — the so-called **"[[midnight judges]]"** — to preserve Federalist influence in the judiciary after his electoral defeat. One of these appointments led directly to **[[Marbury v. Madison]]**.

---

## Election of 1800 and Retirement

Adams was defeated by [[Thomas Jefferson]] in the bitter **[[Election of 1800]]** — the first peaceful transfer of power between opposing parties in American history. Adams left Washington before Jefferson's inauguration, beginning a 12-year estrangement from his old friend.

The two eventually reconciled through a remarkable correspondence and died on the **same day — July 4, 1826** — the 50th anniversary of the [[Declaration of Independence]].

---

## Legacy

- Kept the U.S. out of a potentially devastating war with France at great personal political cost
- His son, **[[John Quincy Adams]]**, became the 6th President
- One of the most intellectually rigorous of the Founders — his writings on government theory remain important today
- His appointment of [[John Marshall]] as Chief Justice shaped American constitutional law for generations
