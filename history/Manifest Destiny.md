# Manifest Destiny

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Term Coined:** 1845 by journalist John O'Sullivan
> **Period:** Most active 1800s–1850s
> **Core Belief:** The United States was divinely destined to expand across the entire North American continent
> **Consequences:** [[Louisiana Purchase]], [[Mexican-American War]], [[Indian Removal Act]], displacement of Native peoples

---

**Manifest Destiny** was the 19th-century American belief that the United States was **destined by God** to expand across the entire North American continent, from the Atlantic Ocean to the Pacific. It served as the driving ideology behind American westward expansion, justifying land acquisitions, wars, and the violent displacement of Native American peoples. The doctrine made American expansion seem not merely desirable but divinely ordained — inevitable, righteous, and unstoppable.

---

## Origins of the Term

The phrase **"Manifest Destiny"** was coined by journalist and editor **John L. O'Sullivan** in 1845, who wrote that it was America's "manifest destiny to overspread the continent allotted by Providence for the free development of our yearly multiplying millions."

Though O'Sullivan named it, the idea was decades older — rooted in Puritan notions of a special American mission, the ideology of republican expansion, and racial beliefs about Anglo-Saxon superiority.

---

## Core Ideas

> [!info] The Three Pillars of Manifest Destiny
> 1. **Divine mission** — God had chosen the American people to spread across and "civilize" the continent
> 2. **Anglo-Saxon superiority** — American (white) settlers were racially and culturally superior to Native Americans, Mexicans, and others who occupied the land
> 3. **Republican virtue** — spreading American democratic institutions across the continent was a moral good for all of humanity

---

## Territorial Expansion Driven by Manifest Destiny

| Acquisition | Date | How |
|---|---|---|
| [[Louisiana Purchase]] | 1803 | Purchased from France for $15 million |
| Florida | 1819 | [[Adams-Onís Treaty]] with Spain |
| Texas | 1845 | Annexed after Texas Revolution |
| Oregon Territory | 1846 | Negotiated with Britain (49th parallel) |
| Mexican Cession | 1848 | Won through [[Mexican-American War]] |
| Gadsden Purchase | 1853 | Purchased from Mexico |

By 1853, the United States stretched from the Atlantic to the Pacific — exactly as Manifest Destiny had proclaimed.

---

## The Dark Side of Manifest Destiny

> [!info] Native American Displacement
> Manifest Destiny provided ideological cover for the **systematic destruction of Native American societies**:
> - The **[[Indian Removal Act]]** (1830) forcibly relocated tens of thousands of Native Americans west of the Mississippi River
> - The **Trail of Tears** (1838) — forced relocation of the Cherokee Nation — resulted in the deaths of thousands from disease, exposure, and starvation
> - Native peoples were routinely described as "savages" who stood in the way of civilization — language that dehumanized them and justified violence
>
> For Native Americans, Manifest Destiny meant dispossession, cultural destruction, and mass death.

---

## Manifest Destiny and Slavery

The expansion of American territory raised an explosive question: would slavery expand into the new lands? This debate — which the [[Missouri Compromise]] (1820) temporarily resolved — was reignited by every new acquisition and ultimately led to the [[Civil War]].

---

## Legacy

- Manifest Destiny transformed the United States from a coastal republic into a continental empire in less than 50 years
- It reshaped the lives of millions — Native Americans, Mexican citizens, and enslaved people — through dispossession, war, and forced migration
- The ideology of American exceptionalism and "special mission" that Manifest Destiny embodied has continued to influence American foreign policy through the 20th and 21st centuries
