# Aaron Burr

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Born:** February 6, 1756 — Newark, New Jersey
> **Died:** September 14, 1836 — Staten Island, New York
> **Role:** 3rd Vice President of the United States (1801–1805)
> **Party:** [[Democratic-Republican Party]]
> **Known For:** Tying [[Thomas Jefferson]] in the [[Election of 1800]]; killing [[Alexander Hamilton]] in a duel

---

**Aaron Burr** was a Founding-era politician, lawyer, and the 3rd Vice President of the United States. He is best remembered for two events: his role in the chaotic **[[Election of 1800]]**, which threw the presidential race to the [[House of Representatives]], and the **1804 duel** in which he fatally shot [[Alexander Hamilton]] — one of the most dramatic moments in American political history.

---

## Early Life and Revolutionary War

Burr was born into a prominent family — his grandfather was the theologian [[Jonathan Edwards]]. He served with distinction in the [[Continental Army]] during the [[American Revolution]], rising to the rank of lieutenant colonel.

---

## Political Career

Burr was a skilled political organizer in New York, serving in the U.S. Senate (1791–1797) and as a powerful figure in New York Democratic-Republican politics. He helped [[Thomas Jefferson]] organize the political machinery that won the [[Election of 1800]].

---

## The Election of 1800

> [!info] The Tied Election
> Under the original [[Electoral College]] rules, each elector cast **two votes for president** without distinguishing between president and vice president. Jefferson's running mate Burr received the same number of electoral votes as Jefferson — **73 each** — throwing the election to the [[House of Representatives]].
>
> [[Alexander Hamilton]], despite loathing Jefferson, threw his support to Jefferson over Burr, whom he considered morally unfit for the presidency. After **36 ballots**, Jefferson was elected president and Burr became vice president.
>
> This crisis led directly to the **[[12th Amendment]]** (1804), which required electors to cast separate ballots for president and vice president.

---

## The Duel with Hamilton (1804)

By 1804, Hamilton had repeatedly undermined Burr's political career — blocking his presidential bid in 1800 and helping defeat his run for governor of New York in 1804. Burr challenged Hamilton to a duel.

> [!info] Weehawken, July 11, 1804
> The two men met at Weehawken, New Jersey. Hamilton reportedly decided to **"throw away his shot"** and deliberately miss. Burr fired directly, hitting Hamilton in the abdomen. Hamilton died the following day.
>
> Burr was charged with **murder** in both New York and New Jersey but fled to avoid prosecution. He returned to Washington to finish his term as vice president — presiding over the Senate while under indictment for murder.

---

## Treason Trial (1807)

After his vice presidency, Burr became involved in a murky conspiracy to either seize control of Spanish-held territory in the Southwest or possibly detach western states from the Union. He was arrested and tried for **treason** in 1807, but was **acquitted** — the government could not produce two witnesses to an overt act of treason as required by the Constitution.

---

## Legacy

Burr's reputation never recovered. He spent years in Europe and died in relative obscurity. Hamilton's death robbed the [[Federalist Party]] of its most dynamic leader and cemented Burr's place in history as a villain of the founding era.
