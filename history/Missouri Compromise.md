# Missouri Compromise

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Date:** 1820
> **Crafted by:** [[Henry Clay]] ("The Great Compromiser")
> **Key Terms:** Missouri admitted as slave state; Maine admitted as free state; 36°30' line established
> **Significance:** Temporarily resolved the crisis over slavery's expansion into new territories
> **Later Repealed by:** [[Kansas-Nebraska Act]] (1854)

---

The **Missouri Compromise** was a landmark 1820 political agreement that temporarily defused the explosive national crisis over the **expansion of slavery** into new territories. Crafted by Senator **[[Henry Clay]]**, it admitted Missouri as a slave state and Maine as a free state, and drew a geographic line across the Louisiana Territory above which slavery would be permanently banned.

---

## Background: The Crisis

When Missouri applied for statehood in 1819, it ignited a national crisis. At the time, the Senate was **perfectly balanced**: 11 free states, 11 slave states — each side had equal representation. Admitting Missouri as a slave state would tip the balance permanently in the South's favor.

> [!info] Tallmadge Amendment
> Congressman James Tallmadge of New York proposed an amendment to Missouri's statehood bill that would have **banned the further introduction of slavery** into Missouri and gradually freed enslaved people already there. It passed the House but was blocked in the Senate. The fight was ferocious and revealed how deeply slavery divided the nation.

Former President [[Thomas Jefferson]] wrote that the crisis struck him "like a fire bell in the night" — a terrifying warning of the catastrophe to come.

---

## The Compromise

**[[Henry Clay]]** engineered a three-part solution:

> [!info] Terms of the Missouri Compromise
> 1. **Missouri** was admitted to the Union as a **slave state**
> 2. **Maine** (previously part of Massachusetts) was admitted as a **free state**, preserving the Senate balance at 12–12
> 3. A geographic line was drawn at **36°30' north latitude** (the southern border of Missouri):
>    - All new states **north** of this line would be **free states**
>    - All new states **south** of this line could be **slave states**

| Territory | Slavery Status |
|---|---|
| Missouri | Slave state (admitted) |
| Maine | Free state (admitted) |
| North of 36°30' | Free territory |
| South of 36°30' | Open to slavery |

---

## Henry Clay — "The Great Compromiser"

> [!info] Henry Clay
> **[[Henry Clay]]** of Kentucky was the architect of the Missouri Compromise and became known as **"The Great Compromiser"** for his ability to broker deals between the slave and free states. He would play a similar role with the **Compromise of 1850**. Clay believed preserving the Union required constant negotiation and was willing to make deals that satisfied no one completely but kept the country together.

---

## Significance

> [!info] Why It Mattered
> - **Bought time** — the compromise kept the peace for over 30 years
> - **Established a dangerous precedent** — drawing geographic lines around slavery treated it as a permanent, negotiable feature of American life rather than a moral problem to be solved
> - **Did not solve the problem** — it only delayed the inevitable confrontation; the question of slavery in new territories continued to fester
> - **Undone by the [[Kansas-Nebraska Act]]** (1854), which repealed the 36°30' line and allowed settlers in new territories to vote on whether to allow slavery — reigniting the sectional crisis that led to the [[Civil War]]
