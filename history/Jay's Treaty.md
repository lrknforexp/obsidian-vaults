# Jay's Treaty

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Formally:** Treaty of Amity, Commerce, and Navigation between His Britannic Majesty and the United States
> **Signed:** November 19, 1794
> **Ratified:** June 24, 1795
> **Negotiated by:** [[John Jay]] (Chief Justice of the U.S.)
> **Significance:** Established the president as chief foreign diplomat; normalized U.S.-British relations

---

**Jay's Treaty** was a diplomatic agreement negotiated between the United States and [[Great Britain]] in 1794, resolving a series of disputes left unresolved since the end of the [[American Revolution]]. Though deeply unpopular with many Americans, it successfully prevented war with Britain and set important precedents for presidential authority in foreign affairs.

---

## Background

By the early 1790s, relations between the U.S. and Britain had deteriorated significantly:

- Britain had **refused to vacate frontier forts** in the American Northwest as required by the [[Treaty of Paris (1783)]]
- British naval vessels were **seizing American merchant ships** trading with France during the French Revolutionary Wars
- Britain was **impressing** American sailors — kidnapping them and forcing them into the Royal Navy
- Britain was **arming and encouraging** Native American tribes along the frontier to resist American expansion

At the same time, war with Britain would have been catastrophic for the young, fragile republic. [[George Washington]] dispatched [[John Jay]], Chief Justice of the Supreme Court, to London to negotiate a settlement.

---

## Terms of the Treaty

> [!info] What Jay Secured
> - Britain agreed to **evacuate its frontier forts** in the Northwest Territory by 1796
> - Britain agreed to pay **compensation** for illegally seized American merchant ships
> - The U.S. gained **limited trading rights** in the British West Indies
> - A joint commission was established to settle remaining debt disputes from the Revolution

> [!info] What Jay Failed to Secure
> - Britain **refused to stop impressing** American sailors
> - Britain **refused to stop seizing** American ships bound for France
> - Britain refused to recognize American **neutral trading rights**
> - No agreement was reached on the **slave trade** or on enslaved people taken by the British during the Revolution

---

## Controversy

Jay's Treaty was one of the most politically divisive events of Washington's presidency:

> [!info] Why It Was Unpopular
> - **[[Democratic-Republicans]]** led by [[Thomas Jefferson]] and [[James Madison]] were outraged — they saw the treaty as a humiliating capitulation to Britain and a betrayal of America's alliance with [[France]]
> - Mobs burned effigies of John Jay; one pamphlet declared: "May it be the last act of his life"
> - Even within Washington's cabinet, [[Edmund Randolph]] opposed it (and was later accused — probably falsely — of being a French agent)
> - The [[Senate]] ratified the treaty by the **bare minimum** of 20–10, exactly the two-thirds needed

Despite the controversy, Washington signed the treaty, viewing it as far preferable to war.

---

## Significance

> [!info] Why Jay's Treaty Mattered
> 1. **Presidential foreign policy authority** — Washington's negotiation and ratification of the treaty, over fierce opposition, established that the **president is the nation's chief foreign diplomat**
> 2. **Prevented war** — kept the U.S. out of the European conflicts that were consuming the continent
> 3. **Deepened partisan divisions** — Democratic-Republicans' fury over the treaty accelerated the formation of organized opposition to the Federalists
> 4. **Triggered the Quasi-War** — France, angered by the treaty's pro-British tilt, began attacking American shipping, leading to the [[Quasi-War]] of 1798–1800
