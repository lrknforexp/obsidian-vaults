# William Clark

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Born:** August 1, 1770 — Caroline County, Virginia
> **Died:** September 1, 1838 — St. Louis, Missouri
> **Role:** U.S. Army officer; co-leader of the [[Lewis and Clark Expedition]]; later Governor of Missouri Territory
> **Known For:** [[Lewis and Clark Expedition]]; detailed maps; [[York]] (enslaved man in his household)

---

**William Clark** was the co-commander of the **[[Lewis and Clark Expedition]]** (1804–1806) and one of the most skilled frontier soldiers and mapmakers of his era. Recruited by his friend **[[Meriwether Lewis]]**, Clark was responsible for the expedition's extraordinarily detailed and accurate maps of the American West — maps that guided explorers and settlers for decades.

## Key Contributions

> [!info] Clark's Role on the Expedition
> - **Cartographer** — Clark's maps of the Missouri and Columbia Rivers and the Rocky Mountains were remarkably accurate given the tools available. He is credited as the primary mapmaker of the Corps of Discovery.
> - **Navigator and wilderness leader** — Clark's frontier experience and calm leadership were essential on the trail
> - **Manager and diplomat** — Clark often handled day-to-day logistics, medical care, and initial diplomatic contacts with Native tribes

## York

Clark brought an enslaved man named **[[York]]** on the expedition — the first African American to cross the North American continent. York's physical presence fascinated many Native peoples who had never seen a Black man; he participated fully in the expedition's work and decisions (including voting on where to build their winter camp). Despite this, Clark refused to free York for years after the expedition's return, finally doing so around 1811.

## After the Expedition

Clark served as Superintendent of Indian Affairs and Governor of the Missouri Territory, where his policies toward Native Americans were often more humane than the era's standard — though still ultimately serving American expansionist interests.
