# Election of 1800

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Date:** 1800
> **Winner:** [[Thomas Jefferson]] (Democratic-Republican)
> **Loser:** [[John Adams]] (Federalist)
> **Also Known As:** "The Revolution of 1800"
> **Significance:** First peaceful transfer of power between opposing parties in American history

---

The **Election of 1800** was one of the most consequential — and chaotic — elections in American history. It marked the **first peaceful transfer of power between rival political parties** in the United States, proving that republican government could survive partisan competition. Jefferson called it a "revolution" — not of violence, but of political principle.

---

## Background

By 1800, the country was bitterly divided:
- The [[Alien and Sedition Acts]] had outraged [[Democratic-Republican Party|Democratic-Republicans]] and much of the public
- The [[Quasi-War]] had strained the nation and split the [[Federalist Party]] between Adams moderates and Hamilton war hawks
- [[Alexander Hamilton]] was actively working to undermine President [[John Adams]]'s reelection within his own party

---

## The Campaign

The election was vicious by any standard. Federalists accused Jefferson of being a dangerous radical and an atheist. Democratic-Republicans portrayed Adams as a would-be king trying to destroy American liberty with the Alien and Sedition Acts.

---

## The Electoral Tie

> [!info] The Crisis
> Under the original [[Electoral College]] rules, each elector cast **two votes for president** — the top vote-getter became president, the second became vice president. Jefferson and his running mate **[[Aaron Burr]]** each received **73 electoral votes** — a tie.
>
> Under the Constitution, the tie was thrown to the **[[House of Representatives]]**, where each **state delegation** cast one vote. The Federalist-controlled House deadlocked for **36 ballots** over several days.
>
> [[Alexander Hamilton]] — despite deeply opposing Jefferson's politics — urged Federalists to vote for Jefferson rather than Burr, whom he considered dangerously unprincipled. On the 36th ballot, Jefferson was elected.

---

## Aftermath: The 12th Amendment

The chaos of 1800 revealed a fundamental flaw in the original Electoral College design. Congress passed the **12th Amendment** (1804), requiring electors to cast **separate ballots** for president and vice president — eliminating the possibility of another tie between running mates.

---

## Significance

> [!info] Why It Mattered
> 1. **Peaceful transfer of power** — proved that a republic could survive the handoff of power from one party to its opponent without violence or military coup
> 2. **End of Federalist dominance** — the Federalist Party never won the presidency again and collapsed as a national force within two decades
> 3. **Democratic-Republican era** — Jefferson and his successors [[James Madison]] and [[James Monroe]] dominated the next 24 years
> 4. **Hamilton and Burr** — Hamilton's role in the election deepened his feud with Burr, which culminated in their fatal 1804 duel
