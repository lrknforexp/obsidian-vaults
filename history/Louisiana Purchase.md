# Louisiana Purchase

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Date:** April 30, 1803
> **Purchase Price:** $15 million (~4 cents per acre)
> **Sold by:** [[Napoleon Bonaparte]] (France)
> **Purchased by:** [[Thomas Jefferson]] (United States)
> **Result:** United States **doubled in size** overnight
> **Territory:** ~828,000 square miles (modern-day 15 states)

---

The **Louisiana Purchase** was the 1803 acquisition of the vast **Louisiana Territory** from France — the largest land purchase in American history, which doubled the size of the United States overnight for approximately $15 million. It opened the continent to American westward expansion and directly led to the **[[Lewis and Clark Expedition]]**.

---

## Background

### The Territory's History
The Louisiana Territory had passed between European powers for decades — France claimed it, ceded it to Spain after the [[French and Indian War]], then Napoleon pressured Spain to return it to France in 1800.

### Napoleon's Change of Plans

> [!info] Why Napoleon Sold
> Napoleon Bonaparte had grand ambitions for a French empire in the Americas, centered on the Caribbean colony of **Saint-Domingue** (modern Haiti). However, the **Haitian Revolution** — a massive slave rebellion led by Toussaint Louverture — destroyed French forces and shattered Napoleon's American dreams.
>
> With war resuming in Europe and his American empire in ruins, Napoleon needed money and had no use for Louisiana. He offered the entire territory to the United States for $15 million.

---

## Jefferson's Dilemma

> [!info] A Strict Constructionist's Problem
> [[Thomas Jefferson]] was a committed **strict constructionist** — he believed the federal government could only do what the Constitution **explicitly** authorized. The Constitution said nothing about the president purchasing foreign territory.
>
> Yet the opportunity was too enormous to pass up. Jefferson ultimately set aside his principles and completed the purchase, privately acknowledging he had "stretched the Constitution." He justified it as serving the best interests of the nation.

This inconsistency drew sharp criticism from [[Federalist Party|Federalists]], who accused Jefferson of rank hypocrisy.

---

## The Territory

The Louisiana Purchase encompassed:
- All or part of 15 modern U.S. states: Louisiana, Arkansas, Missouri, Iowa, Minnesota, North Dakota, South Dakota, Nebraska, Kansas, Oklahoma, Colorado, Wyoming, Montana, and portions of others
- Control of the **[[Mississippi River]]** and the crucial port of **New Orleans**
- Access to the entire western interior of the continent

---

## Lewis and Clark Expedition

Jefferson immediately organized the **[[Lewis and Clark Expedition]]** (1804–1806) to explore and map the new territory, sending **[[Meriwether Lewis]]** and **[[William Clark]]** west. **[[Sacagawea]]**, a [[Shoshone]] woman, served as guide and translator.

---

## Significance

> [!info] Why It Mattered
> - Set the United States on the path to becoming a **continental nation**
> - Secured American control of the Mississippi River and New Orleans — vital for western trade
> - Raised the explosive question of whether new states carved from the territory would be **slave or free** — a debate that led directly to the [[Missouri Compromise]] and ultimately the [[Civil War]]
> - Demonstrated the enormous gap between Jefferson's stated political philosophy and his actions in office
