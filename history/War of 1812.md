# War of 1812

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Dates:** June 18, 1812 – February 18, 1815
> **Combatants:** United States vs. Great Britain (and allied Native American tribes)
> **U.S. President:** [[James Madison]]
> **Result:** Treaty of Ghent (December 1814); status quo ante bellum — essentially a draw
> **Key Battles:** Battle of New Orleans, Burning of Washington D.C., Battle of Lake Erie
> **Significance:** Produced American nationalism; end of British interference with U.S. trade; weakened Native American resistance in the East

---

The **War of 1812** was a military conflict between the United States and Great Britain fought from 1812 to 1815. Declared by Congress at the urging of President **[[James Madison]]**, it was fueled by British impressment of American sailors, interference with American trade during the Napoleonic Wars, and British support for Native American resistance to American westward expansion. The war ended in a draw but produced a surge of American nationalism and effectively ended British interference with American commerce.

---

## Causes

### Impressment

Britain was fighting a life-or-death war with Napoleon's France and desperately needed sailors. The Royal Navy stopped American merchant ships on the high seas and **impressed** (forcibly conscripted) sailors it claimed were British subjects — often taking American citizens. This was a direct violation of American sovereignty and deeply enraged the public.

### Trade Interference

Both Britain and France interfered with American trade during the Napoleonic Wars:
- Britain's **Orders in Council** restricted neutral trade with France
- Jefferson's **[[Embargo Act]]** (1807) and later the Non-Intercourse Act tried to use economic pressure rather than war — both failed
- By 1812, trade disruption had severely damaged the American economy

### "War Hawks" and Expansion

Young congressmen from the South and West — known as **"War Hawks"** (including **Henry Clay** and John C. Calhoun) — pushed for war, motivated partly by desire to:
- Seize **Canada** from Britain
- Eliminate British support for Native American resistance to expansion
- Defend American honor

---

## Key Events

### Burning of Washington, D.C. (1814)

> [!info] The Capital Burns
> In August 1814, British forces marched into **Washington, D.C.** and burned the White House, the Capitol, and other government buildings — the only time since the Revolution that a foreign power has occupied and damaged the U.S. capital. First Lady **Dolley Madison** famously saved a portrait of George Washington before fleeing.

### Battle of New Orleans (January 8, 1815)

> [!info] Jackson's Triumph
> General **[[Andrew Jackson]]** led American forces (a mix of regulars, militia, pirates, and free Black soldiers) to a stunning victory over a British force trying to seize New Orleans. The battle was fought **two weeks after** the peace treaty had already been signed (before news reached America). Nonetheless, it made Jackson a national hero and launched his political career.

### Battle of Lake Erie (September 1813)

American naval forces under Oliver Hazard Perry defeated a British fleet on Lake Erie — securing control of the Great Lakes and enabling future American offensives into Canada.

---

## Treaty of Ghent (December 1814)

The war ended with the **Treaty of Ghent**, which:
- Restored all territories to their pre-war status (**status quo ante bellum**)
- Said nothing about impressment or neutral trading rights (the original causes of the war)
- Was essentially a draw — both sides were exhausted

---

## Consequences

> [!info] What the War Actually Accomplished
> Despite ending in a draw, the War of 1812 had significant consequences:
>
> - **American nationalism surged** — the young republic had fought Britain to a standstill, and the Battle of New Orleans felt like a triumph. Americans called it a "Second War of Independence"
> - **British interference ended** — after the war, Britain never again seriously challenged American sovereignty or trade
> - **Native American resistance collapsed** — British support for Native tribes east of the Mississippi ended; tribes lost their most powerful ally and faced American expansion without protection
> - **Andrew Jackson becomes a hero** — the Battle of New Orleans made Jackson the most famous man in America and eventually the president
> - **The Federalist Party dies** — [[Federalist Party|Federalists]] had opposed the war and even discussed secession at the [[Hartford Convention]] (1814–1815); after the "victory" at New Orleans, they were permanently discredited as unpatriotic and collapsed as a party
