# Samuel F.B. Morse

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Born:** April 27, 1791 — Charlestown, Massachusetts
> **Died:** April 2, 1872 — New York City
> **Known For:** Inventor of the **telegraph** and **Morse Code**
> **First Telegraph Message:** "What hath God wrought" (May 24, 1844, Baltimore to Washington D.C.)

---

**Samuel Finley Breese Morse** was an American painter and inventor who developed the **electric telegraph** and the **Morse Code** system of dots and dashes — inventions that revolutionized communication and made him one of the most consequential figures of the **[[Market Revolution]]**.

---

## The Telegraph

> [!info] How It Worked
> The telegraph transmitted messages as electrical signals along wires. Morse's key innovation was a practical **coding system** — **Morse Code** — that converted letters and numbers into patterns of short and long electrical pulses (dots and dashes) that could be transmitted, received, and decoded at the other end.
>
> Morse's first long-distance telegraph line ran from **Washington, D.C. to Baltimore** (40 miles). On May 24, 1844, Morse transmitted the famous first message: **"What hath God wrought"** (from the Bible, Numbers 23:23).

---

## Impact on American Society

> [!info] Why the Telegraph Mattered
> Before the telegraph, information moved **only as fast as a person, horse, or ship** could carry it. A message from Washington to New York took hours or days. The telegraph collapsed time:
>
> - Messages that once took days now arrived in **minutes**
> - Newspapers could report news from across the country on the **same day** it happened
> - Businesses could coordinate prices, orders, and shipments in **real time**
> - Military commanders could coordinate troop movements over vast distances
> - The telegraph effectively **shrank the nation**, making distant regions feel connected

By 1861, telegraph wires spanned the continent from the Atlantic to the Pacific — completing the transcontinental telegraph. The **Pony Express** (started 1860) was immediately made obsolete.

---

## Morse the Painter

Before he became an inventor, Morse was a respected portrait painter — he founded the **National Academy of Design** in New York. He turned to telegraphy after his wife died while he was away painting in Washington, D.C., and he didn't learn of her death until she was already buried. The delay of information across distance haunted him and drove his obsession with faster communication.
