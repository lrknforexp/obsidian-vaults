# Democratic-Republican Party

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Also Known As:** Jeffersonian Republicans, Jeffersonian Democrats, Republicans (historically)
> **Active:** 1792–1825
> **Leaders:** [[Thomas Jefferson]], [[James Madison]], [[James Monroe]]
> **Ideology:** States' rights, strict constitutional interpretation, agrarian economy, pro-French foreign policy
> **Opposition:** [[Federalist Party]]
> **Successor parties:** [[Democratic Party]], [[Whig Party]]

---

The **Democratic-Republican Party** was the political party founded by [[Thomas Jefferson]] and [[James Madison]] in the early 1790s to oppose [[Alexander Hamilton]]'s [[Federalist Party]]. It was the dominant force in American politics from 1800 to the 1820s, winning every presidential election during the so-called **"Era of Good Feelings."** It eventually split into the modern [[Democratic Party]] and the [[Whig Party]] in the 1820s–1830s.

---

## Origins

The party emerged from opposition to Hamilton's economic program and the Federalist vision of a powerful central government. Jefferson and Madison believed Hamilton's policies:
- **Exceeded constitutional authority** — the Constitution gave Congress no explicit power to charter a bank
- **Favored wealthy elites** over ordinary farmers and workers
- **Dangerously concentrated power** at the federal level, threatening to recreate the kind of tyranny Americans had just fought a revolution to escape

> [!info] The Split
> The party originally formed as a **congressional opposition bloc** in 1792. Jefferson and Madison co-authored anonymous newspaper articles attacking Hamilton's program and began organizing political networks across the country — the first systematic effort to build a national political party in American history.

---

## Core Beliefs

| Belief | Details |
|---|---|
| **States' rights** | States should retain most political power; the federal government should be limited |
| **Strict constitutional interpretation** | The government can only do what the Constitution **explicitly** authorizes |
| **Agrarian ideal** | Farmers and rural citizens were the backbone of republican virtue; commerce and manufacturing bred corruption |
| **Pro-French foreign policy** | France was America's revolutionary ally; [[Jay's Treaty]]'s pro-British tilt was a betrayal |
| **Anti-[[National Bank]]** | A national bank was unconstitutional and served only wealthy creditors |
| **Small military** | A large standing army was a tool of tyranny |

---

## Key Leaders

**[[Thomas Jefferson]]** — primary founder; 3rd President (1801–1809); author of the [[Declaration of Independence]]; purchased the [[Louisiana Territory]].

**[[James Madison]]** — co-founder; 4th President (1809–1817); "Father of the Constitution"; led the U.S. through the [[War of 1812]].

**[[James Monroe]]** — 5th President (1817–1825); presided over the "Era of Good Feelings"; issued the [[Monroe Doctrine]].

---

## The Virginia and Kentucky Resolutions (1798–1799)

One of the party's most consequential acts was Jefferson and Madison's response to the [[Alien and Sedition Acts]]:

> [!info] Virginia and Kentucky Resolutions
> Jefferson (secretly) wrote the **[[Kentucky Resolutions]]** and Madison wrote the **[[Virginia Resolutions]]**, arguing that:
> - The Constitution was a **compact among the states**
> - States therefore had the right to **judge the constitutionality** of federal laws
> - States could **nullify** (declare void within their borders) unconstitutional federal acts
>
> This **nullification doctrine** was immediately controversial and would resurface explosively before the [[Civil War]], when Southern states used similar logic to justify secession.

---

## Rise to Power: The Election of 1800

The **Election of 1800** was a watershed in American history:

> [!info] The "Revolution of 1800"
> Jefferson defeated incumbent President [[John Adams]] in a bitterly contested election. Jefferson called it a "revolution" because it represented the **first peaceful transfer of power** between rival political parties in American history — proof that republican government could work.
>
> The election was initially thrown to the [[House of Representatives]] because Jefferson and his running mate [[Aaron Burr]] tied in the [[Electoral College]] (both received 73 votes). Hamilton ultimately threw his support behind Jefferson over Burr, whom he despised — a decision that contributed to Burr eventually challenging Hamilton to the duel that killed him in 1804.

---

## In Power: Jefferson's Presidency

Despite his small-government rhetoric, Jefferson made several decisions as president that contradicted strict constitutional interpretation:

> [!info] Jefferson's Contradictions
> - **[[Louisiana Purchase]] (1803)** — The Constitution said nothing about buying foreign territory, yet Jefferson completed the $15 million purchase that doubled the nation's size
> - **[[Embargo Act]] (1807)** — Jefferson imposed a total embargo on American foreign trade — an enormous exercise of federal power — to pressure Britain and France during the Napoleonic Wars. It devastated the American economy and was deeply unpopular.

---

## Decline

By the 1820s, the Democratic-Republicans had become the **only** national party — the Federalists had collapsed — but internal divisions made unity impossible. The party split in the Election of 1824 into factions supporting [[John Quincy Adams]] and [[Andrew Jackson]], eventually reorganizing as the [[Democratic Party]] (Jackson's faction) and the [[Whig Party]] (opposition).
