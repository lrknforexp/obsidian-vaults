# Benjamin Franklin

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Born:** January 17, 1706 — Boston, Massachusetts
> **Died:** April 17, 1790 — Philadelphia, Pennsylvania
> **Roles:** Printer, author, scientist, inventor, diplomat, Founding Father
> **Party:** None (pre-party era)
> **Known For:** Electricity experiments, Poor Richard's Almanack, diplomacy in France, [[Declaration of Independence]], [[Constitutional Convention]]

---

**Benjamin Franklin** was the most famous American of the 18th century — a self-made man who rose from poverty to become a printer, bestselling author, groundbreaking scientist, and indispensable diplomat. He helped draft the [[Declaration of Independence]], secured the French alliance that won the [[American Revolution]], and was the oldest delegate at the [[Constitutional Convention]]. He is often called the "First American."

---

## Early Life

Franklin was born the 15th of 17 children in a Boston soap-maker's family. He had only two years of formal schooling. At age 12 he was apprenticed to his older brother, a printer — a trade that shaped his entire career. At 17 he ran away to Philadelphia, where he eventually built a printing empire that made him wealthy.

---

## Scientist and Inventor

> [!info] Electricity
> Franklin's most famous scientific work was his investigation of **electricity**. His kite experiment (1752) — flying a kite with a metal key in a thunderstorm — demonstrated that lightning was electrical in nature. This led directly to his invention of the **lightning rod**, which saved countless buildings and ships from lightning strikes. He coined terms like "positive" and "negative" charge, "battery," and "conductor" that are still used today.

Franklin also invented:
- **Bifocal glasses**
- The **Franklin stove** (more efficient wood-burning stove)
- The **flexible urinary catheter**
- **Swim fins** (as a child)

---

## Diplomat in France

Franklin's most consequential political role was as American diplomatic representative to France during the [[American Revolution]].

> [!info] Securing the French Alliance
> Franklin arrived in Paris in 1778 and became an instant celebrity — the wise, witty, plain-dressed American philosopher was exactly what the French Enlightenment imagination had pictured. He skillfully parlayed his fame into political results, securing the **Franco-American Alliance** (1778) — France's entry into the war against Britain. French troops, money, and naval power were essential to American victory, particularly at **[[Battle of Yorktown|Yorktown]]**.

Franklin also helped negotiate the **[[Treaty of Paris (1783)]]**, which formally ended the Revolution on favorable terms for the United States.

---

## The Constitutional Convention

At 81, Franklin was the oldest delegate at the **[[Constitutional Convention]]** (1787) — carried in on a sedan chair because of gout. Though his direct contributions to the document were limited, his presence lent enormous prestige to the proceedings, and his closing speech urging all delegates to sign the Constitution — even those with reservations — helped secure unanimous ratification by the Convention.

---

## Anti-Slavery Work

In the last years of his life, Franklin became president of the **Pennsylvania Abolition Society** and submitted one of the first anti-slavery petitions to Congress in 1790 — just weeks before his death.

---

## Legacy

Franklin appears on the **U.S. $100 bill** — despite never being president. He is a foundational figure of American identity: the self-made man, the practical genius, the witty sage. His *Autobiography* is one of the first and most influential works of American literature.
