# Triangular Trade

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Period:** 1600s–early 1800s
> **Regions Involved:** Europe, West Africa, the Americas
> **Key Goods:** Manufactured goods, enslaved people, raw materials
> **Core Evil:** The [[Middle Passage]] — the forced transport of enslaved Africans

---

The **Triangular Trade** was the three-legged transatlantic commercial system that connected **Europe**, **West Africa**, and the **Americas** in a cycle of trade from roughly the 1600s through the early 1800s. It was the commercial backbone of the Atlantic slave trade and the engine of European colonial prosperity.

---

## The Three Legs

> [!info] Leg 1: Europe → Africa
> European ships (British, Portuguese, Dutch, French, Spanish) sailed to West Africa carrying **manufactured goods**: guns, gunpowder, iron, textiles, rum, and other goods. These were traded to African rulers and merchants in exchange for **enslaved people** — often prisoners of war, kidnapped individuals, or people sold by rival African kingdoms.

> [!info] Leg 2: Africa → Americas (The Middle Passage)
> Enslaved Africans were transported across the Atlantic in horrific conditions — the **[[Middle Passage]]**. Ships were packed as tightly as possible to maximize profit. Roughly **1 in 7** enslaved people died during the crossing from disease, violence, and starvation. Those who survived were sold at auction in American ports.
>
> An estimated **12 million** Africans were transported across the Atlantic; approximately 10.7 million survived to arrive in the Americas.

> [!info] Leg 3: Americas → Europe
> Ships returned to Europe loaded with **raw materials** produced by enslaved labor in the Americas: sugar, tobacco, cotton, rice, indigo, and other crops. These goods were sold in European markets at enormous profit, funding the next voyage — and the next purchase of enslaved people.

---

## Economic Impact

The Triangular Trade was enormously profitable for European merchants, ship owners, colonial planters, and the nations that taxed them:
- The sugar and tobacco industries of the Caribbean and American South were built entirely on enslaved labor
- British industrial wealth — including the early [[Industrial Revolution]] — was funded in significant part by profits from slave-produced goods
- It enriched port cities like Bristol, Liverpool, Nantes, and Lisbon

---

## Human Cost

> [!info] The Scale of Suffering
> The Triangular Trade was the largest forced migration in human history:
> - Approximately **12–12.5 million** Africans were enslaved and transported
> - **1.5–2 million** died during the Middle Passage
> - Millions more died in Africa during raids and the overland march to slave ports
> - The African diaspora — the dispersal of African peoples across the Americas — is the direct result of the trade
> - Its legacy shapes racial inequality in the Americas to this day

---

## End of the Slave Trade

The **British slave trade** was abolished in 1807; the **American slave trade** was legally ended in 1808 (as permitted by the Constitution). However, smuggling of enslaved people continued, and the institution of slavery itself persisted in the American South until the **Civil War** (1861–1865).
