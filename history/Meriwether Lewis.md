# Meriwether Lewis

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Born:** August 18, 1774 — Albemarle County, Virginia
> **Died:** October 11, 1809 — Natchez Trace, Tennessee (likely suicide)
> **Role:** U.S. Army captain; personal secretary to [[Thomas Jefferson]]; co-leader of the [[Lewis and Clark Expedition]]

---

**Meriwether Lewis** was the primary leader of the **[[Lewis and Clark Expedition]]** (1804–1806) — the first American overland expedition to the Pacific Coast. Appointed by President [[Thomas Jefferson]] (his personal secretary and close friend), Lewis was a skilled naturalist, soldier, and frontier woodsman who led the Corps of Discovery more than 7,000 miles round-trip across the continent.

## Key Facts

- Jefferson selected Lewis specifically because of his scientific curiosity, military experience, and physical toughness
- Before departure, Lewis studied botany, medicine, astronomy, and cartography in Philadelphia
- He recruited his friend **[[William Clark]]** as co-commander
- The expedition documented approximately **178 plant species** and **122 animal species** previously unknown to science
- Lewis suffered from depression; he died under mysterious circumstances (likely suicide) in 1809 at age 35, just three years after the expedition's triumph
