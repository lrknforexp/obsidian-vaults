# Thomas Jefferson

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Born:** April 13, 1743 — Shadwell, Virginia
> **Died:** July 4, 1826 — Monticello, Virginia
> **Presidency:** 1801–1809 (3rd President of the United States)
> **Party:** [[Democratic-Republican Party]]
> **Vice Presidents:** [[Aaron Burr]] (1st term), [[George Clinton]] (2nd term)
> **Known For:** [[Declaration of Independence]], [[Louisiana Purchase]], founding [[Democratic-Republican Party]]

---

**Thomas Jefferson** was the principal author of the **[[Declaration of Independence]]**, the 3rd President of the United States, and the co-founder (with [[James Madison]]) of the [[Democratic-Republican Party]]. His presidency was defined by the massive [[Louisiana Purchase]], the [[Lewis and Clark Expedition]], and a series of contradictions between his stated political philosophy and his actions in office. He enslaved over 600 people over the course of his lifetime, including six who served in the White House.

---

## Early Life and Education

Jefferson was born into a wealthy Virginia planter family. He graduated from the College of William & Mary in 1762 and trained as a lawyer. He became deeply interested in Enlightenment philosophy — particularly the ideas of [[John Locke]] about natural rights, government by consent, and the right of revolution — ideas that directly shaped the Declaration of Independence.

---

## The Declaration of Independence (1776)

> [!info] Declaration of Independence
> Jefferson was selected by the [[Second Continental Congress]] to draft a declaration of independence from Britain. The document he produced, with minor revisions by [[Benjamin Franklin]] and [[John Adams]], declared:
> - All men are created equal and endowed with unalienable rights: **life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness**
> - Governments derive their just powers from the consent of the governed
> - When a government becomes destructive of these ends, the people have the right to alter or abolish it
>
> A passage condemning King George III for the slave trade was removed at the insistence of Southern delegates.

---

## Founding the Democratic-Republican Party

In the 1790s, Jefferson and [[James Madison]] organized political opposition to [[Alexander Hamilton]]'s [[Federalist Party]], founding what became the **[[Democratic-Republican Party]]**. They believed Hamilton's economic program was unconstitutional, corrupt, and dangerous to republican liberty.

Jefferson championed:
- **States' rights** over federal power
- **Strict interpretation** of the Constitution
- An **agrarian republic** of independent farmers as the foundation of democracy
- **Pro-French** foreign policy (in contrast to Federalist pro-British leanings)

---

## Election of 1800

> [!info] The "Revolution of 1800"
> Jefferson defeated incumbent [[John Adams]] in the bitterly contested **[[Election of 1800]]** — the first peaceful transfer of power between opposing parties in American history. The election was thrown to the [[House of Representatives]] after Jefferson and running mate [[Aaron Burr]] tied in the [[Electoral College]] with 73 votes each. [[Alexander Hamilton]] threw his support to Jefferson, helping elect his ideological opponent over his personal enemy Burr.

---

## Presidency (1801–1809)

### The Louisiana Purchase (1803)

> [!info] Louisiana Purchase
> Napoleon Bonaparte offered to sell the entire [[Louisiana Territory]] for $15 million. Jefferson faced a profound dilemma: his strict constructionist principles held that the Constitution gave the president no power to purchase foreign territory. Yet the opportunity to double the nation's size was too great to pass up.
>
> Jefferson ultimately set aside his principles, completing the purchase and acknowledging privately that he had stretched the Constitution. See: **[[Louisiana Purchase]]**

### Lewis and Clark Expedition

Jefferson organized the **[[Lewis and Clark Expedition]]** (1804–1806), sending [[Meriwether Lewis]] and [[William Clark]] to explore and map the newly acquired western territories. [[Sacagawea]] served as a guide and translator.

### The Embargo Act (1807)

> [!info] The Embargo Act
> In response to British and French seizure of American ships during the Napoleonic Wars, Jefferson imposed a total **[[Embargo Act|embargo]]** on American foreign trade — forbidding U.S. ships from trading with any foreign nation. This was one of the most sweeping exercises of federal economic power in early American history, directly contradicting Jefferson's small-government ideology. The embargo devastated the American economy, particularly in New England, and was repealed in 1809.

---

## Contradictions: Slavery and Liberty

> [!info] Jefferson and Slavery
> Jefferson enslaved approximately **600 people** over the course of his life. He wrote eloquently about liberty and equality while personally benefiting from and perpetuating chattel slavery. He had a long-term relationship with enslaved woman **[[Sally Hemings]]**, with whom he fathered at least six children (DNA evidence confirmed this in 1998).
>
> He never freed his enslaved people during his lifetime (unlike [[George Washington]], who freed his upon death). He freed only five people in his will — all believed to be his children with Hemings.

---

## Death

Jefferson died on **July 4, 1826** — the 50th anniversary of the Declaration of Independence — the same day as his old friend and rival [[John Adams]].

---

## Legacy

- Author of the Declaration of Independence — foundational document of American liberty
- The [[Louisiana Purchase]] set the nation on the path to continental expansion
- His democratic philosophy shaped American political culture for generations
- His contradictions on slavery remain central to debates about the founders' legacy
