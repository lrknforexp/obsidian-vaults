# Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Signed:** February 2, 1848
> **Parties:** United States and Mexico
> **Ratified by U.S. Senate:** March 10, 1848
> **Ended:** [[Mexican-American War]] (1846–1848)
> **Result:** Mexico ceded approximately 525,000 square miles to the United States; U.S. paid Mexico $15 million

---

The **Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo** was the peace agreement that ended the **[[Mexican-American War]]**, signed on February 2, 1848, in the Mexico City suburb of Guadalupe Hidalgo. It was one of the most consequential territorial agreements in American history — the United States acquired what is now California, Nevada, Utah, most of Arizona, and parts of New Mexico, Colorado, and Wyoming, roughly **doubling** the pre-war size of the country. The acquisition immediately reignited the explosive debate over slavery's expansion into new territories and set the stage for the political crises of the 1850s that led to the Civil War.

---

## Background

The Mexican-American War began in 1846 under President **James K. Polk**, who sent U.S. troops into disputed territory along the Texas-Mexico border, provoking a Mexican military response that Polk used as a pretext for war. American forces invaded Mexico on multiple fronts, ultimately capturing **Mexico City** in September 1847. With their capital occupied, the Mexican government agreed to negotiate.

---

## Terms of the Treaty

> [!info] What Mexico Surrendered — The Mexican Cession
> Mexico agreed to cede to the United States:
> - **California** — The entire territory, including San Francisco Bay and the Pacific coast
> - **New Mexico** — A vast territory covering present-day New Mexico, Arizona, Nevada, Utah, and parts of Colorado and Wyoming
> - **Texas** — Mexico formally recognized the Rio Grande as the southern border of Texas (accepting the U.S. annexation it had never recognized)
>
> In total, Mexico gave up approximately **55% of its pre-war national territory**.

> [!info] What the U.S. Paid and Promised
> - The United States paid Mexico **$15 million** — the same price Jefferson had paid for the entire [[Louisiana Purchase]] 45 years earlier
> - The U.S. assumed approximately $3.25 million in claims that American citizens had against the Mexican government
> - Mexico's residents in the ceded territories were guaranteed U.S. citizenship within one year if they chose to stay, and their property and cultural rights were to be protected
> - The treaty guaranteed the property rights of Mexican land-grant holders — promises that were widely violated in the following decades as American settlers and lawyers found ways to strip Mexican Americans of their lands

---

## Timing and Gold

Just nine days before the treaty was signed, **gold was discovered at Sutter's Mill** in California (January 24, 1848). The treaty negotiators did not yet know about the discovery. Within a year, the **California Gold Rush** had begun, sending hundreds of thousands of migrants to the newly acquired territory and making California's admission to the Union an urgent political question.

---

## The Slavery Question and the Wilmot Proviso

Even before the war ended, the question of slavery in any acquired territory had become a political firestorm.

> [!info] The Wilmot Proviso
> In 1846, Congressman David Wilmot of Pennsylvania introduced a measure — the **Wilmot Proviso** — that would have banned slavery from any territory acquired from Mexico. It passed the House (dominated by the free North) but failed in the Senate (where slave states had equal representation). The debate over the Proviso revealed a level of sectional bitterness that shocked many Americans and previewed the catastrophic conflicts to come.
>
> The Mexican Cession made the slavery expansion question impossible to avoid: Would the vast new territories be slave or free? This question consumed American politics for the next twelve years and was ultimately resolved only by Civil War.

---

## The Compromise of 1850

The immediate political result of the Mexican Cession was a national crisis over California's application for statehood as a **free state**. The crisis was temporarily resolved by the **[[Compromise of 1850]]**, negotiated by [[Henry Clay]] and shepherded through Congress by Senator Stephen Douglas. See: **[[Compromise of 1850]]**

---

## Legacy

The Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo:
- Completed the territorial expansion of the continental United States from the Atlantic to the Pacific
- Immediately made the slavery question existential for the union — the new territories had to be categorized as slave or free
- Established a large Mexican American population in the Southwest whose land rights were systematically violated in subsequent decades
- Set the stage for the political crises of the 1850s and ultimately the Civil War
