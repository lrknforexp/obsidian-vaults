# American Revolution

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Dates:** 1775–1783
> **Combatants:** Thirteen American Colonies vs. Great Britain
> **Result:** American independence; [[Treaty of Paris (1783)]]
> **Key Leaders:** [[George Washington]], [[Benjamin Franklin]], [[Thomas Jefferson]], [[John Adams]]
> **Key Document:** [[Declaration of Independence]] (1776)

---

The **American Revolution** was the political and military struggle through which the **thirteen American colonies** broke free from British rule and established the **United States of America**. It produced the [[Declaration of Independence]] (1776), the [[Continental Army]] under [[George Washington]], a critical alliance with France, and ultimately American independence confirmed by the [[Treaty of Paris (1783)]].

---

## Causes

### Salutary Neglect Ends
For decades, Britain had practiced **[[Salutary Neglect]]** — loosely enforcing trade laws and leaving colonies largely self-governing. After the costly [[French and Indian War]] (1754–1763), Britain changed course, tightening control and imposing new taxes to pay its war debts.

### Taxation Without Representation
A series of acts inflamed colonial opinion:

| Act | Year | Grievance |
|---|---|---|
| [[Stamp Act]] | 1765 | First direct tax on colonists; taxed legal documents and printed materials |
| [[Townshend Acts]] | 1767 | Taxes on imported goods like glass, tea, and paper |
| Tea Act / Boston Tea Party | 1773 | Monopoly given to East India Company; colonists dumped tea in Boston Harbor |
| Intolerable Acts | 1774 | British punishment closing Boston Harbor and restricting colonial self-government |

> [!info] "No Taxation Without Representation"
> Colonists argued that since they had no elected representatives in the British Parliament, Parliament had no right to tax them. This principle — **"no taxation without representation"** — became the central slogan of colonial resistance.

---

## Key Events

### Boston Massacre (1770)
British soldiers fired into a crowd of colonists, killing five. [[John Adams]] controversially defended the soldiers at trial, securing acquittals for most. The event became powerful anti-British propaganda. See: **[[Boston Massacre]]**

### First and Second Continental Congresses
Colonial leaders met in **[[First Continental Congress|Philadelphia]]** in 1774 and 1775. The [[Second Continental Congress]] effectively became the governing body of the revolution, appointing [[George Washington]] as Commander-in-Chief of the [[Continental Army]].

### Declaration of Independence (1776)
[[Thomas Jefferson]] drafted, and Congress adopted, the **[[Declaration of Independence]]** on July 4, 1776 — formally announcing the colonies' separation from Britain and articulating the philosophical foundations of American democracy.

### Valley Forge (1777–1778)
Washington's army endured a brutal winter at **[[Valley Forge]]** in Pennsylvania. The army emerged more disciplined and better trained, largely due to the efforts of Prussian drillmaster Friedrich von Steuben.

### Battle of Yorktown (1781)
The decisive final engagement. Washington, in alliance with French forces under Rochambeau and the French fleet, trapped British General **[[Cornwallis]]** at Yorktown, Virginia. Cornwallis surrendered on October 19, 1781, effectively ending the war. See: **[[Battle of Yorktown]]**

---

## Treaty of Paris (1783)

> [!info] Peace Terms
> The **[[Treaty of Paris (1783)]]** formally ended the Revolution. Britain recognized American independence and ceded all territory east of the Mississippi River to the United States — a massive territorial gain that set the stage for westward expansion.

---

## Legacy

- Created the United States of America and established the world's first modern democratic republic
- The [[Declaration of Independence]] became a foundational document for democratic movements worldwide
- The Revolution's ideals of liberty and equality existed in deep tension with the institution of slavery — a contradiction that would define American history for the next century
- The experience of self-governance during the revolution shaped the founders' thinking about the [[Articles of Confederation]] and [[U.S. Constitution]]
