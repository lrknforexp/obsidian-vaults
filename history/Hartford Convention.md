# Hartford Convention

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Dates:** December 15, 1814 – January 5, 1815
> **Location:** Hartford, Connecticut
> **Participants:** New England Federalists (delegates from Massachusetts, Connecticut, Rhode Island, and parts of Vermont and New Hampshire)
> **Purpose:** Protest the [[War of 1812]] and propose constitutional amendments to limit Southern and Western power
> **Result:** Political disaster for the [[Federalist Party]]; accelerated the party's collapse

---

The **Hartford Convention** was a secret meeting of New England [[Federalist Party|Federalist]] delegates held in Hartford, Connecticut from December 1814 to January 1815, during the final months of the **[[War of 1812]]**. The delegates met to protest the war, which had devastated New England's maritime economy, and to propose constitutional amendments that would limit the growing political power of the South and West. The convention's timing was catastrophic — it concluded just as news arrived of both the **Treaty of Ghent** (ending the war) and **[[Andrew Jackson]]'s** decisive victory at the **Battle of New Orleans**. The convention's participants were permanently branded as disloyal traitors and the [[Federalist Party]] never recovered.

---

## Background: New England's War Grievances

New England had opposed the War of 1812 from the beginning. The region's economy depended on maritime trade with Britain, which the war had effectively destroyed:

- The [[Embargo Act]] of 1807 and subsequent trade restrictions had already devastated New England commerce
- The British naval blockade — which pointedly left New England ports **unblockaded** early in the war to encourage New England's disaffection — still disrupted trade generally
- New England Federalists viewed the war as a Democratic-Republican project designed to conquer Canada for the benefit of Southern and Western expansion, at New England's expense
- The federal government had refused to use the New England state militias the way New England governors wanted, and had provided inadequate federal troops to defend the region against British raids

> [!info] Secessionist Sentiment
> By 1814, with the war going badly and British forces having burned Washington, D.C., some New England extremists were openly discussing **secession from the Union**. The Hartford Convention was organized partly to provide a moderate alternative to the secessionist fringe — but its very existence associated Federalism with disloyalty.

---

## What the Convention Proposed

The convention's actual resolutions were far more moderate than the worst fears of its critics:

> [!info] The Convention's Proposals
> **Constitutional Amendments:**
> - Eliminate the [[Three-Fifths Compromise]] (which gave Southern states extra congressional representation based on their enslaved population)
> - Require a **two-thirds supermajority** of Congress to admit new states, declare war, or restrict foreign trade (limiting the South and West's ability to drag New England into wars and embargoes)
> - Limit presidents to **one term**
> - Prohibit the election of a president from the same state as his predecessor (targeting the "Virginia Dynasty" — Washington, Jefferson, Madison, and soon Monroe, all Virginians)
>
> **Immediate Demands:**
> If the federal government did not address New England's grievances, the convention hinted that states might take matters into their own hands — language that critics interpreted as a threat of nullification or secession.

---

## The Disastrous Timing

The convention's delegates arrived in Washington in early 1815 to present their demands, only to find:

1. **The Treaty of Ghent** had been signed on December 24, 1814, ending the war — making the convention's complaints immediately obsolete
2. **Andrew Jackson's victory at the Battle of New Orleans** (January 8, 1815) had made the war seem like a glorious American triumph — instantly transforming public perception

> [!info] From Crisis to Triumph Overnight
> Within days of the convention's delegates arriving in Washington, the United States went from a nation exhausted by a failing war to a nation celebrating a great victory. The Federalists who had opposed the war — and had met secretly to discuss limiting federal power — now looked like traitors who had tried to undermine a successful war effort.
>
> The convention delegates were mocked and humiliated. The Hartford Convention became synonymous with disloyalty and defeatism.

---

## Legacy: Death of the Federalist Party

The Hartford Convention was the final blow to the already weakened [[Federalist Party]]:

- The party had lost the presidency in 1800 and never won it back
- [[Alexander Hamilton]]'s death in 1804 had removed its most dynamic leader
- Opposition to the War of 1812 had cost it support outside New England
- The Hartford Convention — coming just before an apparently victorious peace — permanently branded Federalism as disloyal

The Federalist Party ran its last presidential candidate in 1816 and effectively ceased to exist as a national force by 1820. The period that followed — James Monroe's presidency, with virtually no organized opposition — became known as the **"Era of Good Feelings."**

---

## Significance for States' Rights

The Hartford Convention is historically significant not just for ending the Federalist Party but for demonstrating that **both parties** were capable of invoking states' rights and threatening nullification when their interests were threatened. The [[Democratic-Republican Party|Democratic-Republicans]] had done it with the [[Virginia and Kentucky Resolutions]] in 1798–1799; the Federalists did it at Hartford in 1814–1815. The lesson was that states' rights arguments were tools of political opposition, not principled constitutional positions — whoever was out of power tended to invoke them.
