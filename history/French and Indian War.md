# French and Indian War

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Dates:** 1754–1763
> **Also Known As:** The Seven Years' War (global theater)
> **Combatants:** Britain + colonial militia + some Native allies vs. France + other Native allies
> **Result:** British victory; [[Treaty of Paris (1763)]]; France expelled from most of North America
> **Significance:** Direct cause of the [[American Revolution]] — Britain's war debt led to new colonial taxes

---

The **French and Indian War** (1754–1763) was the North American theater of the larger global conflict known as the **[[Seven Years' War]]**. Fought between Britain and France (with various Native American tribes allied to both sides), it ended with a decisive British victory and fundamentally reshaped North America — and set in motion the chain of events that led directly to the **[[American Revolution]]**.

---

## Causes

### Territorial Rivalry

Both Britain and France claimed the **[[Ohio River Valley]]** — a strategically vital region that controlled access to the interior of the continent and the lucrative [[fur trade]]. British colonists were pushing westward; French traders and soldiers were already there, building forts.

> [!info] Fort Necessity (1754)
> A young Virginia militia officer named **[[George Washington]]** led a force to confront French troops in the Ohio Valley. After a brief skirmish, Washington was forced to surrender at **Fort Necessity** — one of his only military defeats. This engagement is often cited as the opening battle of the war.

### Native American Alliances

Native American tribes formed alliances based on existing trade relationships and strategic interest:
- Most tribes initially allied with **France**, whose traders had deeper relationships and whose settlements were less threatening to Native land
- The **Iroquois Confederacy** largely aligned with Britain

## Key Events

| Event | Details |
|---|---|
| **Braddock's Defeat (1755)** | British General Braddock led a disastrous expedition against Fort Duquesne; Washington helped organize the retreat |
| **Fall of Quebec (1759)** | British General Wolfe defeated French forces on the Plains of Abraham in a decisive battle; both commanders died in the fighting |
| **Treaty of Paris (1763)** | Formally ended the war; France ceded virtually all of its North American territory |

---

## Treaty of Paris (1763)

> [!info] Terms
> - France ceded **Canada** and all territory east of the Mississippi to Britain
> - France ceded Louisiana (west of the Mississippi) to Spain as compensation
> - Spain ceded **Florida** to Britain
> - France was effectively eliminated as a power in North America

---

## Consequences

### British Debt → Colonial Taxes → Revolution

> [!info] The Chain Reaction
> The French and Indian War left Britain with a **massive national debt**. To pay it, Parliament decided the American colonies — who had benefited from the war — should contribute through taxes. This produced the [[Stamp Act]] (1765), [[Townshend Acts]] (1767), and other measures that colonists found intolerable, leading directly to the **[[American Revolution]]**.

### Pontiac's Rebellion (1763)

After the war, Britain replaced France's more cooperative approach to Native Americans with harsh policies. **[[Pontiac's Rebellion]]** — a coordinated Native uprising — erupted in response. Britain issued the **Proclamation of 1763** forbidding colonists from settling west of the Appalachians, which further enraged colonists.

### Colonial Military Experience

The war gave colonial leaders — especially [[George Washington]] — invaluable military experience they would use in the Revolution.
