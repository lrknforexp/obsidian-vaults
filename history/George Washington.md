# George Washington

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Born:** February 22, 1732 — Westmoreland County, Virginia
> **Died:** December 14, 1799 — Mount Vernon, Virginia
> **Presidency:** 1789–1797 (1st President of the United States)
> **Party:** None (warned against political parties)
> **Vice President:** [[John Adams]]

---

**George Washington** was the commanding general of the [[Continental Army]] during the [[American Revolution]] and the **first President of the United States**, serving from 1789 to 1797. He is widely regarded as the "Father of His Country" and one of the most consequential figures in American history. His decisions during his presidency established precedents that shaped the executive branch for generations.

---

## Early Life and Military Career

Washington was born into a Virginia planter family and received limited formal education. He gained early military experience serving in the [[French and Indian War]] (1754–1763) as a British colonial officer, where he developed the leadership and tactical instincts that would later define his command of the Continental Army.

> [!info] French and Indian War
> Washington's early military career was shaped by the [[French and Indian War]]. He led Virginia militia forces and experienced both defeat and resilience — lessons he carried into the Revolution two decades later.

---

## Commander of the Continental Army

When the [[Second Continental Congress]] convened in 1775, Washington was appointed **Commander-in-Chief of the Continental Army**. Despite facing a far better-equipped British military, he kept the revolutionary cause alive through a combination of strategic retreats, surprise attacks, and force of character.

Key moments of his command:
- **[[Battle of Trenton]] (1776)** — Surprise crossing of the Delaware River on Christmas night; a critical morale victory
- **[[Valley Forge]] (1777–1778)** — Endured devastating winter conditions; emerged with a more disciplined army
- **[[Battle of Yorktown]] (1781)** — In alliance with French forces, trapped and forced the surrender of British General [[Cornwallis]], effectively ending the war

---

## Presidency (1789–1797)

Washington was unanimously elected president by the [[Electoral College]] — the only president ever to receive unanimous electoral votes, doing so **twice**.

### Key Precedents Set

> [!info] The Two-Term Precedent
> Washington voluntarily stepped down after **two four-year terms**, rejecting any suggestion that he serve for life or become a monarch. This set the **two-term tradition** that all presidents honored until [[Franklin D. Roosevelt]] in 1940. It was later codified into law by the **[[22nd Amendment]]** (1951).

### The Whiskey Rebellion (1794)

> [!info] The Whiskey Rebellion
> When western Pennsylvania farmers revolted against the [[Alexander Hamilton|Hamilton]]-backed excise tax on whiskey, Washington personally led nearly 13,000 federal troops to suppress the uprising — the **only time a sitting U.S. president personally commanded troops in the field**. The rebellion was dispersed without significant bloodshed, but the message was clear: the federal government had the authority and the will to enforce its laws. See: **[[Whiskey Rebellion]]**

### Jay's Treaty (1794)

Washington authorized [[John Jay]] to negotiate a treaty with Britain to resolve lingering conflicts from the Revolution. The resulting **[[Jay's Treaty]]** was deeply controversial — critics felt it conceded too much to Britain — but Washington pushed it through, establishing the principle that the **president is the nation's chief foreign diplomat**.

### Farewell Address (1796)

> [!info] Washington's Farewell Address
> In his 1796 **[[Farewell Address]]**, Washington issued three major warnings to the young nation:
> 1. **Against political parties** — he feared factionalism would tear the nation apart (it already had, as the [[Federalist Party]] and [[Democratic-Republican Party]] were already forming)
> 2. **Against sectionalism** — regional loyalties over national unity would be destructive
> 3. **Against permanent foreign alliances** — entanglements with European powers would drag America into unnecessary wars

---

## Legacy

Washington's legacy is foundational to American political culture. He:
- Proved that a republic could survive the transition of power peacefully
- Established the norm of civilian control over the military
- Set the standard for the presidency that successors measured themselves against
- Is memorialized on the **[[U.S. dollar bill]]**, the **[[Washington Monument]]**, and in the name of the nation's capital, **[[Washington, D.C.]]**
