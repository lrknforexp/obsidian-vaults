# Mexican-American War

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Dates:** April 25, 1846 – February 2, 1848
> **Combatants:** United States vs. Mexico
> **U.S. President:** James K. Polk
> **Result:** U.S. victory; [[Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo]]
> **Territory Gained:** California, Nevada, Utah, Arizona, New Mexico, Colorado, Wyoming (~525,000 sq miles)
> **Cost:** ~$15 million payment to Mexico + assumption of $3.25 million in American claims

---

The **Mexican-American War** (1846–1848) was an armed conflict between the United States and Mexico that resulted in a massive American territorial gain in the Southwest. Driven by the ideology of **[[Manifest Destiny]]** and President James K. Polk's aggressive expansionism, the war ended with the **[[Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo]]**, through which Mexico ceded roughly half its national territory to the United States.

---

## Causes

### Texas Annexation

The immediate cause was the **[[Texas Annexation]]** (1845). Texas had won independence from Mexico in 1836 and functioned as an independent republic, but Mexico never recognized that independence and considered Texas still Mexican territory. When the U.S. Congress voted to annex Texas as a state in 1845, Mexico broke off diplomatic relations.

### Border Dispute

The U.S. and Mexico disagreed on where Texas ended:
- The U.S. claimed the border was the **Rio Grande**
- Mexico claimed it was the **Nueces River** (roughly 100 miles north)

President Polk sent General **Zachary Taylor** to march troops into the disputed zone between the two rivers. Mexican forces attacked, killing 11 American soldiers.

> [!info] "American Blood on American Soil"
> Polk went to Congress claiming Mexican forces had "shed American blood on American soil" — conveniently not mentioning that the soil in question was disputed. Congress declared war on May 13, 1846.
>
> Critics — including a freshman Congressman named **Abraham Lincoln** — challenged Polk to name the exact spot where American blood had been shed, arguing the whole thing was a manufactured pretext.

---

## The War

American forces performed well militarily on multiple fronts:

| Campaign | Details |
|---|---|
| **Northern Mexico** | General Zachary Taylor won key battles at Monterrey and Buena Vista |
| **California** | John C. Frémont and naval forces seized California in the **Bear Flag Revolt** |
| **Mexico City** | General Winfield Scott led an amphibious landing at Veracruz and marched to capture the Mexican capital |

**[[Ulysses S. Grant]]** and **Robert E. Lee** — both future Civil War commanders — served as young officers in the Mexican-American War.

---

## Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo (1848)

> [!info] Terms
> - Mexico recognized the **Rio Grande** as the Texas border
> - Mexico ceded **California, Nevada, Utah, Arizona, New Mexico**, and parts of Colorado and Wyoming to the United States — the **Mexican Cession**
> - The U.S. paid Mexico **$15 million** and assumed $3.25 million in American claims against Mexico
> - Mexican citizens in the ceded territory were offered U.S. citizenship

---

## Consequences and Controversy

> [!info] The Slavery Question Returns
> The vast new territories immediately reignited the explosive question: **would slavery be allowed in the new lands?**
> - The **Wilmot Proviso** (1846) — proposed by Congressman David Wilmot — would have banned slavery from all territory acquired from Mexico. It passed the House repeatedly but was blocked in the Senate.
> - The debate over slavery in the new territories led directly to the **Compromise of 1850**, the [[Kansas-Nebraska Act]] (1854), and ultimately the [[Civil War]].

The war was deeply controversial at the time:
- **Supporters** saw it as fulfillment of [[Manifest Destiny]] and God-given American expansion
- **Critics** — including Ulysses S. Grant (later) and Henry David Thoreau (who went to jail rather than pay taxes to support the war) — called it an immoral land grab designed to extend slavery
