# Farewell Address

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Date:** September 19, 1796 (published; never delivered as a speech)
> **Author:** [[George Washington]] (with assistance from [[Alexander Hamilton]])
> **Published in:** Philadelphia's American Daily Advertiser
> **Significance:** Foundational statement of American foreign and domestic policy; read aloud in Congress annually to this day

---

**Washington's Farewell Address** was a letter published by President **[[George Washington]]** in September 1796 announcing his retirement from the presidency and offering advice to the young nation. It contained three major warnings that shaped American policy for generations — against political parties, against sectionalism, and against permanent foreign alliances.

---

## Background

Washington had planned to retire after one term but was persuaded to serve a second. By 1796, he was exhausted, had been savagely attacked in the partisan press, and was determined to step down. He asked [[Alexander Hamilton]] to help him draft his farewell message — Hamilton wrote a substantial portion of the final text based on Washington's notes.

---

## The Three Warnings

### 1. Against Political Parties

> [!info] The Danger of Factionalism
> Washington warned that **political parties** — which he called "factions" — were the greatest threat to republican government. He feared that parties would:
> - Put **party loyalty above national interest**
> - Become vehicles for "cunning, ambitious, and unprincipled men" to seize power
> - Divide the country into hostile camps that could tear the republic apart
>
> This warning was already being ignored — the [[Federalist Party]] and [[Democratic-Republican Party]] were actively competing for power. But Washington's concern proved prescient: partisan polarization has been a constant challenge in American democracy.

### 2. Against Sectionalism

Washington warned against **regional loyalties** — the tendency of Americans to identify primarily as Northerners, Southerners, Easterners, or Westerners rather than as Americans. He feared that sectionalism would:
- Generate resentment and hostility between regions
- Be exploited by foreign powers seeking to divide and weaken the nation
- Eventually destroy the Union

The [[Civil War]], driven by sectional conflict over slavery, would prove Washington's fears well-founded.

### 3. Against Permanent Foreign Alliances

> [!info] "Entangling Alliances"
> Washington warned against **permanent alliances** with foreign nations: "It is our true policy to steer clear of permanent alliances with any portion of the foreign world."
>
> He was NOT saying America should be isolationist — he explicitly supported **commercial relations** with all nations. But he warned that permanent political-military alliances would:
> - Drag America into European wars that were none of its business
> - Create "artificial ties" and "real enmities" that would compromise American independence
>
> This principle guided American foreign policy for over a century. The U.S. did not enter a permanent peacetime military alliance until **NATO** (1949).

---

## Legacy

The Farewell Address is read aloud in the U.S. Senate every year on Washington's birthday (February 22). Its warnings:
- Against parties — regularly invoked but rarely heeded
- Against sectionalism — proven tragically accurate by the Civil War
- Against entangling alliances — shaped American foreign policy from 1796 through World War II
