# Declaration of Independence

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Adopted:** July 4, 1776
> **Primary Author:** [[Thomas Jefferson]]
> **Also Drafted by:** [[Benjamin Franklin]], [[John Adams]] (with minor revisions)
> **Adopted by:** [[Second Continental Congress]], Philadelphia
> **Significance:** Formally declared American independence from Britain; articulated the philosophical foundation of American democracy

---

The **Declaration of Independence** is one of the most important documents in world history. Adopted on **July 4, 1776**, it announced the thirteen American colonies' separation from [[Great Britain]] and articulated — for the first time in an official government document — that governments derive their authority from the **consent of the governed**, and that people have the right to overthrow governments that fail to protect their rights.

---

## Background

By the spring of 1776, the [[American Revolution]] had been underway for over a year. The [[Second Continental Congress]] appointed a **Committee of Five** to draft a formal declaration: [[Thomas Jefferson]], [[John Adams]], [[Benjamin Franklin]], Roger Sherman, and Robert Livingston. Jefferson was selected as the primary author.

---

## Key Ideas

The Declaration drew heavily on Enlightenment philosophy, particularly the ideas of **[[John Locke]]**:

> [!info] Core Principles
> - **Natural rights** — All men are endowed with unalienable rights: **"life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness"**
> - **Social contract** — Governments are created by the people to protect these rights
> - **Consent of the governed** — Governments derive their "just powers from the consent of the governed"
> - **Right of revolution** — When a government becomes destructive of these ends, the people have the right to "alter or abolish it" and create a new government

---

## Structure

The Declaration has three main parts:

**1. Preamble** — States the philosophical basis for independence (the famous opening paragraphs about natural rights and consent of the governed)

**2. List of Grievances** — A long list of specific complaints against King George III, justifying the decision to separate. These include:
- Imposing taxes without colonial consent
- Quartering soldiers in colonial homes
- Cutting off colonial trade
- Denying colonists the right to trial by jury

**3. Declaration** — The formal announcement that the colonies are "Free and Independent States," absolved of all allegiance to the British Crown.

---

## The Slavery Contradiction

> [!info] Jefferson's Deleted Passage
> Jefferson's original draft included a passage blaming King George III for introducing the slave trade to the colonies. This passage was **removed** at the insistence of Southern delegates from Georgia and South Carolina, who feared it would threaten the institution of slavery.
>
> The Declaration's assertion that "all men are created equal" stood in profound contradiction with the reality of chattel slavery — a contradiction [[Thomas Jefferson]] himself embodied, as he enslaved over 600 people over his lifetime. This tension would define American politics for the next century, culminating in the [[Civil War]].

---

## Legacy

- The Declaration became the **philosophical foundation** of American democracy and self-governance
- Its language about natural rights and human equality has been invoked by nearly every major reform movement in American history — the abolitionist movement, the women's suffrage movement, the civil rights movement
- It inspired democratic revolutions around the world, including the French Revolution and later independence movements in Latin America and beyond
- July 4 — **Independence Day** — is celebrated annually as the national holiday of the United States
