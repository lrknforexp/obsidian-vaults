# Adams-Onís Treaty

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Also Known As:** Transcontinental Treaty of 1819; Florida Purchase Treaty
> **Signed:** February 22, 1819
> **Parties:** United States and Spain
> **Negotiated by:** Secretary of State John Quincy Adams and Spanish Minister Luis de Onís
> **Result:** Spain ceded Florida to the United States; U.S. and Spain established a transcontinental boundary to the Pacific

---

The **Adams-Onís Treaty** (also called the **Transcontinental Treaty**) was a landmark diplomatic agreement between the United States and Spain, signed on February 22, 1819. The treaty accomplished two major things: it transferred **Florida** from Spain to the United States, resolving years of border conflict, and it established a clear boundary line between U.S. and Spanish territory that extended all the way to the **Pacific Ocean** — the first time the United States had a formal claim to Pacific coast territory. It was one of the greatest diplomatic achievements in American history and a defining step toward the continental expansion that would be called [[Manifest Destiny]].

---

## Background

By the late 1810s, Spanish control over Florida had become a fiction. The territory was a source of chronic instability:

- **Runaway enslaved people** from Georgia and the Carolinas escaped into Florida, where Spanish authority was too weak to return them
- **Seminole warriors** raided American settlements across the Georgia border and provided refuge to escaped slaves
- **British agents** used Florida as a base to supply and encourage Native resistance to American expansion
- **Lawless frontier violence** was rampant; Spain could neither control Florida nor protect it

The crisis came to a head in 1817–1818 when President **James Monroe** authorized General **[[Andrew Jackson]]** to pursue Seminole raiders across the border into Spanish Florida.

---

## Jackson's Florida Invasion (1817–1818)

> [!info] Jackson Exceeds His Orders
> Jackson pursued the Seminoles deep into Florida, capturing two Spanish forts, executing two British subjects he accused of inciting the Seminoles, and briefly occupying Pensacola — effectively seizing a foreign colony without a declaration of war.
>
> The Monroe cabinet was alarmed. Only Secretary of State **John Quincy Adams** defended Jackson, arguing that Spain's failure to control Florida justified the American action. Most of the cabinet wanted to disavow Jackson and return the territory. Adams turned the episode into a diplomatic hammer: Spain must either control Florida or cede it.

---

## Terms of the Treaty

> [!info] What the Treaty Accomplished
> **1. Spain ceded all of Florida to the United States**
> The United States paid no money to Spain; instead, it agreed to assume up to $5 million in claims that American citizens held against the Spanish government.
>
> **2. The Sabine River was established as the western boundary**
> The U.S. gave up any claim to Texas (which some Americans argued was part of the [[Louisiana Purchase]]). This was controversial at home.
>
> **3. The Transcontinental Boundary**
> Most significantly, the treaty established a stepped boundary line running from the Sabine River (Texas-Louisiana border) westward and northward to the **42nd parallel**, then due west along that parallel to the **Pacific Ocean**. This gave the United States a formal claim to the Pacific coast for the first time — a claim that would be the foundation of later arguments about **Oregon Territory**.

---

## Significance

> [!info] Why the Adams-Onís Treaty Mattered
> 1. **Florida secured** — Ended the chronic instability on America's southeastern border; Florida became a U.S. territory in 1821 and a state in 1845
>
> 2. **First claim to the Pacific** — The transcontinental boundary established the legal foundation for American claims to the Pacific Northwest, which would become central to the Oregon boundary dispute with Britain in the 1840s and to the ideology of [[Manifest Destiny]]
>
> 3. **Adams's triumph** — The treaty was John Quincy Adams's greatest achievement as Secretary of State and established him as one of the finest diplomats in American history. It demonstrated that skillful diplomacy — backed by Jackson's military muscle — could achieve territorial gains without war.
>
> 4. **Texas controversy** — The U.S. renunciation of Texas claims angered Southerners and Western expansionists who believed Texas should be American. This resentment fueled later efforts to annex Texas and ultimately contributed to the [[Mexican-American War]].
