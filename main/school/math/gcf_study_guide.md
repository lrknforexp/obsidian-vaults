# GCF Study Guide

---

## Finding the GCF of 2 Numbers

1. Use factor trees to break down the numbers completely.
2. List the factors at the bottom of each branch.
3. Look for factors that appear on BOTH lists.
4. The GCF is the product of the common factors.

---

## Finding the GCF of 2 Expressions

1. Use factor trees to break down the expressions. Numbers go to the left, variables to the right.
2. Continue breaking down each side completely.
3. List the factors at the bottom of each branch.
4. Look for factors that appear on BOTH lists.
5. The GCF is the product of the common factors (numbers and variables).

---

## Using GCF to Factor an Expression

1. Use factor trees to find the factors of each term in the expression.
2. List the factors at the bottom of each branch.
3. Look for factors that appear on BOTH lists.
4. The GCF goes OUTSIDE the parentheses.
5. Divide each term by the GCF. That result goes INSIDE the parentheses.

---

## Factoring by Grouping

Use this method when the GCF of all 4 terms is 1.

1. Group the 4 terms into 2 groups of 2 using parentheses.
2. Find the GCF of each group.
3. Factor each group. The same factor should appear in both groups.
4. The shared factor becomes the first factor.
5. Combine the 2 GCFs to create the second factor.

## Worked Examples (Fully Explained)

---

### Example 1: Finding GCF of 2 Numbers
Problem: Find the GCF of 48 and 60.

Step 1: Build a factor tree for 48.
Start with 48. Ask: what two numbers multiply to give 48?
48 = 2 × 24. Now break down 24. 24 = 2 × 12. Break down 12. 12 = 2 × 6. Break down 6. 6 = 2 × 3. Both 2 and 3 are prime, so you stop here.
Full breakdown: 48 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3
List of prime factors for 48: 2, 2, 2, 2, 3

Step 2: Build a factor tree for 60.
60 = 2 × 30. Break down 30. 30 = 2 × 15. Break down 15. 15 = 3 × 5. All prime, so stop.
Full breakdown: 60 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5
List of prime factors for 60: 2, 2, 3, 5

Step 3: Compare both lists and find what appears in BOTH.
48 has: 2, 2, 2, 2, 3
60 has: 2, 2, 3, 5
Both share: 2, 2, and 3. You only take what appears in BOTH lists. 48 has four 2s but 60 only has two 2s, so you take two 2s. Both have one 3, so you take one 3.

Step 4: Multiply the common factors.
GCF = 2 × 2 × 3 = 12
The GCF of 48 and 60 is 12.

---

### Example 2: Finding GCF of 2 Expressions
Problem: Find the GCF of 18x³y² and 24x²y⁴.

Step 1: Break down the numbers separately from the variables.
18 = 2 × 3 × 3
24 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3
You keep numbers and variables separate because they are different types of factors.

Step 2: Find common number factors.
18 has: 2, 3, 3
24 has: 2, 2, 2, 3
Both share: one 2 and one 3. Multiply them: 2 × 3 = 6.
The number part of the GCF is 6.

Step 3: Find common variable factors.
For x: x³ = x × x × x and x² = x × x. Both share x twice, so the common part is x². You always take the LOWEST exponent because that is what both expressions actually have.
For y: y² = y × y and y⁴ = y × y × y × y. Both share y twice, so the common part is y². Lowest exponent wins.

Step 4: Combine number and variable GCFs.
GCF = 6 × x² × y² = 6x²y²
The GCF of 18x³y² and 24x²y⁴ is 6x²y².

---

### Example 3: Using GCF to Factor a 3-Term Expression
Problem: Factor 12x⁴ + 18x³ − 24x²

Step 1: Break down every term completely.
12x⁴ = 2 × 2 × 3 × x × x × x × x
18x³ = 2 × 3 × 3 × x × x × x
24x² = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × x × x
You need to fully factor every single term so you can see what they all share.

Step 2: Find what is common across ALL three terms.
Numbers: 12 has 2,2,3 — 18 has 2,3,3 — 24 has 2,2,2,3. All three share one 2 and one 3. So the number part is 2 × 3 = 6.
Variables: All three terms have at least x². The term with the fewest x's is 24x², which only has two. That sets the limit. GCF for x is x².
GCF = 6x²

Step 3: Divide every term by the GCF.
This tells you what goes inside the parentheses.
12x⁴ ÷ 6x² = 2x² (because 12÷6=2 and x⁴÷x²=x²)
18x³ ÷ 6x² = 3x (because 18÷6=3 and x³÷x²=x)
24x² ÷ 6x² = 4 (because 24÷6=4 and x²÷x²=1, which disappears)

Step 4: Write the final answer.
The GCF goes outside the parentheses. The divided results go inside.
Answer: 6x²(2x² + 3x − 4)

Step 5: Check your answer by distributing back.
6x² × 2x² = 12x⁴ ✓
6x² × 3x = 18x³ ✓
6x² × (−4) = −24x² ✓
You get the original expression back. The answer is correct.

### Example 4: Factoring by Grouping (All Positive Terms)
Problem: Factor 4x³ + 6x² + 10x + 15

Step 1: Check the GCF of all 4 terms first.
4x³, 6x², 10x, 15. The only number that divides all four is 1. There are no shared variables either. So the overall GCF is 1. This means you cannot factor the whole expression at once. Use grouping.

Step 2: Split into 2 groups of 2.
Group 1: (4x³ + 6x²)
Group 2: (10x + 15)
Group the first two terms together and the last two together. Keep the + sign between the groups.

Step 3: Find the GCF of Group 1.
4x³ = 2 × 2 × x × x × x
6x² = 2 × 3 × x × x
Common factors: 2, x, x → GCF = 2x²
4x³ ÷ 2x² = 2x and 6x² ÷ 2x² = 3.
Group 1 factored: 2x²(2x + 3)

Step 4: Find the GCF of Group 2.
10x = 2 × 5 × x
15 = 3 × 5
Common factors: 5 → GCF = 5
10x ÷ 5 = 2x and 15 ÷ 5 = 3.
Group 2 factored: 5(2x + 3)

Step 5: Check that both groups share the same binomial factor.
Group 1 gave you: 2x²(2x + 3)
Group 2 gave you: 5(2x + 3)
Both share (2x + 3). This becomes the first factor of your answer.

Step 6: Combine the outside terms to make the second factor.
The terms outside the shared binomial are 2x² and 5.
Answer: (2x + 3)(2x² + 5)

---

### Example 5: Factoring by Grouping with Negative Signs
Problem: Factor 6x³ − 9x² − 4x + 6

This problem is harder because of the negative signs. They can make it look like the shared binomials do not match. Here is how to handle it.

Step 1: Check the overall GCF. It is 1. Use grouping.

Step 2: Split into 2 groups of 2.
Group 1: (6x³ − 9x²)
Group 2: (−4x + 6)
Keep the negative sign attached to −4x when you write Group 2.

Step 3: Find the GCF of Group 1.
6x³ = 2 × 3 × x × x × x
9x² = 3 × 3 × x × x
Common factors: 3, x, x → GCF = 3x²
6x³ ÷ 3x² = 2x and 9x² ÷ 3x² = 3.
Group 1 factored: 3x²(2x − 3)

Step 4: Find the GCF of Group 2.
−4x and +6 share a factor of 2. But the leading term is negative (−4x). You need to factor out −2 so the binomial inside matches Group 1.
Factor out −2: −2(2x − 3)
Check: −2 × 2x = −4x ✓ and −2 × (−3) = +6 ✓
This is why you factor out a negative: it flips the signs inside so the binomials match.

Step 5: Both groups now share (2x − 3).
Group 1: 3x²(2x − 3)
Group 2: −2(2x − 3)

Step 6: Combine the outside terms.
Outside terms: 3x² and −2.
Answer: (2x − 3)(3x² − 2)

Step 7: Check by distributing.
(2x)(3x²) = 6x³ ✓
(2x)(−2) = −4x ✓
(−3)(3x²) = −9x² ✓
(−3)(−2) = +6 ✓
You get 6x³ − 9x² − 4x + 6. Correct.

### Example 6: GCF of 3 Expressions with Multiple Variables
Problem: Find the GCF of 36a³b²c, 48a²bc³, and 60a⁴b³c²

Step 1: Break down each number.
36 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3
48 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3
60 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5
All three share: 2, 2, and 3. GCF of numbers = 2 × 2 × 3 = 12.

Step 2: Find the GCF of each variable across all three expressions.
For a: a³, a², a⁴. The lowest power is a². GCF for a = a².
For b: b², b, b³. The lowest power is b¹. GCF for b = b.
For c: c, c³, c². The lowest power is c¹. GCF for c = c.
You always use the lowest exponent because that is the highest power that divides into all three.

Step 3: Combine everything.
GCF = 12a²bc

## Original Practice Problems with Full Answers

### Part 1: Find the GCF of Numbers and Expressions

Problem 1: Find the GCF of 30 and 44.
30 = 2 × 3 × 5
44 = 2 × 2 × 11
Common factors: just one 2. The 3, 5, and 11 do not appear in both lists.
GCF = 2

Problem 2: Find the GCF of 28 and 70.
28 = 2 × 2 × 7
70 = 2 × 5 × 7
Common factors: one 2 and one 7. Both lists share these two.
GCF = 2 × 7 = 14

Problem 3: Find the GCF of 4yz and 2y².
Numbers: 4 = 2 × 2 and 2 = 2. Both share one 2.
Variables: y appears in both (y¹ in 4yz and y² in 2y²). Lowest power is y¹. z only appears in the first term and not the second, so z is NOT in the GCF.
GCF = 2y

Problem 4: Find the GCF of 12a⁴b³ and 20a³b.
Numbers: 12 = 2 × 2 × 3 and 20 = 2 × 2 × 5. Both share 2 and 2. Product = 4.
Variables: a⁴ and a³ → lowest is a³. b³ and b¹ → lowest is b¹.
GCF = 4a³b

### Part 2: Use GCF to Factor

Problem 5: Factor 2x² + 8x
Break down each term:
2x² = 2 × x × x
8x = 2 × 2 × 2 × x
Common factors: one 2 and one x. GCF = 2x.
Divide each term: 2x² ÷ 2x = x and 8x ÷ 2x = 4.
Answer: 2x(x + 4)

Problem 6: Factor 15f − 20g²
Break down each term:
15f = 3 × 5 × f
20g² = 2 × 2 × 5 × g × g
Common numbers: just 5. There are no shared variables because f and g are different letters.
GCF = 5.
Divide each term: 15f ÷ 5 = 3f and 20g² ÷ 5 = 4g².
Answer: 5(3f − 4g²)

Problem 7: Factor 10c³d² − 15cd³
Break down each term:
10c³d² = 2 × 5 × c × c × c × d × d
15cd³ = 3 × 5 × c × d × d × d
Common numbers: 5. Common variables: c¹ (lowest of c³ and c) and d² (lowest of d² and d³).
GCF = 5cd².
Divide each term: 10c³d² ÷ 5cd² = 2c² and 15cd³ ÷ 5cd² = 3d.
Answer: 5cd²(2c² − 3d)

Problem 8: Factor 3x⁴ + 9x² − 15x
Break down each term:
3x⁴ = 3 × x × x × x × x
9x² = 3 × 3 × x × x
15x = 3 × 5 × x
Common numbers: 3. Common variable: x¹ (the lowest power across all three terms is x¹ from the 15x term).
GCF = 3x.
Divide each term: 3x⁴ ÷ 3x = x³ and 9x² ÷ 3x = 3x and 15x ÷ 3x = 5.
Answer: 3x(x³ + 3x − 5)

### Part 3: Factor by Grouping

Problem 9: Factor 2x³ + 5x² + 6x + 15
Overall GCF = 1. No single factor divides all 4 terms. Use grouping.
Group 1: (2x³ + 5x²). GCF = x². Divide: 2x³÷x²=2x and 5x²÷x²=5. Factored: x²(2x + 5).
Group 2: (6x + 15). GCF = 3. Divide: 6x÷3=2x and 15÷3=5. Factored: 3(2x + 5).
Both groups share (2x + 5). Outside terms are x² and 3.
Answer: (2x + 5)(x² + 3)

Problem 10: Factor 3n³ − 4n² + 9n − 12
Overall GCF = 1. Use grouping.
Group 1: (3n³ − 4n²). GCF = n². Divide: 3n³÷n²=3n and 4n²÷n²=4. Factored: n²(3n − 4).
Group 2: (9n − 12). GCF = 3. Divide: 9n÷3=3n and 12÷3=4. Factored: 3(3n − 4).
Both groups share (3n − 4). Outside terms are n² and 3.
Answer: (3n − 4)(n² + 3)

Problem 11: Factor 35xy − 5x − 56y + 8
Overall GCF = 1. Use grouping.
Group 1: (35xy − 5x). GCF = 5x. Divide: 35xy÷5x=7y and 5x÷5x=1. Factored: 5x(7y − 1).
Group 2: (−56y + 8). GCF = −8. You factor out −8 (negative) so the signs inside match Group 1.
−56y ÷ −8 = 7y and 8 ÷ −8 = −1. Factored: −8(7y − 1).
Both groups share (7y − 1). Outside terms are 5x and −8.
Answer: (7y − 1)(5x − 8)

Problem 12: Factor az + ac − yz − yc
Overall GCF = 1. Use grouping.
Group 1: (az + ac). GCF = a. Divide: az÷a=z and ac÷a=c. Factored: a(z + c).
Group 2: (−yz − yc). GCF = −y. You factor out −y because both terms are negative.
−yz ÷ −y = z and −yc ÷ −y = c. Factored: −y(z + c).
Both groups share (z + c). Outside terms are a and −y.
Answer: (z + c)(a − y)

## Answers to Hard Practice Problems (Fully Explained)

### Part 4

Problem 1: GCF of 36a³b²c and 48a²bc³
Numbers: 36 = 2×2×3×3 and 48 = 2×2×2×2×3. Common: 2, 2, 3 → product = 12.
a: lowest of a³ and a² is a². b: lowest of b² and b¹ is b. c: lowest of c¹ and c³ is c.
GCF = 12a²bc

Problem 2: GCF of 15m⁴n³, 25m²n⁵, and 35m³n²
Numbers: 15=3×5, 25=5×5, 35=5×7. All three share one 5. Number GCF = 5.
m: m⁴, m², m³ → lowest is m². n: n³, n⁵, n² → lowest is n².
GCF = 5m²n²

Problem 3: GCF of 60x⁵y³z², 84x³y⁴z, and 96x⁴y²z³
Numbers: 60=2×2×3×5, 84=2×2×3×7, 96=2×2×2×2×2×3. All share 2, 2, 3 → product = 12.
x: x⁵, x³, x⁴ → lowest is x³. y: y³, y⁴, y² → lowest is y². z: z², z¹, z³ → lowest is z.
GCF = 12x³y²z

### Part 5

Problem 1: Factor 16x⁵ − 24x⁴ + 40x³
16x⁵ = 2×2×2×2 × x⁵
24x⁴ = 2×2×2×3 × x⁴
40x³ = 2×2×2×5 × x³
Common numbers: 2,2,2 → 8. Common variable: x³ (lowest power across all 3 terms).
GCF = 8x³.
Divide: 16x⁵÷8x³=2x², 24x⁴÷8x³=3x, 40x³÷8x³=5.
Answer: 8x³(2x² − 3x + 5)

Problem 2: Factor 9a³b² − 18a²b³ + 27ab⁴
9a³b² = 3×3 × a³ × b²
18a²b³ = 2×3×3 × a² × b³
27ab⁴ = 3×3×3 × a × b⁴
Common numbers: 3,3 → 9. a: lowest is a¹. b: lowest is b².
GCF = 9ab².
Divide: 9a³b²÷9ab²=a², 18a²b³÷9ab²=2ab, 27ab⁴÷9ab²=3b².
Answer: 9ab²(a² − 2ab + 3b²)

Problem 3: Factor 14m³n² + 21m²n³ − 35m⁴n
14m³n² = 2×7 × m³ × n²
21m²n³ = 3×7 × m² × n³
35m⁴n = 5×7 × m⁴ × n
Common numbers: 7. m: lowest is m². n: lowest is n¹.
GCF = 7m²n.
Divide: 14m³n²÷7m²n=2mn, 21m²n³÷7m²n=3n², 35m⁴n÷7m²n=5m².
Answer: 7m²n(2mn + 3n² − 5m²)

Problem 4: Factor 6x⁵ − 15x⁴ + 9x³ − 3x²
6x⁵ = 2×3 × x⁵
15x⁴ = 3×5 × x⁴
9x³ = 3×3 × x³
3x² = 3 × x²
Common numbers: 3. Common variable: x² (lowest power across all four terms).
GCF = 3x².
Divide: 6x⁵÷3x²=2x³, 15x⁴÷3x²=5x², 9x³÷3x²=3x, 3x²÷3x²=1.
Answer: 3x²(2x³ − 5x² + 3x − 1)

### Part 6

Problem 1: Factor 6x³ + 10x² + 9x + 15
Overall GCF = 1. Use grouping.
Group 1: (6x³ + 10x²). GCF = 2x². 6x³÷2x²=3x and 10x²÷2x²=5. Factored: 2x²(3x + 5).
Group 2: (9x + 15). GCF = 3. 9x÷3=3x and 15÷3=5. Factored: 3(3x + 5).
Both share (3x + 5). Outside terms: 2x² and 3.
Answer: (3x + 5)(2x² + 3)

Problem 2: Factor 8a³ − 12a² + 10a − 15
Overall GCF = 1. Use grouping.
Group 1: (8a³ − 12a²). GCF = 4a². 8a³÷4a²=2a and 12a²÷4a²=3. Factored: 4a²(2a − 3).
Group 2: (10a − 15). GCF = 5. 10a÷5=2a and 15÷5=3. Factored: 5(2a − 3).
Both share (2a − 3). Outside terms: 4a² and 5.
Answer: (2a − 3)(4a² + 5)

Problem 3: Factor 6x³ − 4x² − 15x + 10
Overall GCF = 1. Use grouping.
Group 1: (6x³ − 4x²). GCF = 2x². 6x³÷2x²=3x and 4x²÷2x²=2. Factored: 2x²(3x − 2).
Group 2: (−15x + 10). GCF = −5. You factor out −5 because the leading term is negative. −15x÷−5=3x and 10÷−5=−2. Factored: −5(3x − 2).
Both share (3x − 2). Outside terms: 2x² and −5.
Answer: (3x − 2)(2x² − 5)

Problem 4: Factor 10y³ + 15y² − 4y − 6
Overall GCF = 1. Use grouping.
Group 1: (10y³ + 15y²). GCF = 5y². 10y³÷5y²=2y and 15y²÷5y²=3. Factored: 5y²(2y + 3).
Group 2: (−4y − 6). GCF = −2. Both terms are negative so factor out −2. −4y÷−2=2y and −6÷−2=3. Factored: −2(2y + 3).
Both share (2y + 3). Outside terms: 5y² and −2.
Answer: (2y + 3)(5y² − 2)

Problem 5: Factor 3a³ − 6a² − 5a + 10
Overall GCF = 1. Use grouping.
Group 1: (3a³ − 6a²). GCF = 3a². 3a³÷3a²=a and 6a²÷3a²=2. Factored: 3a²(a − 2).
Group 2: (−5a + 10). GCF = −5. Factor out −5 to force the binomial to match. −5a÷−5=a and 10÷−5=−2. Factored: −5(a − 2).
Both share (a − 2). Outside terms: 3a² and −5.
Answer: (a − 2)(3a² − 5)
Extra check: Can 3a² − 5 be factored further? No. You are done.

Problem 6: Factor x³ + x²y − xy² − y³
Overall GCF = 1. Use grouping.
Group 1: (x³ + x²y). GCF = x². x³÷x²=x and x²y÷x²=y. Factored: x²(x + y).
Group 2: (−xy² − y³). GCF = −y². Both terms are negative so factor out −y². −xy²÷−y²=x and −y³÷−y²=y. Factored: −y²(x + y).
Both share (x + y). Outside terms: x² and −y².
First answer: (x + y)(x² − y²)
Extra check: x² − y² is a difference of squares. It factors as (x + y)(x − y).
Final answer: (x + y)(x + y)(x − y) = (x + y)²(x − y)
Always check if the second factor can be factored again.

## Key Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Stopping the factor tree too early.
Keep breaking down until every factor is prime. 6 is not prime. 4 is not prime. 9 is not prime. Keep going until you cannot break it down further.

Mistake 2: Using the HIGHEST exponent instead of the LOWEST for variables.
The GCF uses the lowest power. If you have x⁵ and x², the GCF is x², not x⁵. The lowest power is what divides evenly into both terms.

Mistake 3: Including a variable that only appears in one term.
If a variable appears in one expression but not the other, it is NOT part of the GCF. For example, in 4yz and 2y², z does not appear in 2y², so z is excluded.

Mistake 4: Not factoring out a negative GCF in grouping.
If the second group starts with a negative term and the binomial inside does not match Group 1, factor out a negative number. This flips the signs inside and makes the binomials match. If you skip this step, your answer will be wrong.

Mistake 5: Forgetting to check for a second round of factoring.
After grouping, look at the second binomial. It might be a difference of squares or factor further. Always check before you call it done.

Mistake 6: Not verifying your answer.
Distribute your final answer back. Multiply everything out. If you get the original expression, you are correct. If not, find the error. This takes 30 seconds and can save your grade.
