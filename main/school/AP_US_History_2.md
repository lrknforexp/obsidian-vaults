# 🇺🇸 AP United States History 2

> [!abstract] Course Overview
> AP United States History 2 covers American history from **Reconstruction (1865)** through the **present day**, focusing on major political, economic, social, and cultural transformations. The AP exam tests 9 historical periods; APUSH 2 emphasizes **Periods 6–9**.

---

## 📅 Period 6 (1865–1898): The Gilded Age

### Reconstruction (1865–1877)

> [!info] What Was Reconstruction?
> Reconstruction refers to the federal government's attempt to reintegrate Confederate states back into the Union and define the status of formerly enslaved people after the Civil War.

#### The Three Plans of Reconstruction

| Plan | Author | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Lincoln's 10% Plan** | Abraham Lincoln | 10% of voters swear loyalty oath; pardon Confederate leaders |
| **Wade-Davis Bill (1864)** | Congress (Radical Republicans) | 50% loyalty oath; stricter terms; pocket-vetoed by Lincoln |
| **Radical Reconstruction** | Congress (1867) | Military districts; 14th & 15th Amendments; Freedmen's Bureau |

#### Constitutional Amendments (Reconstruction Amendments)

- **13th Amendment (1865)** — Abolished slavery throughout the United States and all territories under its jurisdiction, with the exception of slavery as punishment for a crime.
- **14th Amendment (1868)** — Granted citizenship to all persons born or naturalized in the United States, including formerly enslaved people. Established equal protection and due process clauses. Overturned *Dred Scott v. Sandford* (1857).
- **15th Amendment (1870)** — Prohibited denying the right to vote based on race, color, or previous condition of servitude.

> [!warning] Compromise of 1877
> Also called the "Corrupt Bargain," this informal agreement ended Reconstruction. Republicans agreed to withdraw federal troops from the South in exchange for Rutherford B. Hayes winning the disputed 1876 presidential election. The result was the collapse of Reconstruction governments and the rise of Jim Crow laws.

#### The Freedmen's Bureau (1865–1872)
- Officially the **Bureau of Refugees, Freedmen, and Abandoned Lands**
- Provided food, housing, medical aid, schooling, and legal assistance to formerly enslaved people and poor whites
- Established over 1,000 schools, including several HBCUs (Historically Black Colleges and Universities)
- Abolished by Congress in 1872 under political pressure

#### Black Codes & Jim Crow Laws
- **Black Codes** — Laws passed by Southern states (1865–1866) to restrict the freedom of Black Americans; included vagrancy laws, labor contracts, and restrictions on movement
- **Jim Crow Laws** — State and local laws that enforced racial segregation in the Southern U.S. from 1877 to 1965
- ***Plessy v. Ferguson* (1896)** — Supreme Court upheld "separate but equal" doctrine, legalizing racial segregation

---

### Westward Expansion & Native American Conflicts

> [!note] Manifest Destiny Revisited
> After the Civil War, the federal government aggressively pushed westward settlement, displacing and confining Native American nations through warfare, treaties, and reservation policy.

#### Key Events

- **Homestead Act (1862)** — Gave 160 acres of public land to any citizen (or intended citizen) who farmed it for 5 years; ~270 million acres distributed
- **Transcontinental Railroad (1869)** — Completed at Promontory Summit, Utah; built by Central Pacific (west, largely Chinese labor) and Union Pacific (east, largely Irish/Civil War veterans)
- **Battle of Little Bighorn (1876)** — Lakota Sioux and Cheyenne warriors under Sitting Bull and Crazy Horse defeated Lt. Col. George Custer's 7th Cavalry
- **Dawes Act (1887)** — Broke up tribal lands into individual allotments; meant to assimilate Native Americans; resulted in the loss of ~90 million acres of Native land
- **Wounded Knee Massacre (1890)** — U.S. Army killed approximately 250–300 Lakota Sioux, mostly women, children, and elderly; effectively ended the "Indian Wars"

#### The Ghost Dance Movement
- Spiritual movement led by Wovoka (Paiute prophet) that promised a return of ancestral lands and ancestors; feared by the U.S. government as a precursor to uprising

### The Gilded Age: Industry, Labor & Politics

> [!quote] Mark Twain
> "What is the chief end of man?—to get rich. In what way?—dishonestly if we can; honestly if we must." — Mark Twain coined the term "Gilded Age" to describe the era's surface prosperity masking deep social problems.

#### Captains of Industry / Robber Barons

| Figure | Industry | Company | Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| **John D. Rockefeller** | Oil | Standard Oil | Horizontal integration; trusts |
| **Andrew Carnegie** | Steel | Carnegie Steel | Vertical integration |
| **J.P. Morgan** | Finance/Banking | J.P. Morgan & Co. | Mergers; U.S. Steel (1901) |
| **Cornelius Vanderbilt** | Railroads | New York Central RR | Rate manipulation |
| **Jay Gould** | Railroads | Union Pacific RR | Stock manipulation |

#### Economic Concepts

- **Vertical Integration** — Controlling all stages of production (raw materials → manufacturing → distribution); Carnegie's method
- **Horizontal Integration** — Merging with competitors in the same industry; Rockefeller's method via Standard Oil Trust
- **Social Darwinism** — Herbert Spencer's application of Darwin's "survival of the fittest" to society; justified wealth inequality
- **Gospel of Wealth (1889)** — Andrew Carnegie's essay arguing the wealthy had a duty to use their fortunes for public good (philanthropy)

#### Labor Movement

- **Knights of Labor (1869)** — One of the first major labor organizations; open to all workers regardless of race, gender, or skill; declined after Haymarket Affair (1886)
- **American Federation of Labor (AFL, 1886)** — Samuel Gompers; focused on skilled workers; used collective bargaining for concrete economic gains ("bread and butter unionism")
- **Haymarket Affair (1886)** — Bomb thrown at Chicago labor rally; 8 anarchists convicted; setback for the labor movement
- **Homestead Strike (1892)** — Carnegie Steel plant; Pinkerton detectives clashed with striking steelworkers; broke the union
- **Pullman Strike (1894)** — Eugene V. Debs led American Railway Union; President Cleveland used federal troops to break it; Debs imprisoned

#### Political Machines & Reform
- **Tammany Hall** — New York City Democratic political machine; Boss Tweed embezzled millions; symbol of urban political corruption
- **Pendleton Civil Service Act (1883)** — Required federal jobs to be filled by competitive exams rather than political patronage; created Civil Service Commission
- **Interstate Commerce Act (1887)** — First federal law to regulate railroads; created Interstate Commerce Commission (ICC)
- **Sherman Antitrust Act (1890)** — Prohibited business monopolies and trusts; initially used against labor unions more than corporations

---

### Populism

> [!info] The People's Party (Populists)
> Emerged from the **Farmers' Alliance** movement; represented struggling farmers who faced railroad price gouging, deflation, and crop lien systems.

**Key Demands (Omaha Platform, 1892):**
- Free coinage of silver (bimetallism) to inflate currency
- Graduated income tax
- Direct election of U.S. Senators
- Government ownership of railroads and telegraphs
- Secret (Australian) ballot

- **William Jennings Bryan** — Democratic/Populist presidential candidate (1896); gave "Cross of Gold" speech advocating silver coinage
- **William McKinley (R)** won in 1896; supported gold standard; effectively ended the Populist movement

## 📅 Period 7 (1890–1945): Imperialism, Progressivism, WWI & WWII

### American Imperialism (1890s–1910s)

> [!note] Causes of American Imperialism
> - Alfred Thayer Mahan's *The Influence of Sea Power upon History* (1890) argued naval power = global power
> - Frederick Jackson Turner's "Frontier Thesis" (1893) claimed the American frontier was closed; new frontiers needed
> - Social Darwinism & "White Man's Burden" ideology
> - Economic desire for new markets and raw materials

#### Key Events

- **Spanish-American War (1898)** — U.S. defeated Spain; gained Puerto Rico, Guam, Philippines (for $20 million); Cuba became nominally independent
  - "Remember the Maine!" — U.S. battleship exploded in Havana harbor; used as pretext for war
  - **Yellow Journalism** — Sensational reporting by Hearst (New York Journal) and Pulitzer (New York World)
- **Platt Amendment (1901)** — Gave U.S. right to intervene in Cuban affairs; Cuba was not truly independent
- **Open Door Policy (China)** — Secretary of State John Hay proposed equal trading rights in China for all nations
- **Panama Canal (1904–1914)** — Roosevelt supported Panamanian independence from Colombia; U.S. built canal connecting Atlantic and Pacific
- **Roosevelt Corollary (1904)** — Extension of Monroe Doctrine; U.S. claimed right to intervene in Latin American countries to stabilize their economic affairs

---

### The Progressive Era (1890s–1920)

> [!abstract] Progressivism Defined
> A broad reform movement responding to industrialization's negative effects: poverty, urban squalor, political corruption, and corporate power. Progressives believed government should actively solve social problems.

#### Muckrakers (Investigative Journalists)

| Journalist | Work | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Upton Sinclair** | *The Jungle* (1906) | Exposed meatpacking industry; led to Pure Food and Drug Act |
| **Ida Tarbell** | *History of Standard Oil* (1904) | Exposed Rockefeller's monopoly; helped break up Standard Oil |
| **Lincoln Steffens** | *The Shame of the Cities* (1904) | Exposed urban political corruption |
| **Jacob Riis** | *How the Other Half Lives* (1890) | Exposed New York tenement conditions with photography |

#### Progressive Legislation

- **Pure Food and Drug Act (1906)** — Regulated food and medicines; created FDA's predecessor
- **Meat Inspection Act (1906)** — Required federal inspection of meat processing
- **16th Amendment (1913)** — Authorized federal income tax
- **17th Amendment (1913)** — Direct election of U.S. Senators (previously chosen by state legislatures)
- **18th Amendment (1919)** — Prohibition of alcohol
- **19th Amendment (1920)** — Women's suffrage

#### Progressive Presidents

**Theodore Roosevelt (1901–1909):**
- "Square Deal" — Fair treatment of all Americans; regulation of corporations, conservation, consumer protection
- Trust-buster: broke up Northern Securities (railroad trust), Standard Oil, American Tobacco
- Created national parks; worked with Gifford Pinchot on conservation

**William Howard Taft (1909–1913):**
- Actually broke up more trusts than Roosevelt, but seen as less progressive
- Split with Roosevelt; led to formation of Bull Moose Party

**Woodrow Wilson (1913–1921):**
- "New Freedom" — Focused on restoring competition; reduced tariffs (Underwood-Simmons); Federal Reserve Act (1913); Clayton Antitrust Act (1914)
- Segregated federal government; reversed racial progress in federal workforce

#### Women's Suffrage Movement
- **Seneca Falls Convention (1848)** — Beginning of organized women's rights movement; Declaration of Sentiments
- **Susan B. Anthony & Elizabeth Cady Stanton** — Founded National Woman Suffrage Association (NWSA)
- **National American Woman Suffrage Association (NAWSA)** — Led by Carrie Chapman Catt
- **Alice Paul** — More radical; led National Woman's Party; picketed White House; force-fed during hunger strikes
- **19th Amendment (1920)** — Ratified after decades of activism

---

### World War I (1914–1918)

> [!warning] MAIN Causes of WWI
> - **M**ilitarism — European powers built up massive armies and navies
> - **A**lliances — Triple Alliance (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy) vs. Triple Entente (France, Russia, Britain)
> - **I**mperialism — Competition for colonies created tensions
> - **N**ationalism — Ethnic groups sought independence; Pan-Slavism; assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand (June 28, 1914) by Gavrilo Princip

#### U.S. Neutrality & Entry

- **Neutrality (1914–1917)** — Wilson declared neutrality; U.S. traded with both sides
- **Lusitania (1915)** — German U-boat sank British ship killing 1,198 people including 128 Americans; public outrage
- **Sussex Pledge (1916)** — Germany promised not to sink passenger ships without warning
- **Zimmermann Telegram (1917)** — Germany secretly proposed military alliance with Mexico against U.S.; published by British intelligence
- **Unrestricted Submarine Warfare (1917)** — Germany resumed; final push for U.S. entry
- **April 6, 1917** — U.S. declared war on Germany

#### American War Effort
- **Selective Service Act (1917)** — Military draft; 2.8 million men drafted
- **Espionage Act (1917) & Sedition Act (1918)** — Made it illegal to criticize the war; hundreds imprisoned including Eugene V. Debs
- **Committee on Public Information (CPI)** — George Creel; propaganda bureau
- **Great Migration** — 1.6 million Black Americans moved from South to North for factory jobs (1910–1940)

#### Wilson's Fourteen Points & Peace
- **Fourteen Points (January 8, 1918)** — Wilson's vision for post-war peace: freedom of the seas, self-determination, League of Nations, no secret treaties
- **Treaty of Versailles (1919)** — War guilt clause; reparations ($33 billion); stripped Germany of colonies and territory
- **League of Nations** — Wilson's proposed international organization; U.S. Senate rejected membership (Henry Cabot Lodge's opposition); isolationism prevailed

---

### The 1920s: The Roaring Twenties

> [!success] Prosperity & Culture
> The 1920s brought unprecedented economic growth, technological innovation, and cultural transformation — along with significant social tensions.

**Economic Boom:**
- Mass production via assembly line (Ford's Model T)
- Consumer credit and installment buying became widespread
- Stock market speculation exploded

**Cultural Changes:**
- **Jazz Age** — Louis Armstrong, Duke Ellington; African American cultural expression; Harlem Renaissance
- **Flappers** — Young women who challenged traditional gender roles; short hair, short skirts, dancing
- **Radio & Cinema** — Mass media created a national popular culture for the first time

**Social Tensions:**
- **Prohibition (1920–1933)** — 18th Amendment; speakeasies; organized crime (Al Capone); bootlegging
- **Scopes Trial (1925)** — John Scopes convicted for teaching evolution in Tennessee; clash between modernism and fundamentalism
- **KKK Revival** — 4–5 million members at peak; targeted Black Americans, Catholics, Jews, immigrants
- **Immigration Restriction** — Emergency Quota Act (1921); National Origins Act (1924) — severely limited immigration from Eastern/Southern Europe; banned Asian immigration

**Harlem Renaissance (1920s–1930s):**
- Explosion of African American art, literature, and music centered in Harlem, NYC
- Key figures: Langston Hughes (poetry), Zora Neale Hurston (fiction), W.E.B. Du Bois (*The Souls of Black Folk*), Marcus Garvey (Black nationalism/Pan-Africanism)

---

### The Great Depression (1929–1939)

> [!danger] The Stock Market Crash — Black Tuesday (October 29, 1929)
> The Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 12% in a single day. Over the next 3 years, U.S. GDP fell by 30%, unemployment rose to 25%, and thousands of banks failed.

**Causes:**
- Overproduction and under-consumption
- Over-speculation on margin buying in the stock market
- Weak banking system (over 9,000 banks failed 1930–1933)
- Smoot-Hawley Tariff (1930) — raised tariffs to record levels; international trade collapsed
- Drought — "Dust Bowl" in Great Plains devastated farms

**Hoover's Response:**
- President Herbert Hoover believed in "rugged individualism"; opposed direct federal relief
- Reconstruction Finance Corporation (1932) — loaned money to banks and railroads; seen as helping the wealthy
- **Bonus Army (1932)** — WWI veterans marched to D.C. demanding early payment; Hoover ordered General MacArthur to disperse them with force; disaster for Hoover's image

#### The New Deal (FDR, 1933–1939)

> [!success] Franklin D. Roosevelt's New Deal
> "The only thing we have to fear is fear itself." — FDR's First Inaugural Address, March 4, 1933

**The Three R's: Relief, Recovery, Reform**

**First Hundred Days (1933):**
- **Emergency Banking Act** — Closed banks for 4-day "bank holiday"; restored public confidence
- **FDIC (Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation)** — Insured bank deposits up to $2,500
- **CCC (Civilian Conservation Corps)** — Employed 3 million young men in conservation projects
- **AAA (Agricultural Adjustment Act)** — Paid farmers to reduce production; raise prices
- **NIRA (National Industrial Recovery Act)** — Regulated industry; established right to collective bargaining (Section 7a); later struck down by Supreme Court

**Second New Deal (1935):**
- **Social Security Act (1935)** — Created old-age pensions, unemployment insurance, and aid to dependent children
- **Wagner Act (National Labor Relations Act, 1935)** — Protected workers' right to organize and bargain collectively; created NLRB
- **Works Progress Administration (WPA)** — Employed 8.5 million people in public works, arts, theater, and literacy programs

**Opposition to the New Deal:**
- **Huey Long** ("Kingfish") — Louisiana governor/senator; proposed "Share Our Wealth" program; assassinated 1935
- **Father Coughlin** — Radio priest; initially supported FDR then turned anti-Semitic and fascist
- **Supreme Court** — Struck down NIRA, AAA; FDR proposed "court-packing" plan (1937) — failed but Court became more favorable

---

### World War II (1939–1945)

> [!info] Road to War
> - **Rise of Fascism**: Mussolini (Italy, 1922), Hitler (Germany, 1933), militarist Japan
> - **Appeasement**: Munich Agreement (1938) — Britain and France ceded Czechoslovakia's Sudetenland to Hitler
> - **Non-Aggression Pact (1939)** — Ribbentrop-Molotov Pact between Germany and USSR
> - **September 1, 1939** — Germany invaded Poland; Britain and France declared war

**U.S. Path from Neutrality to War:**
- **Neutrality Acts (1935–1937)** — Prohibited arms sales or loans to warring nations
- **Lend-Lease Act (1941)** — U.S. could lend or lease arms to Allies; "Arsenal of Democracy"
- **Atlantic Charter (1941)** — FDR and Churchill defined Allied war aims: no territorial expansion, self-determination, free trade, disarmament
- **Pearl Harbor (December 7, 1941)** — Japanese surprise attack on U.S. naval base in Hawaii; 2,403 killed; U.S. declared war on Japan; Germany and Italy declared war on U.S.

**The Home Front:**
- **War Production Board** — Converted factories to war production
- **Office of Price Administration** — Rationing of food, gas, rubber, metals
- **Rosie the Riveter** — Icon of women entering the workforce (18 million women worked in industry)
- **Japanese American Internment** — Executive Order 9066 (1942); 120,000 Japanese Americans forced into camps; *Korematsu v. United States* (1944) upheld as constitutional (overturned symbolically in 1988, formally in 2018)
- **Double V Campaign** — Black Americans fought for victory abroad and against racism at home; Tuskegee Airmen

**Major Turning Points:**
- **Battle of Midway (June 1942)** — U.S. sank 4 Japanese aircraft carriers; turning point in Pacific
- **Battle of Stalingrad (1942–1943)** — Soviet forces encircled German 6th Army; turning point in Europe
- **D-Day / Operation Overlord (June 6, 1944)** — Allied invasion of Normandy, France; largest amphibious assault in history
- **Battle of the Bulge (Dec. 1944–Jan. 1945)** — Last major German offensive on Western Front; Allied victory

**End of the War:**
- **V-E Day (May 8, 1945)** — Germany surrendered
- **Atomic Bombs** — Hiroshima (August 6) and Nagasaki (August 9), 1945; killed 130,000–226,000 people
- **V-J Day (September 2, 1945)** — Japan surrendered; WWII ended

**The Holocaust:**
- Systematic genocide by Nazi Germany of approximately 6 million Jews plus millions of Roma, disabled people, political prisoners, and others
- **Nuremberg Laws (1935)** — Stripped Jews of citizenship
- **Kristallnacht (1938)** — "Night of Broken Glass"; nationwide pogrom
- **Wannsee Conference (1942)** — Nazis planned the "Final Solution" (systematic extermination)
- **Nuremberg Trials (1945–1946)** — International Military Tribunal; convicted Nazi leaders for war crimes and crimes against humanity; established precedent for international law

## 📅 Period 8 (1945–1980): Cold War, Civil Rights & Vietnam

### The Cold War Begins (1945–1953)

> [!warning] Origins of the Cold War
> The U.S. and USSR emerged from WWII as superpowers with fundamentally opposing ideologies: liberal democracy and capitalism (U.S.) vs. communism (USSR). Tensions developed over Eastern Europe, atomic weapons, and spheres of influence.

**Key Doctrines & Events:**

- **Truman Doctrine (1947)** — U.S. would support free peoples resisting communist takeover; $400 million to Greece and Turkey; beginning of **containment policy**
- **Marshall Plan (1948)** — $13 billion to rebuild Western European economies; prevent communist appeal; political and economic stabilization
- **NATO (1949)** — North Atlantic Treaty Organization; collective defense alliance; "an attack on one is an attack on all"
- **Berlin Blockade & Airlift (1948–1949)** — Soviet blockade of West Berlin; U.S. and UK airlifted supplies for 11 months; Soviets backed down
- **China "Falls" to Communism (1949)** — Mao Zedong's Communist Party defeated Chiang Kai-shek's Nationalists
- **Soviet Atomic Bomb (1949)** — Ended U.S. nuclear monopoly; triggered massive arms race
- **NSC-68 (1950)** — Top-secret policy paper recommending tripling defense spending to contain Soviet expansion

**Korean War (1950–1953):**
- North Korea (Soviet-backed) invaded South Korea (U.S.-backed) in June 1950
- General MacArthur pushed back to Yalu River (Chinese border); China entered war
- MacArthur publicly disagreed with Truman's limited war strategy; Truman fired him
- **Armistice (1953)** — Restored boundary near 38th parallel; no formal peace treaty; technically ongoing

**Second Red Scare & McCarthyism:**
- **HUAC (House Un-American Activities Committee)** — Investigated suspected communist influence in Hollywood and government
- **Hollywood Ten** — Writers and directors imprisoned for refusing to testify
- **Alger Hiss Case (1948–1950)** — State Department official convicted of perjury related to Soviet espionage
- **Julius & Ethel Rosenberg (1953)** — Executed for passing atomic secrets to USSR
- **Senator Joseph McCarthy (1950–1954)** — Claimed widespread communist infiltration; "McCarthyism" = making accusations without evidence; censured by Senate in 1954 after Army-McCarthy Hearings

---

### The 1950s: Postwar Prosperity & Conformity

**Economic Boom:**
- GI Bill (Servicemen's Readjustment Act, 1944) — education, housing loans for veterans; created suburban boom
- Baby Boom (1946–1964) — 76 million births
- Levittown — Mass-produced suburbs; automobile culture; Interstate Highway System (1956)
- **Consumerism** — Television advertising, credit cards, mass consumption

**Eisenhower Presidency (1953–1961):**
- "Military-Industrial Complex" — Eisenhower's farewell address warned of growing power of defense industry
- **Domino Theory** — If one country falls to communism, neighboring countries will follow
- **Massive Retaliation** — Threatened nuclear response to any Soviet aggression
- **NASA (1958)** — Created in response to Sputnik (1957), the Soviet satellite that shocked America

### The Civil Rights Movement (1954–1968)

> [!success] Overview
> A mass social movement to end racial segregation and discrimination against African Americans and secure legal recognition and federal protection of citizenship rights.

#### Key Events Timeline

| Year | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **1954** | *Brown v. Board of Education* | Overturned *Plessy*; "separate but equal" unconstitutional |
| **1955** | Murder of Emmett Till | 14-year-old lynched in Mississippi; galvanized movement |
| **1955–56** | Montgomery Bus Boycott | Rosa Parks arrested; 381-day boycott; MLK emerges as leader |
| **1957** | Little Rock Crisis | Eisenhower sent 101st Airborne to integrate Central High School |
| **1960** | Greensboro Sit-ins | Four Black students at Woolworth's lunch counter; SNCC formed |
| **1961** | Freedom Rides | CORE activists rode interstate buses; met violent opposition |
| **1963** | Birmingham Campaign | Bull Connor's fire hoses and dogs; shocked the nation |
| **1963** | March on Washington | "I Have a Dream" speech; 250,000 people |
| **1964** | Civil Rights Act of 1964 | Banned discrimination in public accommodations and employment |
| **1964** | Mississippi Freedom Summer | Voter registration drive; Chaney, Goodman, Schwerner murdered |
| **1965** | Bloody Sunday / Selma | State troopers attacked marchers on Edmund Pettus Bridge |
| **1965** | Voting Rights Act of 1965 | Banned discriminatory voting practices; federal oversight |
| **1968** | MLK Assassination | April 4, Memphis; riots in 100+ cities |

#### Key Organizations
- **NAACP** — Legal strategy; *Brown v. Board*; Thurgood Marshall
- **SCLC** (Southern Christian Leadership Conference) — MLK; nonviolent direct action
- **SNCC** (Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee) — Student-led; John Lewis; later Stokely Carmichael (Black Power)
- **CORE** (Congress of Racial Equality) — Freedom Rides
- **Black Panther Party (1966)** — Huey Newton & Bobby Seale; self-defense; community programs; confrontational approach

#### Malcolm X & Black Power
- **Malcolm X** — Nation of Islam; advocated Black self-determination, separatism; later moderated views; assassinated 1965
- **Black Power Movement** — Stokely Carmichael popularized term (1966); Black pride, political and economic self-sufficiency, sometimes armed self-defense

---

### Vietnam War (1955–1975)

> [!info] Background
> Vietnam was divided at the 17th parallel after the French defeat at Dien Bien Phu (1954) and the Geneva Accords. The communist North (Ho Chi Minh) and non-communist South (Ngo Dinh Diem, backed by U.S.) were at war.

**U.S. Escalation:**
- **Gulf of Tonkin Incident (August 1964)** — Disputed attack on U.S. destroyers; Congress passed Gulf of Tonkin Resolution giving LBJ broad war powers
- **Operation Rolling Thunder (1965)** — Massive bombing campaign of North Vietnam
- **Troop buildup** — 16,000 advisors under JFK → 543,000 troops at peak (1969) under LBJ

**Key Concepts:**
- **Attrition Strategy** — General Westmoreland's approach: kill enough enemy to break their will
- **Viet Cong** — South Vietnamese communist guerrillas; used tunnels, booby traps, ambushes
- **Agent Orange** — Chemical defoliant used by U.S.; caused cancer, birth defects in millions
- **My Lai Massacre (1968)** — U.S. soldiers killed 347–504 unarmed South Vietnamese civilians; covered up for a year; Lt. William Calley convicted

**The Anti-War Movement:**
- **Tet Offensive (January 1968)** — Viet Cong/NVA attacked 100+ cities simultaneously; military failure but political disaster for LBJ; public turned against war
- **Kent State Massacre (1970)** — National Guard killed 4 students protesting Nixon's invasion of Cambodia; nationwide student strike
- **Pentagon Papers (1971)** — Daniel Ellsberg leaked classified government study showing administrations had lied about the war
- **Veterans Against the War** — John Kerry's testimony before Congress; Winter Soldier Investigation

**End of the War:**
- **Vietnamization** — Nixon's plan to withdraw U.S. troops and transfer fighting to South Vietnamese
- **Paris Peace Accords (1973)** — Cease-fire; U.S. troops withdrew
- **Fall of Saigon (April 30, 1975)** — North Vietnam captured the capital; Vietnam reunified under communist rule

---

### The Great Society & LBJ (1963–1969)

**Key Legislation:**
- **Civil Rights Act of 1964** — Banned discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, or national origin
- **Voting Rights Act of 1965** — Banned literacy tests; federal oversight of elections
- **Medicare & Medicaid (1965)** — Federal health insurance for elderly (Medicare) and low-income Americans (Medicaid)
- **Immigration and Nationality Act (1965)** — Abolished national-origin quotas; transformed American demographics
- **Elementary and Secondary Education Act (1965)** — Federal funding for public schools
- **Fair Housing Act (1968)** — Banned housing discrimination

---

### The Nixon Era & Watergate (1969–1974)

> [!danger] Watergate
> The scandal that led to the only presidential resignation in U.S. history.

**Watergate Break-in (June 17, 1972):** Five men arrested breaking into Democratic National Committee headquarters at the Watergate complex in Washington, D.C. Traced back to Nixon's Committee to Re-Elect the President (CREEP).

**Cover-up:** Nixon tried to obstruct the FBI investigation; White House tapes revealed he ordered the CIA to block the FBI ("smoking gun tape"); House Judiciary Committee voted to impeach.

**Resignation (August 9, 1974):** Nixon resigned before impeachment vote; Gerald Ford pardoned him.

**Nixon's Foreign Policy:**
- **Détente** — Easing of Cold War tensions; arms control agreements (SALT I, 1972)
- **Opening to China (1972)** — Historic visit; ended 23-year isolation of communist China
- **Kissinger & Realpolitik** — Foreign policy based on national interest rather than ideology

## 📅 Period 9 (1980–Present): The Modern Era

### The Reagan Revolution (1981–1989)

> [!info] Reaganomics
> Economic policy based on: supply-side economics ("trickle-down"), massive tax cuts (Economic Recovery Tax Act, 1981), deregulation, reduced domestic spending, massive military buildup.

**Key Policies:**
- Cut top income tax rate from 70% to 28%
- Increased defense spending by 40%; Strategic Defense Initiative ("Star Wars")
- Iran-Contra Affair — Secret arms sales to Iran; proceeds funneled to Nicaraguan Contras; Oliver North; nearly scandal of Nixon proportions

**End of the Cold War:**
- Reagan called USSR the "Evil Empire"; accelerated arms race that strained Soviet economy
- **Mikhail Gorbachev** — Soviet leader (1985–1991); *glasnost* (openness) and *perestroika* (restructuring)
- **INF Treaty (1987)** — Eliminated intermediate-range nuclear missiles
- **Berlin Wall Falls (November 9, 1989)** — Symbol of Cold War's end
- **USSR Dissolved (December 25, 1991)** — 15 independent republics; Cold War over

### Post-Cold War & Into the 21st Century

**Gulf War (1991):** Iraq invaded Kuwait; President George H.W. Bush assembled 34-nation coalition; Operation Desert Storm liberated Kuwait in 100 hours.

**Clinton Era (1993–2001):**
- **NAFTA (1994)** — North American Free Trade Agreement; controversial effects on manufacturing jobs
- **Welfare Reform (1996)** — Personal Responsibility and Work Opportunity Act; time limits on benefits
- **Budget Surplus** — First balanced federal budget since 1969
- **Impeachment (1998–1999)** — Monica Lewinsky scandal; acquitted by Senate

**September 11, 2001:**
- Al-Qaeda terrorist attacks; 2,977 killed; World Trade Center, Pentagon, United Flight 93

## 🧠 Key AP Exam Concepts

> [!tip] APUSH Themes (ESPRIT)
> - **E**nvironment — Human relationship with the natural world
> - **S**ocietal (Culture) — Ideas, beliefs, social structures
> - **P**olitics — Government, power, institutions
> - **R**ace & Ethnicity — Racial identity, discrimination, inequality
> - **I**nternational Relations — U.S. role in the world
> - **T**echnology & Economy — Economic development, labor, trade

### How to Write an APUSH LEQ (Long Essay Question)
1. **Thesis** — Make a historically defensible claim that establishes a line of reasoning
2. **Context** — Describe the broader historical context BEFORE the time period
3. **Evidence** — Use at least THREE specific historical examples
4. **Analysis** — Explain HOW/WHY evidence supports your argument
5. **Complexity** — Corroboration, qualification, modification, or both sides

### Common APUSH DBQ Tips
- Read all documents BEFORE writing
- Group documents into 3+ categories
- Use HAPP(Y) for sourcing: **H**istorical situation, **A**udience, **P**urpose, **P**oint of view, **Y**our evidence
- Use at least one outside (non-document) piece of evidence
- Address at least 4 of 7 documents

## 📚 Essential Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Sectionalism** | Excessive regard for regional interests over national ones |
| **Nativism** | Policy of protecting native-born citizens against immigrants |
| **Laissez-faire** | Economic policy of minimal government intervention |
| **Imperialism** | Extension of a nation's power through territorial acquisition |
| **Isolationism** | Policy of remaining apart from foreign alliances and conflicts |
| **Containment** | U.S. Cold War policy of preventing further Soviet expansion |
| **Détente** | Easing of hostilities between nations, especially U.S.-USSR |
| **Suffrage** | The right to vote |
| **Progressivism** | Reform movement to address problems caused by industrialization |
| **McCarthyism** | Making unsubstantiated accusations of disloyalty or subversion |
