# 12th Amendment

> [!info] 12th Amendment
> **Overview**
>
> **Topic**
> Reform of the Electoral College system
>
> **Ratified**

>
> **Caused by**
> [[Election of 1800]] tie between [[Thomas Jefferson]] and [[Aaron Burr]]

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Ratified:** 1804
> **Key Change:** Electors now cast separate ballots for President and Vice President
> **Triggered by:** The 73-73 electoral tie in the [[Election of 1800]]
> **Still in effect:** Yes — governs every modern presidential election

---

The **12th Amendment** to the U.S. Constitution, ratified in **1804**, reformed the Electoral College by requiring electors to cast **separate ballots** for President and Vice President. It was a direct response to the constitutional crisis of the **[[Election of 1800]]**, in which [[Thomas Jefferson]] and running mate [[Aaron Burr]] tied with 73 electoral votes each, throwing the election to the House of Representatives.

---

## Background

> [!info] Why the Original System Failed
> Under the original Constitution, each elector cast **two votes for president**. The candidate with the most votes became president; the runner-up became vice president. The Founders assumed electors would vote for the best individuals regardless of party. The rise of the **[[Federalist Party]]** and **[[Democratic-Republican Party]]** made party tickets inevitable — and the old system unworkable.

---

## Key Provisions

The amendment required electors to cast **separate ballots** for President and Vice President, preventing another tie between a presidential candidate and his own running mate. It also set rules for House runoff elections when no candidate wins a majority, and gave the Senate the power to choose the Vice President if no candidate received a majority.

---

## Significance

> [!info] Why It Matters
> The 12th Amendment formally recognized the **two-party system** and the role of political parties in American elections. It remains in effect today and governs every modern presidential election. It was the first major structural change to the Constitution since the [[Bill of Rights]].
