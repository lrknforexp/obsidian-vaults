> [!info] Quick Facts

> **Enforced by:** Federal government against state violations

---

The **14th Amendment**, ratified in **1868**, is one of the most consequential amendments to the U.S. Constitution. Passed during **[[Reconstruction]]** after the [[Civil War]], it granted citizenship to formerly enslaved people and guaranteed **equal protection under the law** and **due process** to all persons born or naturalized in the United States.

---

## Key Provisions

> [!info] The Four Major Clauses
> - **Citizenship Clause** — overturned *Dred Scott*; all persons born or naturalized in the U.S. are citizens
> - **Due Process Clause** — states cannot deprive persons of life, liberty, or property without due process
> **Equal Protection Clause** — all persons receive equal protection of the laws
> - **Disqualification Clause** — bars former Confederate officials from federal office

---

## Historical Context

The amendment was designed to protect the rights of **freed African Americans** in the South, where states were passing **Black Codes** to re-establish conditions similar to slavery. It fundamentally shifted power from states to the federal government in protecting individual rights.

---

## Legacy

> [!info] Long-Term Impact
> The 14th Amendment became the constitutional foundation for landmark Supreme Court decisions including *Brown v. Board of Education* (1954). Its Equal Protection Clause has been applied to women, immigrants, and other groups seeking equal rights. It is the most litigated amendment in American history.
