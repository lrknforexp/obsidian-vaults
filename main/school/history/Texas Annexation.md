# Texas Annexation

## Overview

**Texas Annexation** refers to the United States' admission of the **Republic of Texas** as the 28th state on December 29, 1845. Texas had declared independence from Mexico in 1836 after the **Texas Revolution** (including the famous Battle of the Alamo), but remained an independent republic for nine years before joining the U.S. — a decision that directly led to the **[[Mexican-American War]]**.

## Background

American settlers (led by Stephen F. Austin) had colonized Mexican Texas in the 1820s. By 1836, American settlers outnumbered Mexicans in Texas and revolted. The **Battle of the Alamo** (February 1836) and **Battle of San Jacinto** (April 1836) resulted in Texan independence.

## Delay in Annexation

President Andrew Jackson and his successors hesitated to annex Texas because it would likely become a slave state, inflaming the already-heated debate over slavery's expansion. Mexico also threatened war if the U.S. annexed Texas.

## Annexation and War

President John Tyler negotiated annexation in 1845. Mexico, which had never recognized Texan independence, considered annexation an act of war. A dispute over the border (Texas claimed the Rio Grande; Mexico claimed the Nueces River) provided the immediate trigger for the **[[Mexican-American War]]** (1846–1848).
