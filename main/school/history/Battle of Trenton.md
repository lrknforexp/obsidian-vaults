The **Battle of Trenton** (December 26, 1776) was a pivotal American victory during the [[American Revolution]], in which General **[[George Washington]]** led a surprise attack on Hessian forces in Trenton, New Jersey. The victory came at a desperately low moment for the Continental Army and reinvigorated the American cause.

---

## Background

> [!info] How Desperate the Situation Was
> By late 1776, the American Revolution was near collapse. The Continental Army had suffered humiliating defeats in New York, morale was shattered, and thousands of soldiers' enlistments were expiring on January 1, 1777. Washington needed a dramatic victory to boost morale and encourage reenlistment.

---

## The Crossing

On the night of December 25–26, Washington led approximately **2,400 soldiers** across the ice-choked Delaware River in a daring nighttime crossing immortalized in Emanuel Leutze's famous painting. The soldiers endured brutal cold and sleet to reach the New Jersey shore before dawn.

---

## The Battle

> [!info] The Surprise Attack
> The Continental Army attacked the Hessian garrison (German mercenaries hired by Britain) at dawn. The surprise was complete. Within **45 minutes**, Washington's forces captured over **900 prisoners** and killed the Hessian commander Colonel Johann Rall, with minimal American casualties.

---

## Significance

The Battle of Trenton, followed quickly by another victory at Princeton (January 3, 1777), revitalized American morale and encouraged reenlistment. Thomas Paine wrote that "these are the times that try men's souls" — Trenton proved Americans could win.
