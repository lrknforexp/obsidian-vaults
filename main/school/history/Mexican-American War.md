# Mexican-American War

> [!info] Mexican-American War
> **Overview**
>
> **Topic**
> War between the United States and Mexico resulting in massive territorial expansion
>
> **Dates**
> 1846–1848
>
> **President**
> James K. Polk

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Dates:** 1846–1848
> **Trigger:** Disputed border between Texas and Mexico (Rio Grande vs. Nueces River)
> **Result:** U.S. victory; [[Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo]]
> **Territory gained:** ~500,000 square miles (California, Nevada, Utah, Arizona, New Mexico, etc.)
> **Famous critic:** Congressman Abraham Lincoln questioned Polk's justification

---

The **Mexican-American War** (1846–1848) was a conflict between the United States and Mexico triggered by the **[[Texas Annexation]]** (1845) and a disputed border. The U.S. won decisively, gaining over half of Mexico's territory in the **[[Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo]]**.

---

## Causes

> [!info] How the War Started
> Mexico had never recognized Texas's independence and considered U.S. annexation of Texas an act of war. A further dispute arose over the border — Texas (and the U.S.) claimed the **Rio Grande**; Mexico claimed the **Nueces River** (further north). President Polk sent troops to the disputed zone; when Mexican forces attacked, Polk told Congress that Mexico "shed American blood on American soil."

---

## The War

> [!info] American Military Victory
> The U.S. won on multiple fronts — General Zachary Taylor won battles in northern Mexico, General Winfield Scott captured Mexico City, and American forces seized California and the Southwest. Mexico's army was outmatched. The war ended in 1848 with American forces occupying the Mexican capital.

---

## Consequences

> [!info] The Slavery Question Returns
> The vast territory acquired reignited the slavery debate — would slavery be permitted in the new lands? The **[[Compromise of 1850]]** attempted to answer this question. Ultimately, the Mexican-American War accelerated the path to the [[Civil War]].
