**Alexander Hamilton** (c. 1755–1804) was one of America's most brilliant and controversial Founding Fathers. As the first **Secretary of the Treasury**, he created the financial architecture of the United States — a national bank, a funded debt, and a credit system. He co-wrote the **[[Federalist Papers]]** and co-founded the **[[Federalist Party]]**.

---

## Financial Program

> [!info] Hamilton's Vision for America
> Hamilton believed the United States needed a strong, commercially dynamic economy to survive. His financial program included:
> - **Assumption** — the federal government would assume all state Revolutionary War debts, binding states to the federal government
> - **[[National Bank]]** — a federally chartered bank to stabilize currency and provide credit
> - **Protective tariffs** — to develop American manufacturing
> - **Report on Manufactures** — argued America should industrialize, not remain agrarian

---

## Hamilton vs. Jefferson

> [!info] The Founding Debate
> Hamilton and [[Thomas Jefferson]] represented opposite visions of America's future:
> - Hamilton: strong federal government, commercial/industrial economy, loose construction of the Constitution, pro-British foreign policy
> - Jefferson: limited government, agrarian republic, strict construction, pro-French foreign policy
> This rivalry created America's first two-party system.

---

## Death and Legacy

Hamilton was killed in a duel by Vice President **[[Aaron Burr]]** on July 11, 1804. His financial system — despite fierce opposition — ultimately shaped American capitalism. His face appears on the **$10 bill**.
