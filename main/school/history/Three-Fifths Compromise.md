# Three-Fifths Compromise

> [!info] Three-Fifths Compromise
> **Overview**
>
> **Topic**
> Constitutional provision counting enslaved people as 3/5 of a person for representation
>
> **Year**
> 1787
>
> **Context**
> [[Constitutional Convention]]

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Year:** 1787 (Constitutional Convention)
> **Provision:** Enslaved people counted as 3/5 of a person for congressional representation and taxation
> **Benefit to South:** Gave slave states more seats in Congress and more electoral votes
> **Overturned by:** [[14th Amendment]] (1868)

---

The **Three-Fifths Compromise** was an agreement reached at the **Constitutional Convention** (1787) that counted enslaved people as **three-fifths of a person** for the purposes of congressional representation and direct taxation. It gave Southern states significantly more political power than their free population alone would have warranted.

---

## The Debate

> [!info] Why It Was Controversial
> Southern states wanted enslaved people counted **fully** for representation (to increase Southern seats in Congress) but **not at all** for taxation. Northern states wanted the opposite. The three-fifths ratio was a compromise — but one that gave the South enormous and unearned political power.

---

## Political Impact

> [!info] How It Distorted American Politics
> The Three-Fifths Compromise gave Southern slave states:
> - More seats in the **House of Representatives**
> - More **Electoral College** votes
> - Disproportionate influence in electing presidents
>
> This "**slave power**" advantage meant that slaveholders dominated national politics far beyond their actual numbers — contributing to the political crises that eventually caused the [[Civil War]].

---

## Legacy

The Three-Fifths Compromise is one of the most damning examples of the Constitution's accommodation of slavery. It was effectively overturned by the **[[14th Amendment]]** (1868), which requires counting "the whole number of persons" for representation.
