**Aaron Burr** (1756–1836) was the **3rd Vice President of the United States** under [[Thomas Jefferson]]. He is best remembered for killing **[[Alexander Hamilton]]** in a duel on July 11, 1804 — one of the most dramatic events in American political history.

---

## The Election of 1800

> [!info] The Tie That Changed the Constitution
> In the [[Election of 1800]], Burr and Jefferson each received **73 electoral votes**, throwing the election to the House of Representatives. After 36 ballots, Hamilton — who despised Burr — persuaded Federalist representatives to support Jefferson. This episode directly led to the **[[12th Amendment]]** requiring separate ballots for president and vice president.

---

## The Duel

> [!info] Hamilton vs. Burr
> Hamilton had repeatedly undermined Burr's political career. When Burr ran for Governor of New York in 1804 and Hamilton again worked against him, Burr challenged Hamilton to a duel. On July 11, 1804, at Weehawken, New Jersey, Burr shot Hamilton, who died the next day. Burr was charged with murder in both New York and New Jersey but never tried.

---

## Treason Trial

After his vice presidency, Burr allegedly conspired to detach Western territories from the United States. He was tried for treason in 1807 but acquitted — partly because Chief Justice [[John Marshall]] required strict proof of "overt acts."
