# Compromise of 1850

> [!info] Compromise of 1850
> **Overview**
>
> **Topic**
> Legislative package defusing the slavery crisis after the Mexican-American War
>
> **Year**
> 1850
>
> **Key Figures**
> [[Henry Clay]], Stephen Douglas

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Year:** 1850
> **Engineered by:** Senator [[Henry Clay]]; pushed through by Stephen Douglas
> **Key Provisions:** California free state, popular sovereignty in territories, stronger Fugitive Slave Act
> **Effect:** Delayed the [[Civil War]] by roughly a decade

---

The **Compromise of 1850** was a package of five bills that temporarily defused the crisis over slavery in territories gained from the [[Mexican-American War]]. Engineered largely by Senator **[[Henry Clay]]**, it delayed the [[Civil War]] by a decade but solved nothing fundamental.

---

## Key Provisions

> [!info] The Five Bills
> - **California** admitted as a **free state**
> - Territories of New Mexico and Utah organized under **popular sovereignty** (residents vote on slavery)
> - **Slave trade** (but not slavery itself) abolished in Washington, D.C.
> - Texas received **$10 million** for surrendering land claims
> - A strengthened **Fugitive Slave Act** required Northerners to assist in capturing escaped enslaved people

---

## Reaction

> [!info] The Fugitive Slave Act Backfires
> The Fugitive Slave Act outraged Northerners who had been willing to tolerate slavery where it existed but now had to **actively participate** in its enforcement. It drove many toward abolitionism and contributed to the explosive popularity of *Uncle Tom's Cabin* (1852).

---

## Legacy

The compromise bought a decade of uneasy peace but solved nothing fundamental. The [[Kansas-Nebraska Act]] of 1854 reopened the debate over slavery's expansion, leading to "Bleeding Kansas" and the eventual [[Civil War]].
