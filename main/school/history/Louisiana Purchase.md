# Louisiana Purchase

> [!info] Louisiana Purchase
> **Overview**
>
> **Topic**
> U.S. acquisition of the Louisiana Territory from France
>
> **Date**
> 1803
>
> **President**
> [[Thomas Jefferson]]

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Year:** 1803
> **From:** Napoleonic France
> **Price:** $15 million (~4 cents per acre)
> **Size:** ~828,000 square miles — doubled U.S. territory
> **Negotiated by:** Robert Livingston and James Monroe

---

The **Louisiana Purchase** (1803) was the acquisition of approximately **828,000 square miles** of territory from France, which doubled the size of the United States overnight. President **[[Thomas Jefferson]]** paid $15 million — roughly 4 cents per acre — in one of the greatest land deals in history.

---

## Why Napoleon Sold

> [!info] Napoleon's Reasoning
> Napoleon had planned to build a French empire in the Americas, but the **Haitian Revolution** destroyed his army in the Caribbean. Facing renewed war with Britain and unable to defend distant Louisiana, he offered the entire territory to the U.S. — surprising American negotiators who had only gone to buy New Orleans.

---

## Jefferson's Dilemma

> [!info] A Constitutional Problem
> Jefferson believed in **strict construction** of the Constitution — the federal government could only do what was explicitly authorized. The Constitution said nothing about buying foreign territory. Jefferson privately worried the purchase was unconstitutional, but concluded the opportunity was too important to pass up. He rationalized it and submitted it to the Senate.

---

## Significance

The Louisiana Purchase set the stage for westward expansion and [[Manifest Destiny]]. Jefferson immediately commissioned the **[[Lewis and Clark Expedition]]** to explore the new territory. The purchase also reignited debate over slavery's expansion — would slavery be permitted in the new territories? This question would dominate American politics for the next 60 years.
