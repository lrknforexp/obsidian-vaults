# James Monroe

## Overview

**James Monroe** (1758–1831) was the **5th President of the United States** (1817–1825), serving during the "**Era of Good Feelings**" — a period of relative political harmony following the collapse of the [[Federalist Party]]. He is best known for the **[[Monroe Doctrine]]**, a cornerstone of American foreign policy.

## Early Career

Monroe fought in the [[American Revolution]] (he was wounded at the Battle of Trenton), served as a diplomat, U.S. Senator, Governor of Virginia, and Secretary of State and Secretary of War under [[James Madison]].

## Era of Good Feelings

Monroe ran nearly unopposed in 1816 (winning 183 of 217 electoral votes) and was reelected in 1820 with all but one electoral vote — the only president after Washington to run essentially without opposition. His presidency saw relative political consensus as the [[Federalist Party]] dissolved.

## Monroe Doctrine (1823)

Monroe's most lasting contribution was the Monroe Doctrine, which declared the Western Hemisphere closed to future European colonization and warned European powers not to interfere in the affairs of independent nations in the Americas. Though the U.S. lacked the military power to enforce it at the time, the doctrine became a pillar of American foreign policy.
