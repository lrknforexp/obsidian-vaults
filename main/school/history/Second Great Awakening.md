# Second Great Awakening

## Overview

The **Second Great Awakening** (c. 1790–1840) was a Protestant religious revival movement that transformed American religion and society. Emphasizing emotional conversion experiences, free will, and human ability to achieve salvation, it democratized American Christianity and spawned a generation of reform movements including abolitionism, temperance, and women's rights.

## Character

The movement featured dramatic **camp meetings** — outdoor revival gatherings lasting days — where preachers like **Charles Grandison Finney** spoke to thousands. Finney's "new measures" (anxious benches, female prayer, protracted meetings) brought revivalism into cities and made conversion a community event.

## Social Reform

Awakening participants believed Christians must reform society to prepare for Christ's return. This produced: the **abolitionist movement** (William Lloyd Garrison, the American Anti-Slavery Society), the **temperance movement** (American Temperance Society, 1826), **prison reform** (Dorothea Dix), and **women's rights** (leading to the Seneca Falls Convention, 1848).

## New Religions

The religious ferment also produced new American religious movements: **Mormonism** (Joseph Smith, 1830), **Adventism**, and various utopian communities.
