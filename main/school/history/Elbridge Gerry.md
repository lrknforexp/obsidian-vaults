**Elbridge Gerry** was a Massachusetts Founding Father, signer of the [[Declaration of Independence]], and the 5th Vice President of the United States. He is most commonly remembered today for the **"gerrymander"** — the political term for manipulating electoral district boundaries, named after him — and for his controversial role in the **[[XYZ Affair]]** as one of three American envoys sent to France in 1797.

---

## Early Life and the Revolution

Gerry was a prosperous merchant from Marblehead, Massachusetts. He became an early and passionate supporter of American independence, serving in the [[Second Continental Congress]] and signing the [[Declaration of Independence]] in 1776.

Despite signing the Declaration, Gerry was one of three delegates at the **[[Constitutional Convention]]** who **refused to sign the Constitution** — he objected that it lacked a Bill of Rights and that the document gave the federal government too much power. His objections contributed to the push for what became the **[[Bill of Rights]]**.

---

## The XYZ Affair (1797–1798)

President [[John Adams]] sent Gerry, [[Charles Cotesworth Pinckney]], and [[John Marshall]] to France to negotiate a settlement to the naval crisis that preceded the [[Quasi-War]].

> [!info] Gerry's Controversial Decision
> When French agents (the "X, Y, Z" figures) demanded bribes and Pinckney and Marshall left Paris in disgust, Gerry stayed behind — hoping to maintain some diplomatic channel and prevent outright war. His decision was deeply controversial at home: Federalists accused him of being naive at best and pro-French at worst.
>
> Adams later said Gerry's decision to stay was an act of genuine patriotism, though politically damaging.

---

## The Gerrymander

Gerry's most lasting (if dubious) legacy is the **gerrymander** — the practice of drawing electoral district boundaries in bizarre shapes to favor one political party.

> [!info] Origin of the Term
> In 1812, as Governor of Massachusetts, Gerry signed into law a redistricting plan that created a strangely shaped state senate district designed to favor Democratic-Republicans. A Boston newspaper published a cartoon depicting the district as resembling a salamander and labeled it a **"Gerry-mander"** — a portmanteau of his name and "salamander."
>
> The term has been used ever since to describe politically motivated redistricting, regardless of party.

---

## Vice Presidency and Death

Gerry was elected Vice President under [[James Madison]] in 1812. He died in office on November 23, 1814 — only the second Vice President to die in office.
