# Pontiac's Rebellion

## Overview

**Pontiac's Rebellion** (1763–1766) was a widespread Native American uprising against British rule following the **[[French and Indian War]]**. Led initially by **Pontiac**, an Ottawa war chief, warriors from dozens of tribes attacked British forts and settlements across the Great Lakes and Ohio Valley regions.

## Background

After Britain's victory in the French and Indian War, it took over former French territories. Unlike the French, who had maintained alliances with Native peoples through trade and respect, the British cut off gift-giving, raised prices for trade goods, and showed contempt for Native sovereignty. Native peoples also faced increasing colonial encroachment on their lands.

## The Rebellion

In 1763, Pontiac organized a confederacy of tribes that simultaneously attacked British forts across the frontier. Warriors captured eight forts, besieged Detroit, and killed hundreds of settlers. British soldiers sometimes responded with **biological warfare**, deliberately distributing smallpox-infected blankets to Native communities.

## Proclamation of 1763

The British response included the **Proclamation of 1763**, which attempted to limit colonial settlement west of the Appalachian Mountains to reduce frontier conflict. Colonists resented this restriction, viewing it as British interference with westward expansion — adding to revolutionary grievances.
