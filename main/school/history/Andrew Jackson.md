**Andrew Jackson** (1767–1845) was the **7th President of the United States** (1829–1837), the founder of the modern **[[Democratic Party]]**, and the defining political figure of the "Age of Jackson." A self-made frontier hero, he championed the "common man" against Eastern elites — while simultaneously overseeing the forced removal of Native Americans.

---

## Rise to Power

> [!info] From Frontier Fighter to President
> Jackson rose to national fame through military victories, especially at the **Battle of New Orleans** (1815) during the [[War of 1812]]. He ran for president in 1824, winning the popular vote but losing in the House to [[John Quincy Adams]] in the so-called **"Corrupt Bargain."** He won decisively in 1828, bringing a new coalition of Western farmers and Southern planters to power.

---

## Major Policies

> [!info] Jackson's Key Actions as President
> - **[[Indian Removal Act]] (1830)** — forced removal of Native Americans east of the Mississippi; led to the **[[Trail of Tears]]**
> - **Bank War** — vetoed recharter of the **[[National Bank]]** (1832); destroyed it by withdrawing federal deposits
> - **Nullification Crisis** — threatened military force against South Carolina when it tried to nullify federal tariffs
> - **Spoils System** — rewarded political supporters with government jobs

---

## Legacy

> [!info] Contradictions of Jacksonian Democracy
> Jackson expanded democracy for white men while brutally restricting it for Native Americans and enslaved people. He is celebrated as a champion of the common man and condemned as the architect of ethnic cleansing. His presidency fundamentally transformed American politics, creating the Second Party System of Democrats vs. Whigs.
