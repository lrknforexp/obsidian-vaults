# Washington, D.C.

## Overview

**Washington, D.C.** (the District of Columbia) is the capital of the United States, designed as a purpose-built federal city after the nation's founders decided the capital should not be located in any existing state. It was established by the **Residence Act of 1790**, with its location along the **Potomac River** chosen as a compromise between Northern and Southern states.

## The Compromise of 1790

**[[Alexander Hamilton]]** and **[[Thomas Jefferson]]** struck a famous deal: the federal government would assume the states' Revolutionary War debts (Hamilton's priority) in exchange for locating the capital in the South along the Potomac (Jefferson's priority). This "Compromise of 1790" shaped both American finance and geography.

## Design and Construction

President **[[George Washington]]** selected the site and oversaw initial planning. French-American architect Pierre Charles L'Enfant designed the grand street plan. The Capitol and President's House (White House) were under construction when the government moved from Philadelphia in 1800.

## Burning of Washington (1814)

During the **[[War of 1812]]**, British forces captured and burned Washington in August 1814, torching the Capitol, White House, and other federal buildings. It remains the only time since the Revolution that a foreign power has captured and occupied the U.S. capital.
