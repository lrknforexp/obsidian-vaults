# Second Continental Congress

## Overview

The **Second Continental Congress** convened in Philadelphia on **May 10, 1775** — just weeks after the battles of Lexington and Concord — and served as the de facto national government of the United States throughout the [[American Revolution]]. It issued the **[[Declaration of Independence]]**, created the Continental Army, appointed **[[George Washington]]** as its commander, and managed the war effort.

## Key Actions

The Congress voted to raise the **Continental Army** in June 1775 and unanimously chose Washington to command it. It managed diplomacy (pursuing the **[[Franco-American Alliance]]**), issued **Continental currency**, and drafted the **[[Articles of Confederation]]** (ratified 1781), which served as the first American constitution.

## Declaration of Independence

On July 2, 1776, the Congress voted for independence. On July 4, it formally adopted the **[[Declaration of Independence]]**, drafted primarily by **[[Thomas Jefferson]]**. This was the defining act of the Revolution.

## Challenges

The Congress operated without formal taxing authority, relying on requisitions from states that were often ignored. It struggled constantly with money, supplies, and maintaining the army. These weaknesses led directly to the Constitutional Convention of 1787.
