# Jonathan Edwards

## Overview

**Jonathan Edwards** (1703–1758) was the most important American theologian and preacher of the **[[First Great Awakening]]** and one of the greatest intellectuals in American history. His 1741 sermon **"[[Sinners in the Hands of an Angry God]]"** is the most famous sermon in American history.

## Background

Edwards was a child prodigy who entered Yale at age 13. He became pastor of the Congregational church in Northampton, Massachusetts, where he sparked a local revival in 1734–1735 that prefigured the broader Great Awakening.

## Theological Thought

Edwards combined Calvinist theology (human sinfulness, God's sovereignty, predestination) with Enlightenment philosophy and introspective psychology. He argued that genuine religious conversion involved a deep emotional and spiritual transformation, not just intellectual assent to doctrine.

## "Sinners in the Hands of an Angry God"

Delivered in 1741 at Enfield, Connecticut, this sermon depicted God holding sinners over the pit of hell like a spider over a flame. Edwards reportedly delivered it calmly, yet accounts describe the congregation crying out in fear. It became the defining text of revivalist preaching.

## Legacy

Edwards's theological writings influenced American Protestantism for centuries. His emphasis on emotional, experiential religion helped shape American evangelicalism and contributed to the democratic religious culture of the new nation.
