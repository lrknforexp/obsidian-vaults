**Benjamin Franklin** (1706–1790) was the most internationally famous American of the 18th century — a scientist, inventor, printer, philosopher, and diplomat. His success in securing the **[[Franco-American Alliance]]** (1778) was arguably the single most important diplomatic achievement of the [[American Revolution]].

---

## The French Alliance

> [!info] Franklin in Paris
> Franklin served as American minister to France (1778–1785), where his wit, fame, and charm made him enormously popular. He skillfully negotiated French military and financial support that proved decisive in winning American independence. After the victory at **Saratoga** (1777), he convinced France to formally enter the war.

---

## Scientific and Intellectual Contributions

Franklin's experiments proving lightning was electrical made him world-famous. He invented the lightning rod, bifocals, and the Franklin stove. His *Poor Richard's Almanack* shaped American popular culture and values.

---

## Constitutional Convention

> [!info] Elder Statesman
> At 81, Franklin was the oldest delegate to the Constitutional Convention (1787). Though too frail to speak much, his presence lent prestige to the proceedings. His closing speech urged all delegates to sign despite their reservations, arguing the Constitution was as perfect as human institutions could be.
