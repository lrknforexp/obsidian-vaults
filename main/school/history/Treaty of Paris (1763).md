# Treaty of Paris (1763)

## Overview

The **Treaty of Paris (1763)** ended the **[[Seven Years' War]]** (including its American theater, the **[[French and Indian War]]**) and dramatically reshaped the political map of North America. Britain gained Canada and all French territory east of the Mississippi River, while Spain received Louisiana (French territory west of the Mississippi).

## Key Terms

France ceded to Britain: **Canada**, all territory east of the Mississippi River, and several Caribbean islands. France ceded to Spain (its ally): **Louisiana** — the vast territory west of the Mississippi. Spain ceded **Florida** to Britain (in exchange for getting Havana, Cuba back). France was essentially expelled from continental North America.

## Impact on Native Americans

The removal of France — which had maintained trade relationships and alliances with Native peoples — left many tribes without a powerful European ally to balance British power. This contributed to **[[Pontiac's Rebellion]]** (1763).

## Seeds of Revolution

Britain's victory came at enormous financial cost. To pay war debts and fund colonial defense, Parliament began taxing the colonies — first the **[[Stamp Act]]** (1765), then the **[[Townshend Acts]]** (1767). Colonial resistance to these taxes led directly to the [[American Revolution]].
