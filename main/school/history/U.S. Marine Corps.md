# U.S. Marine Corps

## Overview

The **United States Marine Corps** was established by Congress on July 11, 1798, alongside the **[[Department of the Navy]]**, during the **[[Quasi-War]]** with France. Marines served as shipboard soldiers — maintaining discipline aboard naval vessels, leading amphibious landings, and engaging in close combat.

## Early History

Marines served in the **[[Quasi-War]]** and were immortalized in the **[[War of 1812]]**. Their most famous early action was the assault on **Derna** in Tripoli (1805) during the First Barbary War — commemorated in the Marine Corps Hymn line "to the shores of Tripoli."

## Role and Mission

Marines were designed to be adaptable soldiers who could fight on land and sea. They protected naval officers from mutiny, served as sharpshooters in naval battles, and led shore landings. This dual land-sea capability made them distinct from the Army.

## Significance in Early American History

The creation of the Marines and the Navy reflected the young republic's recognition that it needed military power to protect its commercial interests globally — a significant departure from the Founders' suspicion of standing armies.
