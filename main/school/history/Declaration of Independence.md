The **Declaration of Independence**, adopted on **July 4, 1776**, is the founding document of the United States. Drafted primarily by **[[Thomas Jefferson]]** and adopted by the **[[Second Continental Congress]]**, it announced the colonies' separation from Britain and articulated the philosophical principles underlying American self-governance.

---

## Key Ideas

> [!info] The Philosophy of the Declaration
> Drawing on Enlightenment thinker John Locke, Jefferson declared:
> - All men are **created equal** and endowed with unalienable rights
> - These rights include **life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness**
> - Governments derive their **just powers from the consent of the governed**
> - When a government becomes destructive of these ends, the people have the **right to alter or abolish it**

---

## Structure

The Declaration has three main parts: a philosophical preamble stating natural rights principles, a list of **27 grievances** against King George III justifying revolution, and a formal declaration of independence.

---

## Contradictions and Legacy

> [!info] "All Men Are Created Equal"
> The Declaration's promise of equality stood in sharp contradiction to the reality of slavery. Jefferson himself enslaved over 600 people during his lifetime (see [[Sally Hemings]]). The tension between the Declaration's ideals and American practice drove reform movements for centuries — from abolition to women's suffrage to civil rights.
