# Talleyrand

## Overview

**Charles-Maurice de Talleyrand-Périgord** (1754–1838) was one of the most influential and cunning diplomats in European history. He appears in American history primarily through the **[[Quasi-War|XYZ Affair]]** (1797–1798), in which his agents demanded bribes from American diplomats, nearly triggering a full-scale war between France and the United States.

## Role in the XYZ Affair

As French Foreign Minister under the Directory, Talleyrand refused to receive American diplomats **[[Charles Cotesworth Pinckney]]**, John Marshall, and **[[Elbridge Gerry]]** officially. Instead, his agents (code-named X, Y, and Z) demanded a substantial bribe ($250,000) and a loan to France as the price of negotiations. The Americans refused, and Pinckney's reported response — "Millions for defense, but not one cent for tribute!" — became a patriotic rallying cry.

## Broader Career

Talleyrand served as a diplomat under the ancien régime, the Revolution, Napoleon, the Restoration, and the July Monarchy — surviving every regime change through brilliant adaptability. He negotiated the boundaries of post-Napoleonic Europe at the **Congress of Vienna** (1815).

## Connection to Louisiana Purchase

Talleyrand played a role in Napoleon's decision to sell the Louisiana Territory to the United States in 1803, recognizing that France could not defend it and that the sale would prevent Britain from seizing it.
