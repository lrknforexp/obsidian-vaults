---

The **Lewis and Clark Expedition** (1804–1806), commissioned by President **[[Thomas Jefferson]]** after the [[Louisiana Purchase]], was the first American overland journey to the Pacific Ocean. Led by Meriwether Lewis and William Clark, the Corps of Discovery mapped the territory, catalogued its plants and animals, and established American claims to the Pacific Northwest.

---

## Jefferson's Goals

> [!info] Why Jefferson Sent Them
> Jefferson had multiple objectives for the expedition:
> - **Map** the newly purchased Louisiana Territory
> - Find a water route to the **Pacific** (the fabled Northwest Passage)
> - Establish **American presence** in the fur trade
> - Catalog the **plants, animals, and geography** of the West
> - Make **contact** with Native American peoples and assess trade possibilities

---

## Sacagawea's Role

> [!info] The Essential Guide
> **[[Sacagawea]]**, a Shoshone woman traveling with her French-Canadian husband Toussaint Charbonneau, joined the expedition in 1804. Her knowledge of Western geography and languages, her ability to communicate with the **[[Shoshone]]** (whose horses the expedition desperately needed), and her presence (which signaled peaceful intentions) were all crucial to the expedition's success.

---

## Significance

The expedition proved that an overland route to the Pacific existed, provided invaluable geographic and scientific knowledge, and reinforced American claims to the Oregon Territory. It became a symbol of [[Manifest Destiny]] and westward expansion.
