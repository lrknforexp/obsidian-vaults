**John Jay** was a New York lawyer, diplomat, and statesman who served as the **first Chief Justice of the United States** (1789–1795), co-authored the **[[Federalist Papers]]**, helped negotiate the **[[Treaty of Paris (1783)]]** ending the [[American Revolution]], and negotiated **[[Jay's Treaty]]** with Britain in 1794.

---

## Early Career and the Revolution

Jay was a prominent New York lawyer and a delegate to both the First and [[Second Continental Congress]]. Despite initially opposing independence (he favored reconciliation with Britain), he became a committed Patriot once the die was cast and served as President of the Continental Congress (1778–1779) and diplomat to Spain.

---

## The Federalist Papers

Jay was one of three authors — with [[Alexander Hamilton]] and [[James Madison]] — of the **[[Federalist Papers]]** (1787–1788). He wrote **5 of the 85 essays** (Federalist Nos. 2–5 and 64), focusing primarily on foreign policy and the dangers of the country remaining fragmented under the [[Articles of Confederation]].

> [!info] Federalist No. 2
> Jay argued powerfully that the thirteen states were naturally one nation — sharing language, culture, and history — and that dividing into competing republics would make all of them weaker and more vulnerable to foreign threats.

---

## First Chief Justice (1789–1795)

Jay was appointed the first Chief Justice of the United States by President [[George Washington]]. The early Supreme Court had relatively little power and prestige — it decided few cases of significance during Jay's tenure. He resigned to become Governor of New York after negotiating Jay's Treaty.

---

## Jay's Treaty (1794)

> [!info] Controversial Diplomacy
> Washington sent Jay to London to resolve lingering conflicts with Britain — frontier forts, seized ships, and impressment of American sailors. The resulting **[[Jay's Treaty]]** was bitterly controversial:
> - Britain agreed to evacuate frontier forts and pay compensation for seized ships
> - Britain refused to stop impressing sailors or recognize American neutral trading rights
> - The [[Democratic-Republican Party]] was furious; Jay was burned in effigy across the country
>
> Washington pushed it through, establishing the president as chief foreign diplomat and avoiding a war America was not prepared to fight.
