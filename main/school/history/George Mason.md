# George Mason

## Overview

**George Mason** (1725–1792) was a Virginia planter and statesman who was one of the most important founders of the United States, though he is less famous than many of his contemporaries. He drafted the **Virginia Declaration of Rights** (1776), which became a model for the **[[Declaration of Independence]]** and the **[[Bill of Rights]]**, yet he refused to sign the Constitution because it lacked a bill of rights and failed to end the slave trade.

## Virginia Declaration of Rights

Mason's Virginia Declaration of Rights (1776) declared that "all men are by nature equally free and independent" and enumerated fundamental rights including freedom of the press and free exercise of religion. **[[Thomas Jefferson]]** drew heavily on it in drafting the Declaration of Independence.

## Constitutional Convention

Mason was a delegate to the Constitutional Convention of 1787 but refused to sign the final document, citing two major objections: the absence of a **[[Bill of Rights]]** and the compromise allowing the slave trade to continue until 1808. He argued that a strong federal government without explicit protections for individual rights would become tyrannical.

## Legacy

Mason's insistence on a bill of rights, combined with **[[Patrick Henry]]'s** anti-federalist campaigning, made including a bill of rights politically necessary. **[[James Madison]]** drafted and secured passage of the first ten amendments — the **[[Bill of Rights]]** — in 1791.
