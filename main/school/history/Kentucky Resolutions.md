# Kentucky Resolutions

## Overview

The **Kentucky Resolutions** (1798 and 1799) were drafted by **[[Thomas Jefferson]]** and passed by the Kentucky legislature in response to the **[[Alien and Sedition Acts]]**. They argued that the Constitution was a compact between sovereign states, and that states had the power to **nullify** federal laws they deemed unconstitutional.

## Key Arguments

Jefferson argued more forcefully than Madison's [[Virginia Resolutions]] that individual states, as parties to the constitutional compact, retained the right to judge acts of Congress — and to declare them "void and of no force" within their borders.

## Compact Theory

The resolutions articulated **compact theory**: the Constitution was an agreement among the states (not the people directly), and states retained their sovereignty. If the federal government violated the compact, states could refuse to enforce unconstitutional acts.

## Nullification and Legacy

Jefferson's Kentucky Resolutions became the theoretical foundation for **nullification** — the idea that states could void federal laws. South Carolina invoked this doctrine in the **Nullification Crisis of 1832** over tariffs. Jefferson himself may not have intended such a sweeping application.
