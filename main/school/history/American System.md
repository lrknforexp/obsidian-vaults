# American System

> [!info] American System
> **Overview**
>

> **Era**
> Early 19th century

> [!info] Quick Facts

---

The **American System** was an economic program championed by Senator **[[Henry Clay]]** of Kentucky in the early 19th century. It called for high **protective tariffs** to protect American manufacturing, a **[[National Bank]]** to provide stable currency and credit, and federal funding for **internal improvements** (roads, canals, infrastructure) to unite the national economy.

---

## The Three Pillars

> [!info] How It Would Work
> **1. Protective Tariffs**
> High import taxes would shield American manufacturers from cheaper British goods, encouraging domestic industry to grow. The Tariff of 1816 was an early example.
>
> **2. National Bank**
> A central bank like the [[National Bank|Second Bank of the United States]] would regulate currency, provide loans, and stabilize the economy.
>
> **3. Internal Improvements**
> Federal investment in roads, canals like the [[Erie Canal]], and later railroads would connect western farmers to eastern markets, binding the nation economically.

---

## Opposition

[[Andrew Jackson]] and Southern Democrats opposed the American System vigorously. Southerners saw protective tariffs as benefiting Northern manufacturers at their expense — culminating in the "Tariff of Abominations" (1828). Jackson vetoed the recharter of the National Bank in 1832 and opposed federal spending on internal improvements.

---

## Legacy

> [!info] Long-Term Impact
> Though never fully implemented as Clay envisioned, the American System shaped economic debate for decades. It prefigured the Republican Party's economic platform after 1860 and influenced the industrial development of the United States throughout the 19th century.
