> [!info] Definition
> **Mercantilism** is the economic theory — dominant in Europe from roughly 1500 to 1800 — that a nation's wealth and power depend on **accumulating gold and silver**, and that all nations compete in a zero-sum race for these resources. A country must export more than it imports (**trade surplus**) and be as economically **self-sufficient** as possible.

---

Mercantilism was the economic philosophy that drove European colonization of the Americas. Britain, France, Spain, and the Netherlands established colonies not as charitable ventures but as **economic engines** designed to enrich the mother country.

---

## How It Applied to American Colonies

> [!info] Britain's Mercantilist Colonial System
> Under mercantilist logic, Britain's American colonies existed to:
> - Provide **raw materials** (tobacco, timber, furs, cotton) cheaply — saving Britain from buying them abroad
> - Serve as a **captive market** for British manufactured goods
> - Generate **trade surplus** for Britain — colonists bought British goods; Britain sold to the world
>
> The **[[Navigation Acts]]** (1651–1696) were the legal mechanism enforcing this arrangement — restricting whom colonists could trade with and requiring that goods travel on British ships.

---

## Weaknesses of Mercantilism

- It treated colonies as tools for the mother country rather than as societies in their own right — a key driver of colonial resentment
- It assumed trade was **zero-sum** (one nation's gain is another's loss) — later economists like Adam Smith (*Wealth of Nations*, 1776) demonstrated that free trade creates wealth for all parties
- It led to the colonial policies that triggered the [[American Revolution]]
