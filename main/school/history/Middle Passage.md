---

The **Middle Passage** was the forced transatlantic voyage endured by enslaved Africans as the second leg of the **[[Triangular Trade]]** — the journey from West Africa to the Americas. It was one of the most horrific chapters in human history, characterized by extreme cruelty, deliberate dehumanization, and staggeringly high death rates. Over roughly 350 years, an estimated **12 million enslaved people** were transported across the Atlantic, of whom approximately 1.5–2 million died during the crossing.

---

## The Triangular Trade

> [!info] Three-Leg System
> The Middle Passage was the second leg of the [[Triangular Trade]]:
> 1. **Europe → Africa**: European ships carried manufactured goods (guns, textiles, rum, iron) to West Africa, trading them for enslaved people
> 2. **Africa → Americas** *(the Middle Passage)*: Enslaved people were transported across the Atlantic
> 3. **Americas → Europe**: Ships returned carrying raw materials produced by enslaved labor — sugar, tobacco, cotton, rice

---

## Conditions Aboard Ship

> [!info] The Hold
> Enslaved people were loaded into the ship's hold — the interior cargo space — with almost no room to move. Two main loading methods were used:
>
> - **Tight packing** — as many people as possible were crammed in, maximizing the "cargo" per voyage
> - **Loose packing** — fewer people were loaded on the theory that healthier arrivals would sell for more
>
> Most slave traders chose tight packing. People were chained together in pairs, lying on wooden shelves with roughly 18 inches of headroom — unable to sit up, turn over, or move.

| Condition | Details |
|---|---|
| **Space** | Roughly 18 inches per person — less than a coffin |
| **Restraints** | Chained wrist-to-wrist and ankle-to-ankle |
| **Food and water** | Minimal — twice daily rations, often insufficient |
| **Sanitation** | None — people could not reach toilet buckets; disease spread rapidly |
| **Air** | Below-deck air was suffocating, especially in hot weather |
| **Violence** | Crew used whips, thumbscrews, and other torture to maintain control |
| **Duration** | 6–10 weeks across the Atlantic |

## Death and Disease

The mortality rate during the Middle Passage was catastrophic:

> [!info] Causes of Death
> - **Dysentery** ("the bloody flux") was the single greatest killer — spread rapidly in the filthy, overcrowded conditions
> - **Smallpox**, measles, and other diseases swept through the hold
> - **Dehydration** and starvation
> - **Suicide** — some enslaved people threw themselves overboard or refused to eat
> - **Violence** — those who resisted were brutally punished, sometimes killed as an example to others

The crossing killed roughly **1 in 7** of those who boarded ships.

---

## Resistance

Despite horrific conditions, enslaved Africans resisted:

- **Revolts** — shipboard uprisings were relatively rare but did occur. The most famous was the **Amistad** revolt (1839), in which enslaved people seized control of the ship
- **Suicide** — some enslaved people threw themselves overboard rather than accept enslavement
- **Hunger strikes** — refusing to eat was a form of resistance, though slavers used force-feeding devices to prevent starvation

---

## Arrival and Sale

Those who survived were sold at auction in American ports. Enslaved people were examined, prodded, made to exercise to prove fitness, and sold individually or in lots — families were routinely separated. The trauma of the Middle Passage was followed immediately by the additional trauma of sale, separation, and forced labor.

---

## Legacy

The Middle Passage is recognized as one of history's greatest atrocities. Its legacy shaped:
- The demographics of the Americas — the African diaspora
- The economic foundations of American (especially Southern) prosperity
- The centuries-long struggle for Black freedom and equality in America
- The ongoing debates about reparations for slavery
