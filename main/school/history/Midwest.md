# Midwest

## Overview

The **Midwest** (also called the Middle West or the heartland) is the region of the United States comprising the states north of the Ohio River and east of the Great Plains — including Ohio, Indiana, Illinois, Michigan, Wisconsin, Minnesota, Iowa, Missouri, Kansas, Nebraska, and the Dakotas. In the 19th century, the Midwest was transformed from Native American and frontier territory to the agricultural and industrial core of the United States.

## Northwest Ordinance

The **Northwest Ordinance** (1787) established the framework for organizing the original Midwest (Ohio, Indiana, Illinois, Michigan, Wisconsin) into territories and eventually states. Crucially, it banned slavery in the Northwest Territory.

## Settlement and Agriculture

After the [[War of 1812]] removed the major threat of Native American resistance (aided by the British), settlers poured into the Midwest. The region's fertile soil, combined with improved transportation (**[[Erie Canal]]**, **[[Railroad|railroads]]**), made it the breadbasket of America — producing corn, wheat, and eventually hogs and cattle.

## Industrial Growth

Chicago, Cincinnati, Detroit, and other Midwestern cities became manufacturing centers in the mid-19th century, driven by proximity to raw materials (iron ore, coal) and agricultural markets.

## Political Significance

The Midwest was the key swing region of 19th-century American politics, its votes often decisive in presidential elections.
