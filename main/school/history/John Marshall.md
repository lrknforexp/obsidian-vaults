---

**John Marshall** (1755–1835) was the **Chief Justice of the United States** from 1801 to 1835 and the most important figure in the history of the Supreme Court. Through a series of landmark decisions, he established the Court as a co-equal branch of government and dramatically expanded federal power.

---

## Landmark Decisions

> [!info] Marshall's Greatest Cases
> **[[Marbury v. Madison]] (1803)** — established [[Judicial Review]]; Supreme Court can void unconstitutional laws
>
> **[[McCulloch v. Maryland]] (1819)** — upheld the constitutionality of the [[National Bank]]; established doctrine of **implied powers**; federal law is supreme over state law
>
> ***Gibbons v. Ogden* (1824)** — broadly interpreted Congress's power to regulate interstate commerce
>
> ***Worcester v. Georgia* (1832)** — ruled Georgia had no authority over Cherokee territory (Jackson ignored it)

---

## Marshall's Philosophy

> [!info] A Nationalist on the Court
> Marshall was an ardent **nationalist** appointed by Federalist President Adams. Despite serving under Democratic-Republican presidents who tried to limit federal power, he consistently ruled to **strengthen the federal government** and the Supreme Court. His decisions shaped American constitutional law for centuries.
