# PART 1: HEALTH 2

## 🧠 Mental & Emotional Health

### The Mental Health Continuum
Mental health is not binary (sick vs. healthy) — it exists on a **spectrum**:
- **Flourishing** — High well-being; engaged, functioning optimally
- **Languishing** — Low well-being without clinical disorder; emptiness, stagnation
- **Mental Illness** — Diagnosable disorder interfering with daily functioning

### The Dimensions of Health (Holistic Model)
1. **Physical** — Body function, fitness, nutrition, sleep, disease prevention
2. **Mental/Intellectual** — Thinking, problem-solving, learning, creativity
3. **Emotional** — Recognizing, managing, and expressing feelings
4. **Social** — Healthy relationships, communication, belonging
5. **Environmental** — Safe, clean, supportive surroundings
6. **Spiritual** — Sense of purpose, values, meaning (not necessarily religious)
7. **Occupational** — Satisfaction and meaning in work/school activities
8. **Financial** — Stable finances reduce stress and improve overall wellbeing

### Common Mental Health Disorders

> [!note] Important Terminology
> A **mental disorder** is a clinically significant disturbance in cognition, emotional regulation, or behavior. The *DSM-5* (Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, 5th edition) is the standard diagnostic reference used in the U.S.

| Disorder | Key Features | Prevalence |
|---|---|---|
| **Major Depressive Disorder (MDD)** | Persistent sadness, loss of interest, fatigue, hopelessness ≥ 2 weeks | ~7% of U.S. adults |
| **Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD)** | Excessive worry about multiple areas of life; difficult to control | ~3% of U.S. adults |
| **Panic Disorder** | Recurrent unexpected panic attacks; fear of future attacks | ~2–3% |
| **Bipolar Disorder** | Episodes of mania and depression alternating | ~2.8% |
| **Schizophrenia** | Hallucinations, delusions, disorganized thinking | ~1% |
| **ADHD** | Inattention, hyperactivity, impulsivity; begins in childhood | ~5% adults, ~10% children |
| **PTSD** | Flashbacks, nightmares, hyperarousal after traumatic event | ~3.6% per year |
| **OCD** | Unwanted obsessions and compulsive behaviors | ~1.2% |
| **Eating Disorders** | Anorexia, Bulimia, BED; distorted body image | Combined ~5–10% |

### Stress & the Stress Response

**Stress** — The body's physical and psychological response to demands (stressors)

**Types of Stress:**
- **Eustress** — Positive stress that motivates (excitement before a game, job interview)
- **Distress** — Negative stress that overwhelms and harms health
- **Acute stress** — Short-term, immediate response to a specific event
- **Chronic stress** — Prolonged, ongoing stress; most harmful to health

**The General Adaptation Syndrome (Hans Selye, 1936):**
1. **Alarm Stage** — "Fight or flight" response; adrenaline (epinephrine) released; cortisol rises; heart rate and blood pressure increase; pupils dilate
2. **Resistance Stage** — Body adapts and tries to cope with ongoing stressor; resources consumed
3. **Exhaustion Stage** — Body's resources depleted; vulnerability to illness; burnout, depression

**Stress Hormones:**
- **Cortisol** — Primary stress hormone; released by adrenal glands; raises blood sugar; suppresses immune system when chronically elevated
- **Epinephrine (adrenaline)** — Immediate fight-or-flight; increases heart rate and breathing
- **Norepinephrine** — Increases alertness and arousal

**Healthy Coping Strategies:**
- Physical exercise (lowers cortisol, releases endorphins)
- Mindfulness meditation and deep breathing
- Adequate sleep (7–9 hours for teens)
- Social support and connection
- Journaling; creative expression
- Time management and organization
- Therapy or counseling (CBT, DBT)

**Unhealthy Coping Strategies to Avoid:**
- Substance use (alcohol, drugs)
- Emotional eating or restriction
- Social isolation
- Aggression or self-harm
- Avoidance/procrastination

### Suicide Prevention

> [!danger] Warning Signs of Suicide Risk
> - Talking about wanting to die or having no reason to live
> - Giving away prized possessions
> - Sudden calmness after depression (may indicate a decision has been made)
> - Withdrawing from friends and family
> - Reckless behavior; saying "I won't be a problem much longer"
> - Researching methods

**QPR Model (Question, Persuade, Refer):**
1. **Question** — Ask directly: "Are you thinking about suicide?"
2. **Persuade** — Listen without judgment; express care and concern
3. **Refer** — Connect to professional help immediately

**Crisis Resources:**
- **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline** — Call or text 988
- **Crisis Text Line** — Text HOME to 741741
- **Emergency** — 911 if immediate danger

## 🍎 Nutrition Science

### Macronutrients

> [!info] The Three Macronutrients
> Nutrients needed in large amounts for energy and growth.

**Carbohydrates:**
- Primary energy source; 4 calories per gram
- **Simple carbs** — Sugars (glucose, fructose, sucrose); quick energy; spike blood sugar
- **Complex carbs** — Starches and fiber; slower digestion; more stable energy
- **Fiber** — Indigestible; promotes gut health; reduces cholesterol; regulates blood sugar
- Recommended: 45–65% of total calories; 25–38g fiber per day

**Proteins:**
- 4 calories per gram
- Made of amino acids; 20 total, 9 are **essential** (must be consumed in diet)
- **Complete proteins** — Contain all 9 essential amino acids; animal products, quinoa, soy
- **Incomplete proteins** — Missing one or more essential amino acids; most plant proteins; can be combined (rice + beans)
- Functions: build/repair muscle and tissue, enzymes, hormones, immune function, transport (hemoglobin)
- Recommended: 10–35% of calories; ~0.8g/kg body weight (more for athletes)

**Fats (Lipids):**
- 9 calories per gram (most calorie-dense)
- **Saturated fats** — Solid at room temperature; animal products; raises LDL cholesterol
- **Unsaturated fats** — Liquid at room temperature:
  - *Monounsaturated* — Olive oil, avocados; raises HDL ("good" cholesterol)
  - *Polyunsaturated* — Omega-3 (fish, flaxseed) and omega-6 (vegetable oils); essential fatty acids; anti-inflammatory
- **Trans fats** — Artificially hydrogenated; worst for heart health; largely banned in U.S.
- Functions: absorb fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K), cell membrane structure, hormone production, insulation
- Recommended: 20–35% of calories

### Micronutrients

**Vitamins:**

| Vitamin | Key Function | Deficiency |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Vision, immune function, skin | Night blindness, xerophthalmia |
| **B1 (Thiamine)** | Energy metabolism | Beriberi |
| **B3 (Niacin)** | Energy metabolism | Pellagra |
| **B9 (Folate)** | DNA synthesis, pregnancy | Neural tube defects (in infants) |
| **B12** | Nerve function, red blood cells | Pernicious anemia, nerve damage |
| **C** | Antioxidant, collagen synthesis, immune | Scurvy |
| **D** | Calcium absorption, bone health | Rickets (children), osteomalacia |
| **E** | Antioxidant, immune | Rare; nerve damage |
| **K** | Blood clotting, bone metabolism | Bleeding disorders |

**Fat-Soluble:** A, D, E, K — stored in fat tissue; can accumulate to toxic levels
**Water-Soluble:** B vitamins, C — not stored; excess excreted; need regular intake

**Key Minerals:**

| Mineral | Function | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| **Calcium** | Bone/teeth strength, muscle contraction | Dairy, leafy greens, fortified foods |
| **Iron** | Hemoglobin; oxygen transport | Red meat, beans, leafy greens |
| **Sodium** | Fluid balance, nerve signals | Salt; most processed foods |
| **Potassium** | Muscle/nerve function; counters sodium | Bananas, potatoes, avocados |
| **Magnesium** | 300+ enzyme reactions; muscle/nerve | Nuts, seeds, leafy greens |
| **Zinc** | Immune function, wound healing | Meat, shellfish, legumes |
| **Iodine** | Thyroid hormone production | Seafood, iodized salt |

### MyPlate (USDA Dietary Guidelines 2020–2025)
- **Grains** (make half whole grains) — 6–8 oz per day
- **Vegetables** (vary colors) — 2.5–3 cups per day
- **Fruits** — 1.5–2 cups per day
- **Protein** — 5–6.5 oz per day; lean meats, poultry, seafood, eggs, nuts, legumes
- **Dairy** — 3 cups per day; or fortified alternatives

### Energy Balance & Weight Management
- **Caloric balance** — Calories in vs. calories out
- **BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate)** — Calories burned at rest; affected by body size, age, sex, muscle mass
- **TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure)** = BMR × activity factor
- 1 pound of fat ≈ 3,500 calories
- **Healthy weight loss** — 1–2 lbs/week; 500–1,000 calorie daily deficit

## 💊 Substance Use & Prevention

### Classification of Drugs

| Category | Effect | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Depressants** | Slow CNS; reduce activity | Alcohol, benzodiazepines (Xanax), opioids, barbiturates |
| **Stimulants** | Speed up CNS; increase energy | Cocaine, methamphetamine, amphetamines (Adderall, Ritalin), nicotine, caffeine |
| **Hallucinogens** | Distort perceptions, hallucinations | LSD, psilocybin (mushrooms), PCP, mescaline, ketamine |
| **Cannabis** | Varies; THC affects CB1/CB2 receptors | Marijuana, hashish; can cause psychosis in heavy teen users |
| **Opioids** | Pain relief; euphoria; highly addictive | Heroin, fentanyl, oxycodone, morphine, codeine |
| **Inhalants** | Intoxication from inhaled vapors | Glue, paint thinner, nitrous oxide; very dangerous; hypoxia risk |

### Alcohol

> [!warning] Alcohol: The Most Commonly Used Drug Among Teens
> Ethanol (C₂H₅OH) is a legal depressant for adults 21+; illegal for minors; responsible for ~95,000 U.S. deaths per year.

**BAC (Blood Alcohol Content) Effects:**
| BAC | Effects |
|---|---|
| 0.02–0.05 | Lowered inhibitions, relaxation, warm feeling |
| 0.06–0.10 | Impaired judgment, slowed reaction time, poor coordination |
| 0.11–0.15 | Significant impairment, slurred speech, poor balance |
| 0.16–0.20 | Nausea, vomiting, blackouts possible |
| 0.25–0.30 | Stupor, choking on vomit risk, passing out |
| 0.30+ | Coma, respiratory arrest, death |

**Factors Affecting BAC:** Body weight, sex (women absorb more), food intake, rate of drinking, liver metabolism (about 1 standard drink per hour)

**Fetal Alcohol Spectrum Disorders (FASD):** Any alcohol during pregnancy can cause FASDs; leading preventable cause of intellectual disability

### Opioid Crisis
- Synthetic opioids (primarily fentanyl) now drive overdose deaths; fentanyl is 50–100x stronger than morphine
- **Naloxone (Narcan)** — Opioid antagonist; reverses overdose; available without prescription
- Over 80,000 opioid overdose deaths in the U.S. (2021)

### Addiction & the Brain
- **Dopamine reward pathway** — All addictive substances flood the nucleus accumbens with dopamine; eventually the brain downregulates dopamine receptors
- **Tolerance** — Need more drug to achieve same effect
- **Dependence** — Physical need for drug to function; withdrawal when stopped
- **Addiction** — Compulsive use despite negative consequences; brain disease affecting prefrontal cortex (impulse control)

### Nicotine & Tobacco
- Most common preventable cause of death; 480,000 U.S. deaths per year
- **Nicotine** — Highly addictive stimulant; adolescent brains especially vulnerable
- **Carcinogens in tobacco smoke** — Tar, benzene, formaldehyde, arsenic, carbon monoxide; cause lung cancer, COPD, heart disease
- **E-cigarettes/Vaping** — Contain nicotine and harmful aerosols; EVALI (e-cigarette/vaping product use-associated lung injury); especially dangerous for teens

## ❤️ Sexual & Reproductive Health

### Reproductive Anatomy
**Female:** Ovaries (produce eggs and hormones), fallopian tubes (egg transport), uterus (fetal development), cervix, vagina
**Male:** Testes (produce sperm and testosterone), epididymis (sperm maturation), vas deferens, seminal vesicles, prostate, urethra, penis

### The Menstrual Cycle (~28 days average)
1. **Menstrual Phase (Days 1–5)** — Uterine lining sheds
2. **Follicular Phase (Days 1–13)** — FSH stimulates follicle growth; estrogen rises; lining rebuilds
3. **Ovulation (Day 14)** — LH surge triggers egg release from ovary
4. **Luteal Phase (Days 15–28)** — Corpus luteum produces progesterone; prepares uterus for implantation; if no fertilization, cycle restarts

### Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs)

> [!warning] STI Prevention
> Abstinence is the only 100% effective prevention. Correctly used condoms significantly reduce (but don't eliminate) risk. Regular testing is essential for sexually active individuals.

| STI | Pathogen | Symptoms | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chlamydia** | Bacteria (*C. trachomatis*) | Often asymptomatic; discharge, burning | Antibiotics (curable) |
| **Gonorrhea** | Bacteria (*N. gonorrhoeae*) | Discharge, burning; can affect throat/rectum | Antibiotics (increasing resistance) |
| **Syphilis** | Bacteria (*T. pallidum*) | Stages: primary (painless sore), secondary (rash), latent, tertiary | Penicillin (curable) |
| **HIV/AIDS** | Virus (HIV) | Flu-like initially; AIDS when CD4 < 200 | Antiretrovirals (not curable; manageable) |
| **Herpes (HSV)** | Virus (HSV-1 or HSV-2) | Blisters/sores; can be asymptomatic; lifelong | Antivirals (not curable; suppressive therapy) |
| **HPV** | Virus (100+ strains) | Genital warts; cervical cancer | Vaccine (Gardasil 9); no cure |
| **Hepatitis B** | Virus (HBV) | Liver inflammation; can be chronic | Vaccine; antivirals |

**HIV/AIDS:**
- HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) attacks CD4+ T cells, weakening the immune system
- AIDS (Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome) = HIV infection + CD4 count < 200/mm³ or AIDS-defining illness
- Transmission: sexual contact, sharing needles, blood transfusion, mother-to-child (birth/breastfeeding)
- **PrEP (Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis)** — Daily medication that reduces HIV infection risk by ~99%
- **ART (Antiretroviral Therapy)** — U=U: Undetectable = Untransmittable (people with undetectable viral load cannot transmit HIV)

## 🦠 Disease Prevention

### Communicable vs. Non-Communicable Diseases

**Communicable (Infectious) Diseases** — Spread from person to person via:
- **Direct contact** — Touching, kissing, sexual contact
- **Indirect contact** — Touching contaminated surfaces (fomites)
- **Droplet transmission** — Respiratory droplets (coughing, sneezing)
- **Airborne transmission** — Tiny particles suspended in air (TB, measles)
- **Vector-borne** — Via insects/animals (mosquitoes: malaria, Zika; ticks: Lyme disease)
- **Foodborne/Waterborne** — Contaminated food or water

**Chain of Infection:**
1. Infectious agent (pathogen)
2. Reservoir (where it lives)
3. Portal of exit (how it leaves)
4. Mode of transmission
5. Portal of entry (how it enters new host)
6. Susceptible host

**Breaking the chain:** Handwashing, vaccines, antibiotics, isolation, sanitation

### The Immune System

**Innate Immunity (Non-specific):**
- First line: skin, mucous membranes, cilia, stomach acid
- Second line: inflammation, fever, natural killer cells, phagocytes (neutrophils, macrophages)

**Adaptive Immunity (Specific):**
- **B cells** — Produce antibodies specific to antigens
- **T cells:**
  - *Helper T cells (CD4+)* — Coordinate immune response; targeted by HIV
  - *Cytotoxic T cells (CD8+)* — Kill infected cells
  - *Memory T and B cells* — Long-term immunity; basis of vaccination

**Vaccines:**
- **Active immunity** — Stimulate immune system to produce its own antibodies
- Types: live-attenuated (MMR), inactivated (flu shot), mRNA (COVID-19), subunit (hepatitis B), toxoid (tetanus)
- **Herd immunity** — When enough people are immune, disease can't spread easily; threshold varies by disease (measles ~95%; polio ~80%)

### Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs)

**Cardiovascular Disease (CVD):**
- **Atherosclerosis** — Buildup of plaque (LDL cholesterol, calcium) in arteries; narrows/hardens arteries
- **Coronary artery disease** — Plaque in coronary arteries; leads to heart attack
- **Hypertension (High blood pressure)** — "Silent killer"; damages blood vessels and heart; normal < 120/80 mmHg
- **Stroke** — Blocked (ischemic) or burst (hemorrhagic) blood vessel in brain
- **Risk factors:** smoking, obesity, high cholesterol, hypertension, diabetes, physical inactivity, family history

**Type 2 Diabetes:**
- **Insulin resistance** — Cells fail to respond to insulin; pancreas produces more insulin until it can't keep up
- **Hyperglycemia** — High blood glucose; damages blood vessels, nerves, kidneys, eyes
- Risk factors: obesity, physical inactivity, diet high in refined carbs/sugars, family history
- **Type 1 Diabetes** — Autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells; insulin-dependent

**Cancer:**
- Uncontrolled cell growth and division; cells can invade tissues and spread (metastasis)
- **Oncogenes** — Mutated proto-oncogenes that accelerate cell division
- **Tumor suppressor genes** — When mutated, fail to stop uncontrolled growth
- **ABCDE Rule for Melanoma:** Asymmetry, Border, Color, Diameter (>6mm), Evolving
- **Common types:** Lung (most deadly), colorectal, breast, prostate, skin

## 🩺 First Aid & Emergency Response

### Primary Assessment (ABCDE)
1. **A**irway — Is it open? Head-tilt-chin-lift or jaw thrust
2. **B**reathing — Is the person breathing?
3. **C**irculation — Is there a pulse? Is there severe bleeding?
4. **D**isability — Level of consciousness? (AVPU: Alert, Voice, Pain, Unresponsive)
5. **E**xpose — Remove clothing to assess injuries

### CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation)

> [!important] Adult CPR Steps (AHA Guidelines)
> 1. Call 911 (or have someone else call)
> 2. Place heel of hand on center of chest (lower half of sternum)
> 3. **30 chest compressions** — Push hard and fast; at least 2 inches deep; rate 100–120/min
> 4. **2 rescue breaths** — Tilt head, lift chin; pinch nose; breathe 1 second each; watch for chest rise
> 5. Continue 30:2 ratio until AED arrives or patient recovers
> - **Hands-only CPR** — For bystanders without training; compressions only; still effective

**AED (Automated External Defibrillator):**
- Turn on → Follow voice prompts → Attach pads → Analyze rhythm → Deliver shock if advised → Resume CPR
- AEDs are designed for use by untrained bystanders; follow the prompts

### Choking (Heimlich Maneuver)

**Conscious adult:**
1. Stand behind person; wrap arms around waist
2. Make fist with one hand; place thumb side against abdomen (above navel, below breastbone)
3. Cover with other hand; deliver firm upward thrusts
4. Repeat until object is expelled or person becomes unconscious

**Infant (< 1 year):**
- 5 back blows + 5 chest thrusts; do NOT do abdominal thrusts

### Bleeding Control
1. Apply **direct pressure** with clean cloth; do not remove soaked cloth (add more on top)
2. Elevate the wound above heart if possible
3. **Tourniquet** — For life-threatening limb bleeding only; 2–3 inches above wound; tighten until bleeding stops; note time applied

### Burns
- **1st degree** — Epidermis only; red, dry, painful (sunburn); cool water, aloe
- **2nd degree** — Epidermis + part of dermis; blisters, very painful; cool water; do NOT pop blisters
- **3rd degree** — Full thickness; white/charred, may be painless (nerves destroyed); always 911; do not remove stuck clothing

### Shock (Hypovolemic)
Signs: pale/cool/clammy skin, rapid weak pulse, rapid breathing, confusion, nausea
Treatment: Call 911; lay flat; elevate legs 12 inches (if no spinal injury); keep warm; do not give food or water

## 🚦 Traffic Laws & Rules of the Road

### The New York Driver License System
- **Learner Permit (MJ)** — Minimum age 16; pass written test; must have licensed driver 21+ present
- **Junior License (DJ)** — Minimum age 16½; pass road test; restrictions on passengers and nighttime driving
- **Senior License** — Full unrestricted license at 17 (or 18 if no violations)

### Speed Limits (New York State Defaults)
| Location | Default Speed Limit |
|---|---|
| School zones | 15 mph when children present |
| Business/residential districts | 30 mph |
| Rural highways | 55 mph |
| Posted highways/interstates | As posted (typically 55–65 mph) |

### Right-of-Way Rules
1. **Yield sign** — Slow down and yield to traffic and pedestrians
2. **4-way stop** — First to arrive = first to go; ties: yield to vehicle on right
3. **Merging** — Vehicle already in lane has right of way
4. **Pedestrians** — Always have right of way in crosswalks
5. **Emergency vehicles** — Pull to right and stop; "Move over law" — change lanes when passing stopped emergency vehicles

### Traffic Signs by Shape & Color

**Shapes:**
| Shape | Meaning |

| Octagon | Stop |
| Triangle (inverted) | Yield |
| Diamond | Warning |
| Rectangle (vertical) | Regulatory (rules) |
| Rectangle (horizontal) | Guide/information |

**Colors:**
| Color | Meaning |

## 🛣️ Safe Driving Practices

### The SIPDE Process (Defensive Driving)
> **S**can → **I**dentify → **P**redict → **D**ecide → **E**xecute

1. **Scan** — Continuously search the road environment (ahead, sides, mirrors)
2. **Identify** — Recognize hazards and potential problems
3. **Predict** — Anticipate what might happen; what could go wrong?
4. **Decide** — Choose the best response (slow, stop, swerve, communicate)
5. **Execute** — Carry out your decision smoothly and in time

### Following Distance
- **3-Second Rule** — Choose a fixed object; when the car ahead passes it, count "one-thousand-one, one-thousand-two, one-thousand-three" before you reach it
- In poor conditions (rain, fog, ice): increase to **4–6 seconds**
- With large trucks: increase to **4+ seconds** (blind spots; longer stopping distance)

### Braking Distance vs. Stopping Distance

| Speed | Reaction Distance | Braking Distance | Total Stopping Distance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 mph | 22 ft | 20 ft | 42 ft |
| 30 mph | 33 ft | 45 ft | 78 ft |
| 40 mph | 44 ft | 80 ft | 124 ft |
| 55 mph | 60 ft | 150 ft | 210 ft |
| 65 mph | 71 ft | 210 ft | 281 ft |

*Reaction time average = 1.5 seconds; braking distance doubles with speed squared*

### Passing
1. Check that it's legal (no solid double yellow, no no-passing zones)
2. Check that it's safe (sight distance, traffic ahead)
3. Signal, check mirrors and blind spot
4. Accelerate to safely pass; do not linger alongside
5. Return to lane when car passed is visible in rearview mirror
6. Cancel turn signal

### Night Driving
- Use **headlights** from sunset to sunrise and whenever visibility < 1,000 feet
- **High beams** — Use on dark roads with no oncoming traffic; switch to low beams when oncoming vehicle is within 500 feet
- Reduced visibility + slower reaction time = need greater following distance
- Watch for deer and pedestrians

### Driving in Adverse Conditions

**Rain:**
- Reduce speed; increase following distance
- **Hydroplaning** — Tires lose contact with road; ease off gas; do not brake suddenly; steer straight

**Fog:**
- Use **low beams** (high beams reflect back); hazard lights if stopped
- Reduce speed dramatically; use road edge markings as guides

**Ice & Snow:**
- Accelerate slowly, brake slowly, steer gently
- **Black ice** — Thin, nearly invisible ice; bridges and overpasses freeze first
- If in a skid: **steer in the direction you want to go**; ease off gas; do not brake hard
- All-season tires lose effectiveness below 45°F; winter tires recommended

**Tire Blowout:**
- Grip steering wheel firmly; do NOT brake suddenly
- Ease off gas; maintain straight line
- Gradually slow using gentle braking
- Pull off road safely

## 🍺 Impaired Driving

### Alcohol & Driving

> [!danger] Drunk Driving
> Impaired driving is the #1 cause of death on American roads (~10,500 deaths/year). In New York, it is illegal to drive with a BAC of 0.08% or higher. For those under 21, the limit is **0.02%** (zero tolerance).

**Effects of Alcohol on Driving:**
- Slowed reaction time
- Impaired judgment and decision-making
- Reduced coordination and balance
- Narrowed field of vision
- Difficulty tracking moving objects
- Drowsiness

**Legal Consequences (New York):**
| Offense | BAC | Charge | Penalty |
|---|---|---|---|
| DWAI | 0.05–0.07% | Driving While Ability Impaired | Fine; possible license suspension |
| DWI | 0.08%+ | Driving While Intoxicated | Fine, jail, license revocation |
| Aggravated DWI | 0.18%+ | | Higher penalties |
| Zero Tolerance | 0.02–0.07% (under 21) | | License suspension |

### Drugs & Driving
- All psychoactive substances (cannabis, opioids, stimulants, sedatives) impair driving ability
- Cannabis impairs reaction time, tracking, divided attention, and lane keeping
- Drug testing: law enforcement can use drug recognition experts (DRE) and oral fluid tests
- **Drugged driving** is treated similarly to drunk driving under NY law

### Distracted Driving

> [!warning] The Three Types of Distraction
> 1. **Visual** — Eyes off the road
> 2. **Manual** — Hands off the wheel
> 3. **Cognitive** — Mind off the task of driving
> **Texting involves all three simultaneously**

- Sending/reading a text at 55 mph = driving the length of a football field blindfolded
- **New York Law** — Illegal to use handheld electronic devices while driving; $50–$450 fine; 5 points on license; under 18: automatic 120-day suspension

**Other Distractions:**
- Eating and drinking
- Applying makeup / grooming
- Adjusting radio or GPS
- Passengers (teen drivers with multiple teen passengers have dramatically higher crash risk)

### Drowsy Driving
- 100,000+ crashes per year attributed to drowsy driving (NHTSA)
- Microsleep — 4–5 second episodes of sleep; at 65 mph, covers ~100 yards
- Signs: yawning, heavy eyelids, drifting, missing exits
- Solution: Pull over and sleep; do not rely on caffeine alone for long drives

## 🚙 Vehicle Operation & Maintenance

### Pre-Drive Inspection (SMOG)
- **S**eatbelts — All passengers buckled
- **M**irrors — Adjust rearview and side mirrors
- **O**bstructions — Clear windows, check behind vehicle
- **G**auges — Gas, engine temperature, warning lights

### Warning Lights
| Light | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Check Engine | Engine/emissions system issue | Get diagnosed soon; if blinking, stop driving |
| Oil Pressure | Low/no oil pressure | Stop immediately; engine damage risk |
| Temperature | Engine overheating | Pull over; turn off engine |
| Battery | Charging system failure | Drive to mechanic soon |
| Tire Pressure (TPMS) | One or more tires low | Check and inflate tires |
| ABS | Anti-lock braking system issue | Brakes still work; get checked |
| Airbag | SRS (supplemental restraint) issue | Get checked; airbags may not deploy |

### Basic Maintenance
- **Oil change** — Every 5,000–7,500 miles (synthetic); check dipstick monthly
- **Tire pressure** — Check monthly; typical 32–35 PSI; affects fuel economy and handling
- **Tire tread** — Penny test: insert penny into tread; if you see Lincoln's head, replace tires (< 2/32")
- **Brake pads** — Replace when under 3mm; listen for squealing
- **Fluid checks** — Coolant, brake fluid, power steering, transmission, windshield washer
- **Wipers** — Replace every 6–12 months

### Parking Laws
- **No parking zones:** Within 15 feet of a fire hydrant; 20 feet of crosswalk; 30 feet of traffic signal; double-parking
- **Parallel parking:** Signal; check mirrors; reverse into space; align rear bumpers; angle back/in
- **Parking on a hill (no curb):** Turn wheels away from road; if facing uphill with curb, turn wheels toward curb

## 📝 Driver's Ed Exam Prep

> [!tip] Key Numbers to Memorize
> - DWI limit: **0.08% BAC** (adults); **0.02%** (under 21)
> - Headlights required when visibility < **1,000 feet**
> - Dim headlights for oncoming traffic within **500 feet**; when following within **200 feet**
> - Stop for school bus (flashing red) in **both directions** (unless divided highway)
> - Speed in school zone: **15 mph**
> - Minimum following distance: **3 seconds**
> - Points for speeding 1–10 mph over: **3 points**; 11–20 mph: **4 points**; 21–30 mph: **6 points**; 31–40 mph: **8 points**; 41+ mph: **11 points**
> - 11+ points in 18 months = license suspension
