# 🎨 IB Design Technology 1

> [!abstract] Course Overview
> IB Design Technology (DT) is an interdisciplinary course that bridges science, engineering, and creativity. It develops understanding of design principles, materials science, manufacturing processes, and the social and ethical impact of designed products. Students engage in the **design cycle** to develop innovative solutions to real-world problems. This course aligns with the IB learner profile and emphasizes global thinking, sustainability, and human-centered design.

---

## 🔄 The Design Cycle

> [!info] The IB Design Cycle (also: Design Process)
> A systematic, iterative process used to create solutions to problems. Not linear — designers constantly loop back through stages.

### Four Stages

```
INQUIRING & ANALYZING
        ↓
DEVELOPING IDEAS
        ↓
CREATING THE SOLUTION
        ↓
EVALUATING
        ↑_____(feedback loop back to any stage)
```

### Stage 1: Inquiring & Analyzing
- **Design Brief** — Clear statement of the design problem and context
- **Design Specification** — Detailed, measurable criteria the solution must meet
- **Research Plan** — How information will be gathered (primary/secondary research)
- **Stakeholder Analysis** — Identify who is affected by the design
- **Existing products analysis** — Evaluate similar products (success criteria, materials, method of production)
- **Market research** — Surveys, interviews, focus groups

### Stage 2: Developing Ideas
- **Brainstorming** — Generating many ideas without judgment
- **Mind mapping** — Visual idea organization
- **Morphological charts** — Matrix combining features from different categories
- **Sketching & Annotated drawings** — Quick idea visualization
- **Mood boards** — Visual aesthetic direction
- **Presentation drawings** — Isometric, orthographic, perspective sketches
- **Modeling** — Physical or digital prototypes (3D printing, CAD)
- **Design justification** — Explaining why each idea meets the specification

### Stage 3: Creating the Solution
- **Working drawings** — Technical, dimensioned drawings for manufacture
- **Bill of materials** — List of all components with specifications and quantities
- **Manufacturing plan** — Step-by-step production process
- **Quality control** — Checking against specification during production
- **Safety considerations** — Identify hazards and mitigation measures

### Stage 4: Evaluating
- **Testing** — Does the product meet each design specification criterion?
- **Client/user feedback** — Stakeholder evaluation
- **Reflection** — Strengths, weaknesses, improvements
- **Impact assessment** — Social, cultural, environmental, and economic impacts
- **Further development** — Suggested modifications and iterations

## 🪵 Materials Science

### Classification of Materials

#### Metals

> [!note] Ferrous vs. Non-Ferrous
> **Ferrous metals** contain iron; generally magnetic; prone to corrosion (rust)
> **Non-ferrous metals** do not contain iron; generally non-magnetic; better corrosion resistance

**Common Ferrous Metals:**
| Material | Composition | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mild Steel** | Fe + 0.1–0.3% C | Strong, ductile, weldable, cheap; rusts | Construction, car bodies, tools |
| **High Carbon Steel** | Fe + 0.6–1.4% C | Very hard, brittle, holds edge well | Cutting tools, springs, saw blades |
| **Stainless Steel** | Fe + Cr (18%) + Ni (8%) | Corrosion resistant, hard, lustrous | Cutlery, medical instruments, sinks |
| **Cast Iron** | Fe + 2–4% C + Si | Hard, brittle, compressive strength; cannot be bent | Engine blocks, pipes, cookware |

**Common Non-Ferrous Metals:**
| Material | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Aluminum (Al)** | Lightweight, good conductor, corrosion resistant, ductile; oxide layer protects | Aircraft, drink cans, window frames |
| **Copper (Cu)** | Excellent electrical/thermal conductor; malleable; expensive | Electrical wiring, plumbing, PCBs |
| **Titanium (Ti)** | Very strong, lightweight, corrosion resistant, biocompatible; expensive | Aerospace, medical implants, high-end bikes |
| **Gold (Au)** | Excellent conductor; doesn't tarnish/corrode; very expensive | Electronics contacts, jewelry |
| **Zinc (Zn)** | Low melting point; corrosion resistant | Galvanizing (coating steel), die casting |

**Alloys (Metal Mixtures):**
| Alloy | Base Metals | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Brass** | Cu + Zn | Corrosion resistant, machinable, aesthetic | Musical instruments, fittings, valves |
| **Bronze** | Cu + Sn | Hard, corrosion resistant, good bearing | Sculptures, bearings, ship propellers |
| **Duralumin** | Al + Cu + Mg + Mn | Light, strong (aircraft-grade aluminum) | Aircraft structures |
| **Solder** | Pb + Sn (or Sn + Ag + Cu lead-free) | Low melting point, good conductivity | Electronics soldering |

#### Polymers (Plastics)

> [!info] Polymer Structure
> Polymers are long chains of repeating monomer units; formed by polymerization

**Thermoplastics** — Soften when heated, harden when cooled; *recyclable*; can be reprocessed repeatedly
- Long chains; not cross-linked; weak intermolecular forces

| Thermoplastic | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Polyethylene (PE)** | Flexible, cheap, chemical resistant, light | Plastic bags, bottles, packaging |
| **Polypropylene (PP)** | Harder than PE; fatigue resistant; living hinges | Food containers, ropes, medical equipment |
| **PVC (Polyvinyl Chloride)** | Rigid or flexible; weather resistant; cheap | Pipes, window frames, electrical insulation, flooring |
| **Acrylic (PMMA)** | Transparent, brittle, good optical properties | Windows, signs, display stands |
| **Nylon (Polyamide, PA)** | Strong, wear resistant, self-lubricating | Gears, bearings, clothing, toothbrush bristles |
| **ABS (Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene)** | Tough, rigid, good surface finish; easy to machine | Lego, electronic housings, 3D printing, automotive parts |
| **Polystyrene (PS)** | Rigid, brittle; expanded form (EPS) is a great insulator | Packaging (EPS), CD cases, disposable cups |
| **PTFE (Teflon)** | Very low friction, chemical resistant, high temp | Non-stick coatings, plumbing tape, lab equipment |

**Thermosets** — Harden permanently when set; *not recyclable*; cross-linked polymer networks

| Thermoset | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Epoxy Resin** | Very strong adhesive; corrosion resistant | Adhesives, carbon fiber composites, boat hulls |
| **Polyester Resin** | Cheaper than epoxy; shrinks slightly | Fiberglass composites, surfboards |
| **Melamine Formaldehyde** | Hard, heat resistant, scratch resistant | Kitchen surfaces, crockery, laminates |
| **Urea Formaldehyde** | Hard, brittle, good insulator | Electrical fittings, adhesives in MDF |
| **Phenol Formaldehyde (Bakelite)** | Hard, heat resistant, electrical insulator | Early plastics; now electrical sockets |

**Elastomers (Rubbers):**
- Natural rubber — excellent elasticity; vulcanized (cross-linked with sulfur) for strength
- Silicone rubber — heat resistant, inert, food-safe; medical implants, baking molds

#### Timber (Wood)

**Hardwoods** — From deciduous (broad-leaf) trees; slower growing; generally denser
| Wood | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Oak** | Strong, durable, attractive grain | Furniture, flooring, wine barrels |
| **Mahogany** | Easy to work, stable, beautiful | Fine furniture, guitars, boat-building |
| **Teak** | Very durable, weather resistant, oily | Outdoor furniture, decking, boat decks |
| **Beech** | Hard, tough, close grain | Furniture, tool handles, school chairs |

**Softwoods** — From coniferous (cone-bearing, evergreen) trees; faster growing; generally cheaper
| Wood | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Pine** | Common, cheap, knotty; workable | Construction framing, shelving |
| **Spruce** | Strong, lightweight; good resonance | Musical instruments (guitar tops), aircraft |
| **Cedar** | Light, aromatic, weather resistant | Outdoor decking, shingles, storage chests |

**Manufactured Boards (Wood Composites):**
| Board | Composition | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Plywood** | Odd number of veneers glued at 90° angles | Strong, warp-resistant | Furniture, flooring, formwork |
| **MDF (Medium Density Fiberboard)** | Wood fibers + urea formaldehyde resin | Uniform, smooth surface; no grain; heavy | Flat-pack furniture, routing |
| **Chipboard (Particle Board)** | Wood chips + resin | Cheap, heavy; poor moisture resistance | Flat-pack furniture, flooring substrate |
| **OSB (Oriented Strand Board)** | Oriented wood strands + resin | Strong, cost-effective | Structural sheathing, packaging |

#### Ceramics

- **Structure** — Ionic/covalent crystal lattice
- **Properties** — Hard, brittle, high melting point, electrical insulator, chemical resistant, compressive strength
- **Examples:** Glass (SiO₂), porcelain (kaolin + feldspar + quartz), alumina (Al₂O₃), silicon carbide

| Ceramic Type | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Glass** | Transparent, brittle, non-porous | Windows, containers, lenses, fiber optics |
| **Porcelain/China** | Non-porous, hard, aesthetic | Tableware, bathroom fixtures, tiles |
| **Alumina (Al₂O₃)** | Very hard, high temp, electrical insulator | Spark plugs, cutting tools, medical implants |
| **Zirconia (ZrO₂)** | Tough, biocompatible, white | Dental crowns, thermal barrier coatings |

#### Composites

> [!success] Composites
> Composed of two or more materials with different properties, creating a material superior to either component alone.
> - **Matrix** — Surrounds and binds the reinforcement
> - **Reinforcement** — Provides strength and stiffness

| Composite | Matrix | Reinforcement | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **GRP / Fiberglass** | Polyester or epoxy resin | Glass fibers | Strong, lightweight, corrosion resistant, cheap | Boats, car body panels, helmets |
| **CFRP / Carbon Fiber** | Epoxy resin | Carbon fibers | Extremely strong and stiff, very lightweight, expensive | Aerospace, F1 cars, bicycles, sports equipment |
| **Kevlar (KFRP)** | Epoxy resin | Aramid fibers | High tensile strength, cut/impact resistant | Bulletproof vests, boat hulls |
| **Concrete** | Cement paste | Aggregate (sand, gravel) | Very strong in compression; weak in tension | Buildings, bridges, roads |
| **Reinforced Concrete** | Concrete | Steel rebar | Strong in both tension and compression | Structural engineering |
| **Plywood** | Lignin (natural) | Wood veneers | (also a composite; see timber) | |

#### Smart Materials & Modern Materials

| Material | Property | Response to |
|---|---|---|
| **Thermochromic** | Changes color | Temperature |
| **Photochromic** | Changes color | Light |
| **Piezoelectric (e.g., quartz)** | Generates electricity / changes shape | Mechanical stress / electric field |
| **Shape Memory Alloys (Nitinol)** | Returns to original shape | Temperature |
| **Magnetorheological fluids** | Changes viscosity | Magnetic field |
| **Electrochromic glass** | Changes transparency | Electric current |
| **Hydrogels** | Absorbs/releases water; changes size | Water/humidity |

## 🏭 Manufacturing Processes

### Wasting (Subtractive) Processes — Remove material

| Process | Material | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Turning (Lathe)** | Metal, wood | Rotating workpiece; cutting tool removes material; produces cylindrical shapes |
| **Milling (CNC)** | Metal, wood, plastic | Rotating cutter; workpiece moves; produces complex 3D shapes |
| **Drilling** | Most materials | Rotating drill bit; creates holes |
| **Sawing** | Wood, metal, plastic | Various saws; straight cuts |
| **Filing/Grinding** | Metal | Abrasive; smoothing and shaping |
| **Laser Cutting** | Wood, plastic, thin metal | Computer-controlled laser; very precise; smooth edges |
| **Plasma Cutting** | Metal | Electrically ionized gas; cuts thick metal |
| **Waterjet Cutting** | Almost anything | High-pressure water + abrasive; no heat distortion |
| **Routing (CNC)** | Wood, MDF, plastic | High-speed rotating cutter; carves patterns |
| **Electro-discharge machining (EDM)** | Hard metals | Electrical sparks erode material |

### Forming Processes — Reshape material without removing it

| Process | Material | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Bending** | Sheet metal, acrylic (heated) | Applying force to change shape |
| **Forging** | Metal | Hammering/pressing heated metal into dies; strong grain structure |
| **Rolling** | Metal | Passing through rollers; produces sheets and sections |
| **Casting** | Metal, plastic, concrete | Liquid poured into mold; solidifies into shape |
| **Die Casting** | Non-ferrous metals | Molten metal forced into metal die under pressure; high precision; high volume |
| **Sand Casting** | Metal | Mold made of sand; cheap tooling; rough surface |
| **Investment Casting** | Metal | Wax pattern coated in ceramic; wax melted out; molten metal poured in; very precise |
| **Extrusion** | Metal, plastic | Material forced through die; produces constant cross-section (pipes, rods) |
| **Injection Molding** | Thermoplastics | Melted plastic injected into mold under pressure; ideal for high-volume production |
| **Blow Molding** | Thermoplastics | Air blown into heated tube in mold; produces hollow objects (bottles) |
| **Vacuum Forming** | Thermoplastic sheet | Sheet heated and drawn over mold by vacuum; cheap tooling; rapid | 
| **Rotational Molding** | Polyethylene | Mold rotated; polymer coats inside; hollow forms; no pressure |
| **Compression Molding** | Thermosets | Material pressed between heated dies |
| **Press Forming (Stamping)** | Sheet metal | Punching/forming sheet metal with dies; car body panels |
| **Spinning** | Sheet metal | Rotating mold; sheet pressed against it; produces axisymmetric forms |

### Joining Processes

**Permanent Joints:**
| Process | Materials | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Welding (MIG, TIG, Arc)** | Metal | Heat melts base metals (and filler rod); fused joint; strongest permanent joint |
| **Soldering** | Metal, electronics | Filler metal (solder) melts below 450°C; joins without melting base metal |
| **Brazing** | Metal | Filler melts above 450°C; stronger than soldering |
| **Riveting** | Sheet metal, composites | Mechanical fastener deformed to secure joint; used in aircraft |
| **Adhesive Bonding** | Many | Epoxy, contact cement, hot glue, etc. |
| **Welding polymers** | Thermoplastics | Ultrasonic, hot gas, or friction welding |

**Semi-Permanent/Temporary Joints:**
| Fastener | Use |
|---|---|
| Bolts & Nuts | Removable; various sizes; metric/imperial |
| Screws (wood/machine) | Into wood or metal tapped holes |
| Pop rivets | One-sided access; quick |
| Press fit / interference fit | Tight tolerances; no fastener needed |

### Surface Finishes

| Finish | Material | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Painting** | Metal, wood, plastic | Aesthetic; protection from corrosion/weather |
| **Varnishing** | Wood | Protects and shows grain |
| **Staining** | Wood | Color while showing grain |
| **Electroplating** | Metal | Thin metallic coating; aesthetic + corrosion resistance (chrome, gold, nickel) |
| **Galvanizing** | Steel | Zinc coating; sacrificial protection from corrosion |
| **Anodizing** | Aluminum | Thickens natural oxide layer; dye can be added; hardness |
| **Powder Coating** | Metal | Electrostatically charged powder; baked on; durable, smooth |
| **Lacquering** | Metal, wood | Clear or pigmented protective coating |
| **Sandblasting** | Many | Abrasive blasting; cleans, textures surface |

## 🌍 Sustainability & Design

### The 6 R's of Sustainability

> [!success] 6 R's Framework
> 1. **Refuse** — Avoid using unsustainable products or processes entirely
> 2. **Reduce** — Minimize material use, waste, energy consumption
> 3. **Reuse** — Use products/components multiple times before disposal
> 4. **Recycle** — Process materials to create new products; closes material loop
> 5. **Repair** — Extend product life through maintenance and fixing
> 6. **Rethink** — Question design assumptions; could there be a better approach?

### Life Cycle Assessment (LCA)

> [!note] What is LCA?
> A systematic analysis of the environmental impact of a product throughout its entire life from "cradle to grave" (or "cradle to cradle").

**LCA Stages:**
1. **Raw material extraction** — Mining, logging, drilling; habitat destruction, energy use
2. **Processing/Manufacturing** — Energy, water, chemicals; waste and emissions
3. **Distribution/Transport** — Fuel, emissions; packaging
4. **Use phase** — Energy consumption, consumables during operation
5. **End of life** — Disposal, recycling, landfill, incineration

**Cradle to Grave** — Linear; product ends at disposal
**Cradle to Cradle** — Circular; end-of-life becomes input for new product

### Environmental Impact Categories
- **Carbon footprint** — Total greenhouse gas emissions (CO₂ equivalent)
- **Water footprint** — Total water consumption (direct + virtual water)
- **Land use** — Habitat destruction; biodiversity loss
- **Toxicity** — Chemicals released to ecosystems
- **Embodied energy** — Total energy to produce a material/product

### Design for Sustainability Strategies
- **Design for Disassembly** — Make it easy to take apart for recycling/repair
- **Modular design** — Interchangeable parts; replace only broken components
- **Design for longevity** — Durable, timeless, repairable over disposable
- **Biomimicry** — Design inspired by nature's solutions (Velcro from burrs; sharkskin swimsuits)
- **Circular Economy** — Eliminate waste; keep materials in use; regenerate natural systems
- **Sustainable materials** — Recycled content, renewable materials (bamboo, bioplastics, hemp)
- **Energy efficiency** — Reduce operational energy consumption

### Social and Ethical Dimensions of Design
- **User-centered design** — Prioritize end-user needs, safety, comfort, and experience
- **Universal design / Inclusive design** — Design for the full spectrum of human diversity; accessibility for disabled users
- **Fair trade** — Ensure workers in supply chains receive fair wages and safe conditions
- **Planned obsolescence** — Deliberately limiting product lifespan to drive purchases; ethically problematic
- **Cultural sensitivity** — Designs must respect and not appropriate or offend cultural heritage

## ✏️ Graphic Communication

### Types of Drawing

**Sketching:**
- **Isometric drawing** — 3D representation; 30° angles to horizontal; sides drawn to scale
- **Oblique drawing** — Front face drawn true shape; depth at 45°, usually half scale (cabinet oblique)
- **Perspective drawing** — One-point (depth lines converge to single vanishing point); two-point (two vanishing points)
- **Orthographic projection** — Multiple 2D views (front, side, top); first-angle (European) or third-angle (American)

**Annotation:** Written notes explaining features, dimensions, materials, processes

### Technical Drawing Standards
- **Dimensions** — All in mm; dimension lines with arrows; clear and unambiguous
- **Title block** — Name, date, scale, drawing number, material, projection symbol
- **Line types:**
  - Continuous thick: visible outlines
  - Continuous thin: dimension lines, hatching
  - Dashed: hidden outlines
  - Chain (dot-dash): centerlines, cutting planes

### Computer-Aided Design (CAD)
- **2D CAD** — AutoCAD; technical drawings; precise dimensioning
- **3D CAD** — Solidworks, Fusion 360, Tinkercad, Onshape; solid modeling, assembly, simulation
- **Parametric modeling** — Dimensions drive the model; changing one parameter updates entire design
- **Rendering** — Photorealistic visualization; applies materials/lighting for presentation
- **CAM (Computer-Aided Manufacturing)** — CAD files drive CNC machines, 3D printers

### Prototyping & Modeling
- **Paper/card models** — Quick, cheap; test form and proportion
- **Foam models** — Polystyrene or polyurethane; carved to shape; tactile feel
- **3D printing (FDM)** — Fused deposition modeling; thermoplastic filament; layered; low cost; great for testing
- **SLA/Resin printing** — Higher detail; photopolymer resin cured by UV light
- **Laser cut models** — Precise flat parts assembled into 3D forms
- **Working prototypes** — Functional; test performance; include electronics/mechanisms

## 🔩 Mechanical & Structural Principles

### Forces & Structural Loads
- **Tension** — Pulling/stretching force; cables under tension
- **Compression** — Pushing/squeezing force; columns under compression
- **Shear** — Force causing layers to slide; bolts in shear
- **Torsion** — Twisting force; drive shafts
- **Bending** — Combination of tension and compression; beams

### Structural Shapes
- **I-beam** — Efficient in bending; web resists shear; flanges resist bending moment
- **Box section** — Good in bending and torsion; closed cross-section
- **Triangulation** — Triangles are inherently rigid; used in trusses, bicycles, bridges

### Mechanisms
**Levers:**
- Class 1 — Fulcrum between effort and load (seesaw, scissors)
- Class 2 — Load between fulcrum and effort (wheelbarrow, nutcracker)
- Class 3 — Effort between fulcrum and load (tweezers, fishing rod)

**Mechanical Advantage (MA):**
> MA = Load / Effort = Effort Arm / Load Arm

**Gear Trains:**
> Gear Ratio = Teeth on driven gear / Teeth on driver gear
> Higher ratio → more torque, less speed; Lower ratio → more speed, less torque

**Cams & Cranks:** Convert rotary motion to reciprocating (back and forth) motion
**Pulleys:** Redirecting forces; block and tackle systems multiply force
**Linkages:** Four-bar linkage; Watt's linkage; convert motion type

### Electricity & Electronics in Design Technology

**Basic Components:**
- **Resistor** — Limits current flow; Ω; colour code for resistance value
- **Capacitor** — Stores electrical energy; filters; F (farads)
- **LED (Light Emitting Diode)** — Emits light when forward biased; needs current-limiting resistor
- **Transistor** — Switch or amplifier; BJT (NPN/PNP), MOSFET
- **Integrated circuit (IC)** — Multiple components on a single chip; 555 timer, op-amp
- **Microcontroller (Arduino, Raspberry Pi)** — Programmable control; sensor input → actuator output

**Ohm's Law:**
> **V = IR** (Voltage = Current × Resistance)
> P = IV = I²R = V²/R (Power)

**Input-Process-Output Model:**
- **Input:** Sensors (LDR, thermistor, switch, microphone, IR sensor)
- **Process:** Microcontroller/logic circuit (Arduino)
- **Output:** Actuators (motor, LED, speaker, solenoid, display)

## 📋 Assessment & IA (Internal Assessment)

### IB Design Technology HL/SL Structure
- **External Assessment:** Paper 1 (MCQ), Paper 2 (extended response), Paper 3 (HL only)
- **Internal Assessment (IA):** Design project (SL: 20 hrs; HL: 40 hrs)
  - Criterion A: Inquiring and Analysing (10 marks)
  - Criterion B: Developing Ideas (10 marks)
  - Criterion C: Creating the Solution (10 marks)
  - Criterion D: Evaluating (10 marks)

### Key Command Terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Analyse** | Break down in detail; show cause and effect |
| **Compare** | Similarities and differences |
| **Contrast** | Differences only |
| **Define** | Give the precise meaning |
| **Design** | Produce a plan or solution |
| **Evaluate** | Make a judgment based on evidence; weigh merits and limitations |
| **Explain** | Give reason(s) for; cause and effect |
| **Identify** | Provide an answer from a number of possibilities |
| **Justify** | Give valid reasons for; support with evidence |
| **Outline** | Brief account |

### IA Tips
- Use specific, measurable design specifications (not vague)
- Include a range of ideas (min. 6 initial ideas with annotation)
- Photograph all stages of making
- Test against EVERY specification criterion
- Evaluation must be honest — acknowledge limitations
- Client consultation/feedback is essential at multiple stages
