# Baptists

## Overview

**Baptists** are a Protestant Christian denomination that became one of the largest and most influential religious groups in American history. Distinguished by their belief in **believer's baptism** (baptism only of those who make a personal confession of faith, not infants) and **separation of church and state**, Baptists grew enormously during the revivals of the **[[First Great Awakening]]** and **[[Second Great Awakening]]**.

## Colonial Origins

Roger Williams, who founded Rhode Island in 1636 as a haven for religious liberty, became one of America's first Baptists. Baptists faced persecution in Puritan New England and Anglican Virginia, making them passionate advocates for religious freedom and the separation of church and state.

## Growth Through Revivals

Baptist preachers embraced the emotional, democratic style of the Great Awakenings. Their emphasis on personal conversion, lay participation, and congregational autonomy (each congregation governs itself) appealed strongly to frontier and rural Americans.

## Significance for Religious Liberty

Baptists were among the most important advocates for the **[[First Amendment]]'s** religious liberty protections. Baptist minister John Leland worked closely with **[[James Madison]]** and **[[Thomas Jefferson]]** to secure religious freedom in Virginia and nationally.
