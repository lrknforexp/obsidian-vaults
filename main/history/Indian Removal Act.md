> [!info] Quick Facts

> **Affected Tribes:** Cherokee, Chickasaw, Choctaw, Creek, Seminole ("Five Civilized Tribes")

> **Supreme Court Response:** *Worcester v. Georgia* ruled against removal — Jackson ignored it

---

The **Indian Removal Act** (1830), signed by President **[[Andrew Jackson]]**, authorized the federal government to negotiate treaties to relocate Native American tribes from their ancestral homelands in the southeastern United States to territory west of the Mississippi River — what is now Oklahoma.

---

## Jackson's Justification

> [!info] How Jackson Defended Removal
> Jackson argued removal would **protect** Native Americans from state governments (like Georgia) that were encroaching on their lands. He claimed it was more humane than allowing them to be absorbed or destroyed by white settlers. Most historians reject this framing — removal was driven by white settlers' desire for Native land, particularly rich cotton-growing land in the Southeast.

---

## Cherokee Resistance

> [!info] The Legal Battle
> The **Cherokee Nation** fought removal in court. In *Worcester v. Georgia* (1832), the Supreme Court under Chief Justice **[[John Marshall]]** ruled that Georgia had no authority over Cherokee territory. Jackson reportedly responded, "John Marshall has made his decision; now let him enforce it," and proceeded with removal anyway.

---

## The Trail of Tears

The forced removal that followed — particularly the **Cherokee removal** of 1838–1839 — became known as the **[[Trail of Tears]]**. Approximately 15,000 Cherokees were marched west at gunpoint in winter; roughly **4,000 died** from disease, starvation, and exposure.
