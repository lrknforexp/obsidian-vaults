# Virginia and Kentucky Resolutions

> [!info] Virginia and Kentucky Resolutions
> **Overview**
>
> **Topic**
> State resolutions challenging the constitutionality of the Alien and Sedition Acts
>
> **Year**
> 1798
>
> **Authors**
> [[James Madison]] (Virginia) and [[Thomas Jefferson]] (Kentucky)

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Year:** 1798
> **Virginia Resolutions:** Written by [[James Madison]]
> **Kentucky Resolutions:** Written by [[Thomas Jefferson]] (anonymously)
> **Target:** [[Alien and Sedition Acts]]
> **Key Argument:** States can "interpose" or "nullify" unconstitutional federal laws

---

The **Virginia and Kentucky Resolutions** (1798) were statements passed by the Virginia and Kentucky legislatures arguing that the **[[Alien and Sedition Acts]]** were unconstitutional and that states had the right to resist unconstitutional federal laws.

---

## Key Arguments

> [!info] The Compact Theory
> Both sets of resolutions argued that the Constitution was a **compact between the states** — not a grant of power from the states to the federal government. If the federal government exceeded its powers, the states (as parties to the compact) had the right to judge and resist.
>
> **Madison's Virginia Resolutions** called for "interposition" — a collective political protest by states
> **Jefferson's Kentucky Resolutions** went further, suggesting states could "nullify" unconstitutional acts within their borders

---

## Later Misuse

> [!info] The Nullification Problem
> South Carolina's **nullifiers** in the 1830s cited these resolutions to justify their claim that individual states could nullify federal tariff laws. Madison himself vigorously rejected this interpretation, insisting he had envisioned collective political protest — not unilateral state action.

---

## Significance

The resolutions articulated the most important early theory of **states' rights** and became the intellectual foundation for later nullification arguments, ultimately contributing to the secession crisis that caused the [[Civil War]].
