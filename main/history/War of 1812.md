# War of 1812

> [!info] War of 1812 **Overview**
> 
> **Topic** Second war between the United States and Great Britain
> 
> **Dates** 1812–1815
> 
> **President** James Madison

> [!info] Quick Facts **Dates:** 1812–1815 **Combatants:** U.S. vs. Britain (and Native American allies) **Causes:** Impressment, trade restrictions, British support of Native resistance, land disputes **Key Events:** Burning of Washington D.C., Battle of New Orleans **Result:** Status quo ante bellum (neither side gained territory)

---

The **War of 1812** was a conflict between the United States and Great Britain, fought largely over British interference with American shipping and impressment of American sailors during the Napoleonic Wars, as well as British support for Native American resistance to U.S. expansion. Tensions had remained high with Britain since the American Revolution, making this second major conflict between the two nations.

- After the **war of 1812** **henry clay** proposed the **American system** a plan to make the U.S. more **independent** and self sufficient
- Being at war with Britain made us realize how **dependent** we were on them for trade and goods.
- while his plan would fail because of regional tensions with slavery and self-interest, parts would stay alive.

- Different regions produce different goods:
- **North**: manufactured goods
- **South**: raw materials for those goods
- **West**: food for both regions
### Market revolution
- In order to stimulate this country while trading, we need better transportation.
- **Roads**: Roads already existed but were made of dirt. rain would make them Muddy and practically useless, funding went into making roads better.
- **Canals**: canals were used or created, second best transportation as used water transport that can connect to other canals, rivers, etc.
- **Railroads**: railroads were the biggest and best transportation, they were the cheapest, easiest to build, and fastest.
### 19th century immigration
- As Americans went west to populate and explore the Louisiana territory immigrants populated industrializing northern cities
- **Irish immigrants**
- Potato famine in 1845 pushes people out approximately 1 million people
- **German immigrants**
- Farmers looking for work opportunities
- forty-eighters: political refugees (revolutions)
### corrupt bargain
- After the alien and sedition acts, the federalist party is basically gone
- The democratic-republicans are the only party left
- This period of time is known as the era of good feelings
- Voting rights in the 1820s are extended to all white males not just land-owning ones.
- Elections of 1823: all candidates are democratic-republicans
- None of the candidates get the 131 electoral votes needed to win
- election falls between john Quincy Adams & Andrew Jackson
- HOR decides who is the winner
- Andrew Jackson is a war of 1812 war hero, got the most popular and electoral votes but not the majority he needed.
- Adams barely wins with the help of henry clay who he appoints as secretary of state after becoming president
- Jackson accuses them of making a deal, calling it a “corrupt bargain”
### second party system
- After the **corrupt bargain**, factions form in the **democratic-republican party**
- **Jackson’s supporters** in the democratic-republican-party **break off** and form their **own party**
- **Democrats:**
- Led by **Andrew Jackson**
- Limited government, states’ rights, think about the average person
- **Whigs:**
- Led by **henry clay**
- opposed “king Andrew” Jackson, American system beliefs (high tariffs), Legislative power over executive

## Causes

> [!info] Why America Went to War
> 
> - **Land Disputes** — Britain refused to leave territories occupied during the American Revolution, and fighting continued over the northwest territories
> - **Impressment** — Britain forcibly taking sailors from American ships into the Royal Navy
> - **Blockade/Trade interference** — British Orders in Council blocked American trade with Napoleonic Europe, and Britain blockaded French ports during the Napoleonic Wars, cutting off U.S. commerce
> - **Native American alliances** — Britain encouraged and armed Native resistance to American westward expansion
> - **War Hawks** — Congressional leaders like Henry Clay pushed for war to expand into Canada and Florida

---

## Key Events

> [!info] Major Moments
> 
> - **Burning of Washington D.C.** (1814) — British forces captured and burned the capital, including the White House; the President's House was painted white to cover the burn marks and became known as the White House
> - **Battle of Baltimore** — American defense inspired Francis Scott Key to write "The Star-Spangled Banner"
> - **Battle of New Orleans** (January 1815) — [[Andrew Jackson]]'s stunning victory, fought after the peace treaty was signed
> - **Treaty of Ghent** (1814) — ended the war with no territorial changes; the war ended in a stalemate with both sides returning captured land

---

## Legacy

> [!info] Long-Term Consequences The War of 1812 produced a surge of American nationalism, increased foreign respect for the U.S., boosted American manufacturing, and ushered in the "**Era of Good Feelings**." It ended significant Native American resistance east of the Mississippi (Tecumseh died in battle). The **[[Hartford Convention]]**'s opposition to the war destroyed the **[[Federalist Party]]**. Andrew Jackson's fame from New Orleans launched his political career.

---

## The Monroe Doctrine

> [!info] Monroe Doctrine (1823) During President [[James Monroe]]'s annual message to Congress in 1823, Monroe issued a warning to European nations to stay out of the Western Hemisphere. This part of his address became known as the **Monroe Doctrine**. Newly independent nations in Latin and South America were seen as vulnerable to European powers who might seek to reclaim former colonies. The doctrine evolved over the years to assert U.S. dominance over other nations in the Americas.
