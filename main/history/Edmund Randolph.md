# Edmund Randolph

## Overview

**Edmund Randolph** (1753–1813) was an American statesman who played a crucial role at the **Constitutional Convention** of 1787 and in the early republic. He presented the **Virginia Plan** — the blueprint for the new Constitution — yet ultimately refused to sign the document. He later served as the first **Attorney General** and second **Secretary of State** of the United States.

## Constitutional Convention

Randolph, as Governor of Virginia, presented James Madison's **Virginia Plan** on the Convention's opening days, proposing a strong national government with three branches and proportional representation. However, when the final Constitution emerged with compromises he found objectionable (particularly the lack of a bill of rights), he refused to sign.

## Ratification Reversal

At the Virginia ratifying convention (1788), Randolph switched positions and supported ratification, arguing that with amendments forthcoming and nine states already ratified, Virginia had little choice but to join.

## Attorney General and Secretary of State

Randolph served as the nation's first **Attorney General** under Washington (1789–1794), then as **Secretary of State** (1794–1795). He resigned amid accusations of improper contact with France, a charge he denied but could not conclusively refute.
