> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Era:** 1730s–1740s (peak: 1739–1742)
> **Key Preachers:** [[Jonathan Edwards]], [[George Whitefield]]
> **Theology:** Calvinist; emphasized personal conversion and emotional experience
> **Impact:** Created shared intercolonial culture; democratized religion
> **Preceded:** [[Second Great Awakening]] (1790s–1840s)

---

The **First Great Awakening** was a wave of Protestant religious revivals that swept the American colonies in the **1730s and 1740s**. Led by preachers like **[[Jonathan Edwards]]** and **[[George Whitefield]]**, it emphasized emotional conversion experiences ("new birth") over cold doctrinal formalism and dramatically transformed colonial religious life.

---

## Key Preachers

> [!info] The Leading Voices
> **[[Jonathan Edwards]]** — Congregationalist minister in Northampton, Massachusetts; his 1741 sermon **"[[Sinners in the Hands of an Angry God]]"** is the most famous in American history; combined Calvinist theology with Enlightenment psychology
>
> **[[George Whitefield]]** — British Anglican minister; theatrical open-air preacher who drew crowds of up to 20,000; made seven tours of the colonies; created the first transatlantic celebrity

---

## Impact on Colonial Society

> [!info] Why the Awakening Mattered
> - Created a **shared intercolonial culture** — colonists from Georgia to Massachusetts experienced similar religious events
> - **Democratized religion** — challenged established clergy; ordinary people could have direct religious experiences
> - **Split denominations** into "New Light" (pro-revival) and "Old Light" (anti-revival) factions
> - Helped create the **conditions for political unity** that made the Revolution possible
> - Spurred growth of **Baptist and Methodist** congregations
