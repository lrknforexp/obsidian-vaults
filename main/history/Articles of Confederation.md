> [!info] Quick Facts
> **In Effect:** 1781–1789
> **Drafted by:** [[Second Continental Congress]]
> **Key Weakness:** No power to tax, no executive, no national court system
> **Crisis that exposed weaknesses:** Shays' Rebellion (1786–1787)
> **Replaced by:** The Constitution at the [[Constitutional Convention]] (1787)

---

The **Articles of Confederation** were America's first constitution, drafted by the **[[Second Continental Congress]]** and ratified in 1781. They created a very weak central government — a "firm league of friendship" among sovereign states rather than a true national government — and proved unable to manage the challenges of the new nation.

---

## Key Weaknesses

> [!info] What the Articles Could NOT Do
> - **No power to tax** — Congress could only request money from states; states usually ignored the requests
> - **No executive branch** — no president to enforce laws
> - **No national court system** — no way to settle disputes between states
> - **No power to regulate commerce** — states competed with each other economically
> - **Amendment required unanimity** — any one state could block changes
> - **No power to raise an army** directly

---

## The Crisis: Shays' Rebellion

> [!info] The Breaking Point
> In 1786–1787, Massachusetts farmers led by Daniel Shays rebelled against debt collection and foreclosures. The federal government under the Articles was powerless to respond — it had no army and no money. This crisis convinced many leaders that a stronger central government was urgently needed, leading directly to the **Constitutional Convention**.
