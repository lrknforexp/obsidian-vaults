# Democratic-Republicans

## Overview

**Democratic-Republicans** were members of the **[[Democratic-Republican Party]]**, America's first major opposition party, founded by **[[Thomas Jefferson]]** and **[[James Madison]]** in the early 1790s to oppose **[[Alexander Hamilton]]'s** Federalist program. They favored states' rights, limited federal government, agrarian values, and French over British interests in foreign policy.

## Core Beliefs

Democratic-Republicans believed: the [[Federalist Party|Federalists]] were creating an aristocratic, monarchy-like government; the Constitution should be interpreted **strictly** (only explicitly granted powers are legitimate); **states** are the true guardians of liberty; an **agrarian** society of independent farmers was the ideal republican foundation; and France (despite its revolutionary excesses) was a more natural American ally than monarchical Britain.

## Rise to Power

After Jefferson defeated [[John Adams]] in the **[[Election of 1800]]** — the "Revolution of 1800" — Democratic-Republicans dominated American politics for the next two decades. The [[Federalist Party]] gradually collapsed, and by the "Era of Good Feelings" under [[James Monroe]], the Democratic-Republicans had no significant opposition.

## Split and Legacy

The Democratic-Republican Party split in the 1820s into factions supporting [[John Quincy Adams]] (which became the Whig Party) and [[Andrew Jackson]] (which became the modern **[[Democratic Party]]**).
