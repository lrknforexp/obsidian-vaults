# Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo

## Overview

The **Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo** (February 2, 1848) ended the **[[Mexican-American War]]** and was one of the most consequential land deals in American history. Mexico ceded approximately **500,000 square miles** of territory to the United States — including present-day California, Nevada, Utah, Arizona, New Mexico, Colorado, and Wyoming — in exchange for $15 million.

## Negotiation

U.S. diplomat Nicholas Trist negotiated the treaty even after President Polk recalled him, recognizing that any delay might result in worse terms or collapse of negotiations. The treaty was signed in the Mexico City suburb of Guadalupe Hidalgo.

## Key Terms

Mexico recognized the **Rio Grande** as the border with Texas (accepting U.S. claims over disputed territory). Mexico ceded the **Mexican Cession** — the vast Southwest. The U.S. paid $15 million and assumed $3.25 million in American citizens' claims against Mexico. Mexicans living in ceded territory were given the choice to become U.S. citizens.

## Consequences

The Mexican Cession reignited the slavery debate — would these new territories allow slavery? The **[[Compromise of 1850]]** attempted to answer this question. California's gold rush (1848) accelerated the region's development. The treaty's land formed the foundation of the American Southwest as we know it today.
