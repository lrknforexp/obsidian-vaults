> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Year:** 1794 (ratified 1795)
> **Negotiated by:** Chief Justice John Jay
> **Key Gains:** British evacuation of Northwestern forts; limited trade rights with British West Indies
> **Controversial:** Did not address impressment; seen as pro-British
> **Result:** Outrage among Democratic-Republicans; nearly torpedoed Washington's presidency

---

**Jay's Treaty** (1794) was a controversial agreement between the United States and Britain negotiated by Chief Justice John Jay. While it averted war and achieved some American goals, it was widely seen as a humiliating capitulation to Britain and sparked fierce political conflict.

---

## Background

> [!info] Why Negotiations Were Needed
> Britain had never evacuated forts in the American Northwest (as required by the [[Treaty of Paris (1783)]]). British ships were seizing American merchant vessels trading with France and impressing American sailors into the Royal Navy. War seemed possible — but the young U.S. was in no position to fight Britain.

---

## Terms of the Treaty

> [!info] What Jay Achieved (and Didn't)
> **Achieved:**
> - British agreed to evacuate Northwestern forts
> - Limited American trading rights in the British West Indies
> - Joint commissions to resolve pre-war debts and ship seizure claims
>
> **Failed to achieve:**
> - No agreement on **impressment** of American sailors
> - No compensation for enslaved people carried off by British forces

---

## Political Fallout

The treaty outraged **[[Democratic-Republican Party|Democratic-Republicans]]** who saw it as shameful submission to Britain and betrayal of France. Protests erupted across the country. Washington's decision to sign it was one of the most controversial of his presidency and intensified the split between Federalists and Democratic-Republicans.
