---

**Robert Fulton** was an American inventor and engineer who developed the first commercially successful **steamboat** in the United States, transforming river transportation and fueling the **[[Market Revolution]]**.

---

## The Steamboat

> [!info] The Clermont (1807)
> On August 17, 1807, Fulton's steamboat — popularly called the *Clermont* — completed a 150-mile voyage up the Hudson River from New York City to Albany in **32 hours**. Earlier sail-powered vessels took **four days** for the same trip.
>
> The significance was enormous:
> - Steamboats could travel **upriver** as well as down — something sail and flatboat-powered vessels could not reliably do
> - This made river commerce in **both directions** practical for the first time
> - Shipping costs dropped dramatically
> - The entire interior river system of the United States — the Ohio, Mississippi, Missouri, and their tributaries — became economically accessible

Fulton did not invent the steam engine (James Watt did, in Britain) or even the steamboat concept — earlier experimental versions existed. What Fulton did was engineer a **reliable, commercially viable** steamboat that could operate profitably on American rivers.

---

## Impact on the Market Revolution

Steamboats became one of the key engines of the **[[Market Revolution]]**:
- Goods from the interior Midwest could reach Eastern markets cheaply and reliably
- Cities along major rivers — Cincinnati, Louisville, St. Louis, New Orleans — grew dramatically
- Combined with the **[[Erie Canal]]** and later the **[[Railroad]]**, steamboats knit the American economy together into a national market

---

## Other Inventions

Fulton also worked on **submarine designs** (the *Nautilus*) and was involved in early development of **torpedoes** — both for the French navy during the Napoleonic Wars. Neither achieved significant success in his lifetime.
