# French and Indian War

> [!info] French and Indian War
> **Overview**
>
> **Topic**
> North American theater of the Seven Years' War; Britain vs. France
>
> **Dates**
> 1754–1763
>
> **Result**
> British victory; France expelled from North America

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Dates:** 1754–1763
> **Combatants:** Britain (+ colonial militia) vs. France (+ Native American allies)
> **Global Context:** Part of the [[Seven Years' War]]
> **Result:** France ceded Canada and eastern North America to Britain
> **Young officer:** [[George Washington]] had his first military experience here

---

The **French and Indian War** (1754–1763) was the North American theater of the global **[[Seven Years' War]]**. Britain and France — aided by their respective Native American allies — fought for control of the continent. Britain's decisive victory expelled France from North America but planted the seeds of the [[American Revolution]].

---

## Origins

> [!info] The Ohio Valley Conflict
> Both France and Britain claimed the **[[Ohio River Valley]]** — the gateway to the interior. France built forts to assert control. Virginia sent young **[[George Washington]]** to demand French withdrawal in 1753 — they refused. Washington's skirmish at Fort Necessity (1754) triggered the war.

---

## Native American Role

> [!info] The "Indian" in "French and Indian War"
> Most Native American nations allied with France, which had maintained trade relationships and treated them more as partners than subjects. Britain's victory was catastrophic for Native peoples — it removed their most powerful European ally. **[[Pontiac's Rebellion]]** (1763) was the immediate Native American response.

---

## Seeds of Revolution

> [!info] How the War Caused the Revolution
> Britain won but accumulated enormous war debts. To pay for the war and colonial defense, Parliament began taxing the colonies — the **[[Stamp Act]]** (1765), then the **[[Townshend Acts]]** (1767). Colonial resistance to these taxes launched the path to revolution.
