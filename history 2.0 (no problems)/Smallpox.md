# Smallpox

## Overview

**Smallpox** was a devastating infectious disease that played a catastrophic role in the European colonization of the Americas. When Europeans arrived in the 15th and 16th centuries, they carried smallpox (and other diseases) to which indigenous Americans had no immunity. The resulting epidemics killed an estimated **50–90% of Native American populations** — one of the greatest demographic catastrophes in human history.

## Impact on Indigenous Peoples

Native Americans had been isolated from Eurasian diseases for thousands of years and had no natural immunity. When the Spanish arrived in Mexico in 1519, smallpox preceded and accompanied the **[[Conquistadors]]**, devastating the Aztec Empire before Cortés had even fully conquered it. Similar epidemics swept North America as European contact expanded.

## Role in Colonization

Disease made European conquest far easier than it would otherwise have been. Populations that had numbered in the millions were reduced to remnants, leaving land "empty" that settlers quickly claimed. The **[[Encomienda System]]** collapsed partly because the labor force it relied on was dying faster than it could be replaced.

## Biological Warfare

During **[[Pontiac's Rebellion]]** (1763), British officers at Fort Pitt deliberately gave smallpox-infected blankets to Native representatives — one of history's most documented cases of biological warfare.
