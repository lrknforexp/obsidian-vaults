# Washington Monument

## Overview

The **Washington Monument** is a 555-foot obelisk in Washington, D.C., built to honor **[[George Washington]]**, the first President of the United States and commander of the Continental Army during the [[American Revolution]]. Construction began in 1848 but was halted for over 20 years due to funding shortages and the [[Civil War]], finally completed in 1884.

## History of Construction

Congress authorized a monument to Washington shortly after his death in 1799, but actual construction didn't begin until **July 4, 1848**. Private fundraising through the Washington National Monument Society supported the project. Construction halted in 1854 when the Know-Nothing Party (nativists) seized control of the Society and diverted funds, leaving the monument at only 152 feet for over 20 years.

## Completion

Congress appropriated funds to complete the monument in 1876, and the Army Corps of Engineers finished it in 1884. The capstone was set on December 6, 1884. If you look closely, there's a visible color difference in the marble where construction resumed.

## Symbolism

The monument represents America's reverence for Washington as the **"indispensable man"** of the founding era — the general who held the Continental Army together, the president who set democratic precedents, and the leader who voluntarily surrendered power.
