---

The **Alien and Sedition Acts** (1798) were four laws passed by the Federalist-controlled Congress that restricted immigration and criminalized criticism of the government. They were used as a political weapon against **[[Democratic-Republican Party|Democratic-Republicans]]** and became one of the most controversial acts of the early republic.

---

## The Four Acts

> [!info] What the Laws Did
> - **Naturalization Act** — extended residency requirement for citizenship from 5 to 14 years (targeting Irish and French immigrants who voted Democratic-Republican)
> - **Alien Friends Act** — allowed president to deport any non-citizen deemed dangerous
> - **Alien Enemies Act** — allowed deportation of citizens of enemy nations during wartime
> - **Sedition Act** — criminalized "false, scandalous, and malicious" writing against the government or president

---

## The Sedition Act in Practice

The Sedition Act was used almost exclusively against Democratic-Republican newspaper editors and politicians. **[[Matthew Lyon]]**, a Vermont congressman, was jailed for four months for criticizing Adams — and won reelection from prison.

---

## The Response

> [!info] Virginia and Kentucky Resolutions
> **[[Thomas Jefferson]]** (secretly) and **[[James Madison]]** drafted the **[[Virginia and Kentucky Resolutions]]**, arguing the acts were unconstitutional and that states had the right to judge — and resist — unconstitutional federal laws. This was an early articulation of **states' rights** and **nullification** theory.
