# Free Enterprise

## Overview

**Free enterprise** (also called capitalism or free market economics) is an economic system in which private individuals and businesses, rather than the government, make economic decisions about production, prices, and trade. In early American history, debates over the proper role of government in the economy — free enterprise vs. government direction — were central political conflicts.

## Hamilton vs. Jefferson

**[[Alexander Hamilton]]** believed the federal government should actively promote economic development through a national bank, tariffs, and subsidies to manufacturing. **[[Thomas Jefferson]]** favored more limited government interference, trusting free markets and individual enterprise among agrarian communities. This debate shaped American economic policy for generations.

## Market Revolution and Free Enterprise

The **[[Market Revolution]]** of the early 19th century was largely driven by private enterprise: entrepreneurs built factories, canals, and railroads; investors funded new businesses; and markets coordinated economic activity across vast distances.

## Tensions

Free enterprise created enormous wealth but also stark inequality, worker exploitation, and cyclical financial panics. The debates between those who wanted government to regulate markets and those who wanted minimal interference remain central to American politics.
