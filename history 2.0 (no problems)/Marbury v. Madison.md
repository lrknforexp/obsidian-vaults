**Marbury v. Madison** (1803) is the most important Supreme Court decision in American history. Chief Justice **[[John Marshall]]** used a minor dispute over a judicial appointment to establish the Supreme Court's power of **[[Judicial Review]]** — the authority to declare laws unconstitutional.

---

## Background

> [!info] The Midnight Judges Crisis
> Outgoing President [[John Adams]] appointed William Marbury as a justice of the peace in his final hours (see **[[midnight judges]]**). Incoming President [[Thomas Jefferson]] ordered Secretary of State [[James Madison]] to withhold Marbury's commission. Marbury sued directly in the Supreme Court.

---

## Marshall's Brilliant Strategy

> [!info] How Marshall Seized Power While Appearing to Lose
> Marshall ruled that while Marbury deserved his commission, the law under which he sued (*Judiciary Act of 1789*) was **unconstitutional** because it expanded the Supreme Court's original jurisdiction beyond what the Constitution allowed.
>
> This meant: Marbury lost his case — but the Court gained enormous power. Jefferson couldn't object because the ruling went against Marbury. Yet Marshall had established that the Supreme Court could void acts of Congress.

---

## Significance

Judicial review made the Supreme Court the **ultimate interpreter of the Constitution** and created a genuine system of checks and balances between branches. Every major constitutional question since 1803 has been decided under the authority this case established.
