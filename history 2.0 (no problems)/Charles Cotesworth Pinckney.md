# Charles Cotesworth Pinckney

## Overview

**Charles Cotesworth Pinckney** (1746–1825) was a South Carolina statesman, Revolutionary War general, and diplomat. He is best known for his role in the **[[Quasi-War|XYZ Affair]]** (1797–1798), for which he is famous for reportedly responding to French demands for bribes with **"Millions for defense, but not one cent for tribute!"**

## Revolutionary Career

Pinckney served as an aide-de-camp to [[George Washington]] during the [[American Revolution]] and was captured by the British in 1780. He was a delegate to the Constitutional Convention (1787) and signed the Constitution.

## XYZ Affair

President [[John Adams]] sent Pinckney, along with John Marshall and Elbridge Gerry, to France to negotiate commercial disputes. French agents (referred to as X, Y, and Z in diplomatic dispatches) demanded bribes before negotiations could begin. Pinckney's reported refusal became a rallying cry for American national honor.

## Federalist Presidential Candidate

Pinckney ran as the **[[Federalist Party]]** candidate for President in 1804 and 1808, losing both times to **[[Thomas Jefferson]]** (in 1804 he lost to James Madison as Jefferson's party ran Madison). The defeats reflected the Federalist Party's declining national appeal.
