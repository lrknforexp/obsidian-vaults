> **Linked to:** [[Industrial Revolution]], [[Second Great Awakening]]

---

The **Market Revolution** was the transformation of the American economy from **subsistence farming and local barter** to **commercial capitalism** with national markets. Driven by improvements in transportation and communication, it reshaped American society from roughly 1790 to 1850.

---

## Drivers of Change

> [!info] What Made It Happen
> - **[[Erie Canal]]** (1825) — connected Great Lakes to New York City; cut shipping costs by 90%
> - **[[Railroad|Railroads]]** — fastest and cheapest overland transport; created national markets
> - **[[Steamboat]]** — made rivers two-way commercial highways
> - **[[Telegraph]]** — enabled real-time business communication across distances
> - **Factory system** — concentrated manufacturing in cities ([[Lowell, Massachusetts]])

---

## Social Consequences

> [!info] Winners and Losers
> The Market Revolution created enormous wealth but also new forms of inequality and dependence:
> - **Winners:** Entrepreneurs, merchants, commercial farmers, factory owners
> - **Losers/Changed:** [[subsistence farmers]] forced into cash economy, factory workers dependent on wages, traditional artisans replaced by factory production
> - New **working class** emerged, leading to the early **[[labor movement]]**
> - Massive **[[Urbanization]]** as workers moved to factory towns

---

## Connection to Reform

The disruptions of the Market Revolution helped fuel the **[[Second Great Awakening]]** and the reform movements it inspired — temperance, abolitionism, women's rights — as Americans sought to impose moral order on a rapidly changing society.
