# midnight judges

## Overview

**"Midnight judges"** refers to the approximately 59 judicial appointments made by President **[[John Adams]]** in the final hours of his administration on the night of March 3, 1801, before **[[Thomas Jefferson]]** was inaugurated. The appointments were part of the **Judiciary Act of 1801**, which Adams signed to pack the courts with **[[Federalist Party|Federalist]]** judges before Democratic-Republicans took power.

## Background

Adams and the Federalists were furious at losing the [[Election of 1800]] to Jefferson. In their final weeks in power, they passed the Judiciary Act of 1801, which created 16 new circuit court judgeships and reduced the Supreme Court from 6 to 5 members (to deny Jefferson an immediate appointment).

## The Crisis

When Jefferson took office, he discovered that several commissions had been signed but not delivered, including that of **William Marbury**. Jefferson's Secretary of State, **[[James Madison]]**, refused to deliver them. Marbury sued, leading to **[[Marbury v. Madison]]** — the landmark case establishing **[[Judicial Review]]**.

## Significance

The midnight judges episode reflects the fierce partisan struggle of the early republic and led directly to the most important Supreme Court decision in American history. Jefferson and the Democrats repealed the Judiciary Act of 1801, eliminating the new circuit court positions.
