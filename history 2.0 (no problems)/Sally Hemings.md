# Sally Hemings

## Overview

**Sally Hemings** (c. 1773–1835) was an enslaved woman at **Monticello** owned by President **[[Thomas Jefferson]]**. Historical evidence — including DNA analysis, documentary records, and oral history from her descendants — strongly indicates that Jefferson fathered her six children, making their relationship one of the most discussed and contested in American history.

## Background

Hemings was the half-sister of Jefferson's late wife, Martha Wayles Jefferson, making her children Jefferson's relatives by blood. She accompanied Jefferson's daughter to Paris in 1787, where Jefferson was serving as U.S. Minister to France. (In France, she was legally free.)

## The Relationship

The relationship began in France around 1788 and continued until Jefferson's death in 1826. Hemings agreed to return to America reportedly after Jefferson promised to free their children when they came of age — a promise he kept. She herself was never formally freed; she was quietly allowed to go free by Jefferson's family after his death.

## Historical Significance

The Jefferson-Hemings relationship illustrates the fundamental contradiction at the heart of the American founding: a man who wrote "all men are created equal" held hundreds of people in slavery. DNA evidence published in 1998 confirmed paternity with near certainty.
