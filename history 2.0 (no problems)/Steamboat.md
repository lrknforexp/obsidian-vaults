# Steamboat

## Overview

The **steamboat** was a revolutionary transportation technology that transformed American commerce and travel in the early 19th century. Powered by steam engines rather than wind or human muscle, steamboats could travel upstream on America's great rivers — making the **[[Mississippi River]]** a two-way commercial highway for the first time.

## Robert Fulton's Breakthrough

**[[Robert Fulton]]** successfully demonstrated the first commercially practical steamboat, the *Clermont*, on the Hudson River in August 1807. It traveled from New York City to Albany (150 miles) in 32 hours — a journey that took sailing vessels four days.

## Transformation of River Commerce

Before steamboats, flatboats could carry goods downstream on the Mississippi and Ohio Rivers, but returning upstream was impractical. Steamboats changed everything: they could travel upstream at commercially viable speeds, connecting inland farmers to markets in New Orleans, Cincinnati, and St. Louis.

## Economic Impact

Steamboats dramatically reduced shipping costs and times. The price of shipping goods from Louisville to New Orleans fell by over 90% between 1815 and 1860. Cities along major rivers — New Orleans, St. Louis, Cincinnati, Pittsburgh — boomed as commercial centers.
