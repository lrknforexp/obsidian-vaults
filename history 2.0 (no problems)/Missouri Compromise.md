# Missouri Compromise

> [!info] Missouri Compromise
> **Overview**
>
> **Topic**
> Congressional agreement over slavery in new territories
>
> **Year**

>
> **Key Figure**
> Senator [[Henry Clay]]

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Year:** 1820
> **Brokered by:** Senator [[Henry Clay]] ("The Great Compromiser")
> **Key Line:** 36°30' latitude — slavery permitted south, banned north
> **Admitted:** Missouri (slave) and Maine (free) simultaneously
> **Repealed by:** [[Kansas-Nebraska Act]], 1854

---

The **Missouri Compromise** (1820) was a congressional agreement that temporarily resolved the crisis over slavery's expansion into territories gained from the [[Louisiana Purchase]]. Brokered by Senator **[[Henry Clay]]**, it admitted Missouri as a slave state and Maine as a free state, and drew a line at **36°30' latitude** — slavery permitted south of the line, banned north of it.

---

## The Crisis

> [!info] Why Missouri Caused a Crisis
> When Missouri applied for statehood in 1819, it threatened to upset the balance of 11 free and 11 slave states in the Senate. Northerners proposed the **Tallmadge Amendment** to gradually abolish slavery in Missouri — which Southern states saw as an existential threat. The debate paralyzed Congress for over a year.

---

## The Solution

> [!info] Clay's Compromise
> - **Missouri** admitted as a **slave state**
> - **Maine** (carved from Massachusetts) admitted as a **free state** — maintaining the balance
> - Slavery **banned** in all Louisiana Purchase territory north of **36°30'** latitude (Missouri's southern border)

---

## Jefferson's Warning

Thomas Jefferson called the Missouri Compromise debate "a fire bell in the night" — warning that the slavery question, if not resolved, would ultimately destroy the Union. He was right. The compromise held for 34 years until the **[[Kansas-Nebraska Act]]** repealed it in 1854.
