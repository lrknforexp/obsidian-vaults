# Stamp Act

> [!info] Stamp Act
> **Overview**
>
> **Topic**
> British tax on colonial documents that sparked major resistance
>
> **Year**
> 1765
>
> **Response**
> "No taxation without representation"

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Year:** 1765
> **What it taxed:** All printed materials — newspapers, legal documents, pamphlets, playing cards
> **Colonial Objection:** Taxation without representation in Parliament
> **Repealed:** 1766 (but accompanied by Declaratory Act asserting Parliament's authority)
> **Key Opponent:** [[Patrick Henry]]

---

The **Stamp Act** (1765) was a British law imposing a direct tax on all printed materials in the American colonies — newspapers, legal documents, pamphlets, even playing cards. It was the first direct tax Parliament had imposed on the colonies and provoked furious resistance, marking a major escalation in tensions that led to the [[American Revolution]].

---

## Why It Caused Outrage

> [!info] "No Taxation Without Representation"
> Colonists argued that Parliament had no right to tax them because they had **no representatives in Parliament**. This was not just an economic complaint — it was a constitutional argument about the nature of British liberty. The **Stamp Act Congress** (1765) brought delegates from nine colonies together for the first time to coordinate resistance.

---

## Forms of Resistance

> [!info] How Colonists Fought Back
> - **Stamp Act Congress** — first intercolonial political body
> - **Non-importation agreements** — boycotts of British goods
> - **Sons of Liberty** — groups that intimidated (and sometimes attacked) stamp distributors
> - **[[Patrick Henry]]'s Virginia Resolves** — argued only Virginia's assembly could tax Virginians
> - Stamp distributors were forced to resign throughout the colonies

---

## Repeal and Aftermath

Britain repealed the Stamp Act in 1766 but simultaneously passed the **Declaratory Act**, asserting Parliament's absolute right to legislate for the colonies "in all cases whatsoever." The underlying constitutional conflict was unresolved.
