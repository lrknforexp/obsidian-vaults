# George Clinton

## Overview

**George Clinton** (1739–1812) was an American politician who served as the first and third Governor of New York and as Vice President under both **[[Thomas Jefferson]]** and **[[James Madison]]**. He was one of the most powerful **[[Anti-Federalists]]**, opposing ratification of the Constitution, and was a major figure in New York and national politics for decades.

## Revolutionary Career

Clinton served as a general in the [[American Revolution]] and was elected the first Governor of New York in 1777, a position he held for six consecutive terms (1777–1795). He was known as an effective wartime governor and a committed republican.

## Anti-Federalism

Clinton was one of the most prominent Anti-Federalists, writing under the pseudonym "**Cato**" to oppose the Constitution. He argued it created an overly powerful central government that threatened states' rights and individual liberties. New York barely ratified the Constitution, 30-27, due partly to Anti-Federalist opposition he led.

## Vice Presidency

Despite his opposition to the Constitution, Clinton became Vice President under Jefferson (1805–1809) and continued under Madison. He cast the deciding vote against rechartering the First National Bank in 1811.
