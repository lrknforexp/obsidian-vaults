# French Revolution

## Overview

The **French Revolution** (1789–1799) was a period of radical political and social transformation in France that overthrew the monarchy, established a republic, and eventually led to **[[Napoleon Bonaparte]]'s** rise to power. It profoundly influenced American politics and helped define the divide between [[Federalist Party|Federalists]] and [[Democratic-Republican Party|Democratic-Republicans]].

## Causes

France faced a severe financial crisis (partly from funding the [[American Revolution]]), mass poverty, food shortages, and a rigid social hierarchy. Inspired by Enlightenment ideas and American independence, the French people revolted against King Louis XVI.

## Key Phases

The Revolution moved from constitutional monarchy (1789–1792) to the radical **Reign of Terror** (1793–1794), in which the revolutionary government executed thousands including the king and queen, to the more moderate Directory, and finally to Napoleon's coup in 1799.

## Impact on America

The Revolution sharply divided Americans. [[Thomas Jefferson]] and Democratic-Republicans sympathized with the French, seeing it as an extension of revolutionary ideals. [[Alexander Hamilton]] and Federalists were horrified by the violence and feared radicalism. The conflict contributed to the **[[Quasi-War]]** with France (1798–1800).
