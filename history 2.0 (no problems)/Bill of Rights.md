---

The **Bill of Rights** — the first ten amendments to the U.S. Constitution — was ratified in **1791**. Drafted by **[[James Madison]]** and demanded by **[[Anti-Federalists]]** as a condition of supporting ratification, it protects fundamental individual liberties from government infringement.

---

## Why It Was Added

> [!info] The Anti-Federalist Demand
> **[[Anti-Federalists]]** like [[Patrick Henry]] and [[George Mason]] refused to support the Constitution without explicit protections for individual rights. They feared the new federal government would suppress the very liberties the Revolution was fought to secure. The promise that a bill of rights would be added immediately after ratification was essential to winning enough support for the Constitution.

---

## Key Amendments

> [!info] Most Important Protections
> - **1st** — Freedom of religion, speech, press, assembly, petition ([[First Amendment]])
> - **2nd** — Right to bear arms
> - **4th** — Protection from unreasonable searches and seizures
> - **5th** — Due process; no self-incrimination; no double jeopardy
> - **6th** — Right to speedy trial by jury; right to counsel
> - **8th** — No cruel and unusual punishment
> - **10th** — Powers not given to federal government reserved to states

---

## Legacy

The Bill of Rights has become the foundation of American civil liberties law. Its provisions — especially the **[[First Amendment]]** — have been interpreted expansively by the Supreme Court over two centuries to protect individual freedoms against government power.
