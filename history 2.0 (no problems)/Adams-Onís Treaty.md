# Adams-Onís Treaty

> [!info] Adams-Onís Treaty
> **Overview**
>
> **Topic**
> U.S.-Spain border settlement and acquisition of Florida
>
> **Also Known As**
> Transcontinental Treaty
>
> **Signed**
> 1819

> [!info] Quick Facts
> **Signed:** 1819
> **Negotiated by:** Secretary of State [[John Quincy Adams]] and Spanish minister Luis de Onís
> **Key Gain:** Florida ceded to the United States
> **Also established:** Transcontinental boundary line to the Pacific

---

The **Adams-Onís Treaty** (also called the **Transcontinental Treaty**), signed in **1819**, settled long-standing border disputes between the United States and Spain and dramatically expanded U.S. territory. Secretary of State **[[John Quincy Adams]]** negotiated the treaty with Spanish minister Luis de Onís.

---

## Key Terms

> [!info] What the U.S. Gained
> - Spain ceded **Florida** to the United States
> - The U.S. assumed up to **$5 million** in claims American citizens had against Spain
> - A **transcontinental boundary line** was established running from the Gulf of Mexico to the Pacific Ocean
> - Spain recognized U.S. claims north of the line; the U.S. recognized Spanish claims (including Texas) to the south

---

## Context

The treaty came after American forces under [[Andrew Jackson]] invaded Spanish Florida in 1818 during the First Seminole War, embarrassing Spain and demonstrating its inability to control the territory. Spain, weakened by colonial revolts in Latin America, concluded that surrendering Florida was preferable to continued conflict.

---

## Significance

> [!info] Why It Matters
> The Adams-Onís Treaty gave the U.S. its **first Pacific coastline claims** and set the stage for future westward expansion. It was a major diplomatic triumph for John Quincy Adams and a milestone in the growth of American continental ambitions — a preview of [[Manifest Destiny]].
