# New York City

## Overview

**New York City** was the first capital of the United States under the Constitution (1789–1790), the nation's largest city by 1790, and the commercial and financial hub of the new republic. Its growth in the 19th century — driven by immigration, commerce, and the completion of the **[[Erie Canal]]** — made it the economic center of America.

## Revolutionary History

New York City was occupied by British forces from September 1776 until November 1783 — throughout most of the [[American Revolution]]. The city's Loyalist population and strategic harbor made it Britain's most important American base.

## First Capital

**[[George Washington]]** was inaugurated as the first president at Federal Hall in New York City on April 30, 1789. Congress met there until the capital moved to **[[Philadelphia]]** in 1790 and then to **[[Washington, D.C.]]** in 1800.

## Erie Canal and Commercial Growth

The completion of the **[[Erie Canal]]** in 1825 was the most important factor in New York City's explosive growth. Connecting the Hudson River to the Great Lakes, the canal funneled the agricultural products of the Midwest through New York City, making it the undisputed commercial capital of America.

## Immigration and Tammany Hall

Massive Irish and German immigration in the 1840s–1850s transformed New York City's demographics and politics. The Democratic political machine **Tammany Hall** organized immigrant votes and became one of the most powerful political organizations in American history.
