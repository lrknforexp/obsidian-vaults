# Whig Party

## Overview

The **Whig Party** was one of the two major American political parties from roughly **1833 to 1856**. It formed in opposition to President **[[Andrew Jackson]]** and the Democratic Party, drawing together those who opposed Jackson's strong executive power. The party elected two presidents (**William Henry Harrison** and **Zachary Taylor**) but collapsed over the slavery issue in the 1850s.

## Formation

The Whigs emerged from the coalition opposing Jackson's policies — his destruction of the **[[National Bank]]**, his use of the veto, and his expansion of executive power. The name "Whig" deliberately echoed the British Whigs who had opposed royal tyranny.

## Key Figures and Beliefs

**[[Henry Clay]]** was the Whigs' most important leader, advocating his **[[American System]]** of protective tariffs, a national bank, and internal improvements. **Daniel Webster** was the party's great orator. Whigs generally favored a stronger Congress over the executive and supported economic modernization.

## Collapse

The **[[Kansas-Nebraska Act]]** of 1854 destroyed the Whigs. The party could not agree on whether to allow slavery's expansion in the territories. Northern Whigs flooded into the new **Republican Party**, while Southern Whigs drifted to the Democrats or other parties.
