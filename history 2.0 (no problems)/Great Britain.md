# Great Britain

## Overview

**Great Britain** (formally the Kingdom of Great Britain after 1707, the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland after 1801) was the colonial power that established the thirteen American colonies and against which Americans revolted in the **[[American Revolution]]**. British-American relations — marked by conflict, trade, and shared culture — shaped American history from colonization through the 19th century.

## Colonial Rule

Britain established thirteen colonies along the Atlantic coast, governing them through royal governors, Parliament, and the Navigation Acts that regulated colonial trade for British benefit. The tension between British authority and colonial self-governance built for 150 years before exploding in revolution.

## American Revolution

Colonial resistance to taxation without representation (**[[Stamp Act]]**, **[[Townshend Acts]]**) led to the **[[American Revolution]]** (1775–1783). Britain's defeat, formalized in the **[[Treaty of Paris (1783)]]**, created the United States.

## Continued Conflicts

Britain and America remained in tension: Britain refused to evacuate Northwestern forts (resolved by **[[Jay's Treaty]]**, 1795), impressed American sailors, and supported Native American resistance to westward expansion. These conflicts contributed to the **[[War of 1812]]**.

## Later Relations

After the War of 1812, Britain and the U.S. established more peaceful relations. The **[[Monroe Doctrine]]** (1823) implicitly relied on British naval power to keep other European powers out of the Americas.
